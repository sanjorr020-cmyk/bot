# Media & Press Outreach

Earn coverage by giving journalists a story their editor will approve, delivered to the right reporter at the right moment in under 150 words. This skill covers the full earned-media workflow: finding the angle, building and vetting a target list, writing pitches that get replies, following up without burning the relationship, winning expert-quote placements, and tracking relationships so the second story is easier than the first. The deliverable is a ready-to-send outreach kit, not just advice.

## When to use this skill

Use for:
- Getting press coverage for a launch, funding round, data report, milestone, or founder story
- Building or vetting a journalist/podcast/newsletter target list
- Writing or critiquing pitch emails to reporters, editors, or producers
- Exclusive and embargo strategy for an announcement
- Newsjacking a breaking story or trend
- Responding to source requests (HARO, Qwoted, Help a B2B Writer, #journorequest)
- Planning follow-up cadence after a pitch, or reviving a cold journalist relationship

Do NOT use for (route to the sibling skill instead):
- Writing the press release document itself → **press-releases** (this skill writes the pitch that carries it)
- Cold outreach to prospects/customers → **cold-email-outreach**
- Paid influencer or creator placements → **influencer-marketing**
- Guest posts pitched for backlinks → **link-building**
- Positioning and message hierarchy → **brand-messaging** (consume its output here)
- Full launch orchestration where press is one channel → **product-launch**
- Podcast guesting prep and production → **podcast-production**

## Inputs to gather

Ask the user for these; where they don't know, use the stated default and say you assumed it.

1. **The news or story.** What happened or will happen? If the answer is "nothing, we just want press," flag that immediately — you will need to manufacture an angle in Step 1 (data, contrarian take, or trend-tie).
2. **Timing.** Announcement date if any. Default: no hard date → run a rolling pitch over 2 weeks. If there is a date, work backward: exclusives offered 7–10 business days out, embargo pitches 3–5 days out.
3. **Goal and tier.** Awareness, credibility for sales, SEO/backlinks, investor signal? Default: credibility → prioritize 1 tier-1 swing plus 5–10 trade/vertical outlets over a spray at 100 outlets.
4. **Audience → outlets.** Who must see the coverage? Default: the buyer's trade press, not TechCrunch. Trade press converts; tier-1 flatters.
5. **Spokesperson.** Who gives quotes and interviews? Their title, one credibility line, availability windows. Default: founder/CEO.
6. **Assets.** Logo, product screenshots, headshots, data charts, demo video. Default: assume a shared folder must be assembled — add it to the deliverable checklist.
7. **Prior coverage and existing press contacts.** Any journalist who has covered them before goes to the top of the list.
8. **Constraints.** Legal/comms review, public company quiet periods, competitor sensitivities, geographies/languages.

## Process

1. **Stress-test the story for coverability.** Score the raw story against the TRUE-D filter (see Frameworks). A story needs at least 3 of 5 elements to survive an editor. If it scores 0–2, do not pitch it as news — convert it: (a) run a data play (survey ≥500 respondents or mine internal usage data for a surprising stat), (b) attach it to a live trend (newsjack), or (c) sharpen a contrarian founder take. Tell the user plainly when their "news" isn't news; a funding round under ~$5M, a minor feature release, or a new hire below C-level rarely clears the bar at tier-1 and belongs in trade press or a contributed article instead.

2. **Write 3 candidate angles as headlines.** Draft each as the headline the journalist would actually publish, in that outlet's voice, ≤80 characters. If you can't write the headline, the angle doesn't exist. Present all 3 to the user with a recommendation. Decision point: one angle can serve multiple outlet types only if you re-lede it per beat (business angle for business press, technical angle for trade, human angle for local/lifestyle).

3. **Build the target list (15–30 contacts for a normal campaign).** Structure it in three tiers:
   - Tier 1 (3–5): dream outlets; candidates for the exclusive.
   - Tier 2 (8–15): trade, vertical, and regional outlets; the realistic core.
   - Tier 3 (5–10): newsletters, podcasts, industry blogs; fastest yeses, compounding value.
   Find names by reading who actually wrote the last 3 stories on this topic (search `site:outlet.com <topic>`, check bylines, X/Twitter bios, Muck Rack or the outlet's masthead). For each contact capture: name, outlet, beat, email, 2 recent relevant headlines with dates, preferred channel, and a one-line personalization hook.

4. **Vet every contact — 3 hard checks.** (a) Published on this beat within the last 90 days (reporters change beats constantly; stale databases are the #1 cause of dead pitches). (b) Covers companies at the user's stage — a reporter who only writes about public companies won't cover a seed startup. (c) Correct role: reporters write stories, editors assign them, producers book guests. Pitch reporters by default; pitch editors only for contributed articles/op-eds. Cut anyone who fails a check. A vetted list of 15 outperforms a scraped list of 200.

5. **Choose the distribution strategy.** Decision point:
   - **Exclusive** (default for genuinely strong news): offer one tier-1 outlet the story alone, 7–10 business days before the date, with a 48-hour decision window stated in the pitch. If declined, move down the tier-1 list one at a time. After the exclusive publishes (or all decline), open to everyone.
   - **Embargo** (for big news needing simultaneous coverage, e.g., major funding, M&A): pitch 5–10 outlets 3–5 days out with "Would you like this under embargo until [date, time, timezone]?" Never send embargoed details until the journalist agrees to the embargo — agreement first, materials second.
   - **Open pitch** (default for everything else): staggered sends, tier 1 first, tiers 2–3 two days later.
   - **Newsjack**: skip tiers; speed wins. Pitch within 2–12 hours of the breaking story, 24 hours maximum, offering an expert comment of 2–3 quotable sentences ready to paste.

6. **Draft the pitches.** Per-tier customization: tier 1 gets fully bespoke emails; tiers 2–3 get a template with 3 personalized fields (first line, angle framing, why-them). Hard specs for every pitch: subject ≤60 characters and specific ("Data: 61% of nurses now use AI scheduling — new report" not "Exciting news from Acme"); body 100–150 words, 200 absolute max; the news and the number in the first two sentences; one clear offer (interview, data, exclusive, comment); zero attachments (link to an asset folder instead); no "I hope this finds you well," no "revolutionary/disruptive/game-changing." Plain text, no HTML templates, no press release pasted into the body — link to it.

7. **Send on journalist time.** Tuesday–Thursday, 7–10 a.m. in the *recipient's* timezone (many reporters triage before standups). Avoid Mondays, Friday afternoons, and the day of major industry events unless newsjacking. Send individually — never CC/BCC a media list; one visible mass-send can burn every relationship on it.

8. **Follow up on a fixed cadence, then stop.** Follow-up 1: 3 business days after the pitch, reply in-thread, 2–3 sentences, add one NEW element (fresh stat, new asset, customer available for interview) — never "just bumping this." Follow-up 2 (optional): 4–5 business days later, one line offering to close the loop. Then stop; two follow-ups maximum. Benchmarks to set expectations: 3–8% reply rate on cold pitches is normal, 10–20% on personalized tier-1/2 pitches with a real hook; roughly half of eventual replies come from a follow-up, not the original send. Silence is a "no for this story," not a "no forever."

9. **Run the reactive lane in parallel.** Monitor HARO/Connectively, Qwoted, Help a B2B Writer, and #journorequest on X daily. For each relevant query, respond within 2 hours (source requests are largely first-come, first-served), using the HARO response template below. This lane wins quotes and backlinks between campaigns and warms up reporters for future pitches.

10. **When coverage lands, work it.** Thank the journalist same-day (short, no notes on the piece unless there is a factual error — corrections politely, by email, never publicly). Share the article with the journalist tagged. Log the placement. Set a tickler to send that reporter something useful (data point, intro, comment offer) within 60 days — the second story is where relationships pay.

11. **Deliver the tracker and report.** Hand over the relationship tracker (schema below) populated with every contact and touch, plus a campaign summary: pitches sent, reply rate, placements, and the top 3 learnings for the next round.

## Frameworks & templates

### TRUE-D coverability filter (score the story, 1 point each)

- **T — Timely**: tied to a date, trend, season, or news cycle. Evergreen ≠ news.
- **R — Relevant**: matters to that outlet's specific readers, stated in their terms.
- **U — Unexpected**: contradicts an assumption, or a stat that makes people say "wait, really?"
- **E — Evidence**: a number, named customer, or demonstrable proof — not adjectives.
- **D — Drama (human stakes)**: a person affected, money at stake, a conflict or reversal.

3+/5 → pitch as news. 2/5 → repackage (data play, trend-tie, contrarian op-ed). 0–1 → tell the user there's no story yet and propose how to build one.

### Template 1 — Exclusive offer pitch

```
Subject: Exclusive: [company] [concrete news] — [most surprising number/detail]

Hi [First name],

Loved your piece on [their article, linked] — especially [specific point,
one clause]. This is squarely in that lane:

On [date], [company] is announcing [news in one sentence with the number:
"$12M Series A led by X" / "data from 40,000 users showing Y"].

Why it matters to [outlet]'s readers: [one sentence tying to a trend
they cover].

I'd like to offer you this as an exclusive. If it's a fit, I can get you
[CEO name, title] this week and the full data/materials today. Could you
let me know by [day, 48h out]? If it's not for you, no hard feelings —
I'll open it up after that.

[Name] · [role, company] · [phone]
```

### Template 2 — Newsjack / expert comment pitch

```
Subject: Expert comment on [breaking story] — [credential in 4 words]

Hi [First name],

Saw you're covering [breaking story]. If you're doing a follow-up,
[spokesperson name], [title] at [company] — who [one-line credential:
"ran fraud ops at PayPal for 6 years"] — can offer a take you likely
won't get elsewhere:

"[2–3 sentence quote, ready to use verbatim, containing one specific
number or concrete prediction.]"

Happy to make [him/her/them] available within the hour by phone, or use
the quote as-is with attribution. No follow-up needed if not useful.

[Name] · [phone]
```

### Template 3 — HARO / source-request response

```
Subject: [Their exact query title] — [credential in 5 words]

Hi [name if given, else "there"],

Responding to your query on [topic].

Credentials in one line: [Name], [title] at [company]; [one proof point:
years, scale, notable employer or client].

[Direct answer to their exact question: 2–4 tight paragraphs or 3
bullets. Include one stat and one concrete example. Quotable as-is —
assume they will paste it.]

Headshot, bio, and verification: [link]. Happy to expand or hop on a
call today. If used, attribution: [Name, Title, Company].

[Name] · [email] · [phone]
```

### Follow-up 1 (in-thread, day 3)

```
Hi [First name] — adding one thing since my note: [NEW element: "we can
now share the underlying dataset" / "[Customer] agreed to speak on the
record" / "the number is now X"]. Still yours if you want it — happy to
work around your deadline. If it's a pass, a one-word "pass" is welcome
and I'll stop here.
```

### Embargo language (paste into any pitch)

> "Happy to share full details under embargo until **Tuesday, March 10, 9:00 a.m. ET** — just confirm you'll honor the embargo and I'll send everything over."

Rule: agreement before materials, always. A journalist who never agreed to an embargo has no obligation to honor it.

### Relationship tracker schema (deliver as a table/CSV)

| Field | Example |

| Tier | 2 |
| Email + preferred channel | m.chen@… / email, active on X |
| Personalization hook | Wrote 3 pieces on nurse burnout, skeptical of AI hype |

| Touch log | 3/4 pitch sent · 3/9 FU1 · 3/11 replied, asked for data |
| Status | Pitched / Replied / Interviewing / Published / Passed / Dormant |

## Output format

Deliver a single outreach kit document containing, in order:

1. **Story memo** (½ page): the news, TRUE-D score with justification, 3 headline-formatted angles with the recommended one marked, chosen strategy (exclusive/embargo/open/newsjack) with reasoning.
2. **Target list**: 15–30 vetted contacts in the tracker schema, grouped by tier, each with a personalization hook and a "last covered this beat" date.
3. **Pitch set**: bespoke tier-1 pitch(es), the tier-2/3 template with personalization fields marked `[LIKE THIS]`, both follow-ups, and embargo language if applicable — every email within the length/subject specs.
4. **Send calendar**: dated table of who gets what and when, including the exclusive decision deadline, embargo lift time (with timezone), follow-up dates, and the stop date.
5. **Reactive-lane setup**: 3–5 saved-search/alert queries for HARO-style platforms and X, plus the response template pre-filled with the spokesperson's credentials.
6. **Asset checklist**: what must exist in the shared folder before send day (press release via **press-releases**, headshots, logo pack, data charts, boilerplate, spokesperson bio).

## Quality checklist

Pass/fail every item before delivering:

- [ ] Story scores ≥3/5 on TRUE-D, or the user was explicitly told it doesn't and given a repackaging path
- [ ] Each angle is written as a publishable headline ≤80 characters
- [ ] Every listed journalist has a byline on this beat dated within the last 90 days
- [ ] Every contact has a specific personalization hook (an article title or stated stance, not "covers tech")
- [ ] Every subject line is ≤60 characters and contains the news or the number
- [ ] Every pitch body is ≤200 words with the news in the first two sentences
- [ ] Zero attachments; assets are linked
- [ ] No banned phrases: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "disruptive," "I hope this finds you well," "quick question"
- [ ] Exclusive pitch states a decision deadline; embargo pitch requires agreement before materials
- [ ] Follow-up cadence is exactly: day 3 with a new element, optional day 7–8 close-out, then stop
- [ ] Send times fall Tue–Thu, 7–10 a.m. recipient's timezone (except newsjacks)
- [ ] No CC/BCC mass sends anywhere in the plan
- [ ] Tracker includes a next-action date for every non-dead contact
- [ ] Press release drafting is delegated to the press-releases skill, not duplicated here

## Common mistakes

- **Pitching the company instead of the story.** "Acme launches v2" is a fact about you; "61% of nurses now let AI build their schedules" is a story about the reader's world. Journalists cover the second.
- **Leading with the press release.** Pasting a 600-word release into the email body signals mass-blast. The pitch is 120 words; the release is a link.
- **Scraped lists from media databases used unvetted.** Beat changes and job moves make databases 20–40% stale at any moment; one "I haven't covered this in two years" reply marks you as a spammer to that reporter permanently.
- **Offering an "exclusive" to five outlets.** Journalists compare notes. A double-booked exclusive ends the relationship with all of them.
- **Sending embargoed details before agreement.** With no agreement, the journalist can publish immediately — and some will.
- **Following up daily, or calling to ask "did you get my email?"** The single most-cited journalist pet peeve. Two follow-ups, spaced, each adding value, then silence.
- **Newsjacking too slowly or too tastelessly.** A trend comment 3 days late is inventory nobody wants; a brand angle on a tragedy is a crisis of your own making. 24-hour window, judgment first.
- **Treating trade press as a consolation prize.** A trade placement read by 5,000 buyers usually outperforms a tier-1 mention read by nobody who buys. Match tier ambition to the actual goal from the inputs step.
- **Burying the number.** If the pitch's most surprising stat appears after sentence two, most readers on a phone preview never see it. Preview text shows ~90 characters — earn the open there.
- **Going dark after coverage.** The reporter who covered you once has demonstrated interest; failing to feed them anything for a year resets the relationship to cold. The 60-day tickler is not optional.

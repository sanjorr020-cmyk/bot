# Employee Onboarding

Design structured onboarding programs that take a new hire from signed offer to fully ramped contributor. The deliverable is always concrete and calendar-ready: a 30/60/90 plan with pass/fail exit criteria, a day-by-day first-week schedule, a provisioning checklist that is 100% complete before day one, a buddy assignment with defined time commitments, a manager check-in cadence, and feedback surveys at fixed milestones. Structured onboarding matters because roughly 20% of voluntary turnover happens in the first 45 days, and industry studies (Brandon Hall, Glassdoor) associate strong onboarding programs with 50-80% higher new-hire retention — the plan you build here is the difference between a hire that ramps in 90 days and one that quietly stalls or quits.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Onboard a specific new hire (any role, any level, remote or onsite)
- Create or improve a 30/60/90-day plan, first-week schedule, or day-one agenda
- Build an IT/tooling/access provisioning checklist
- Design or fix a buddy/mentor program
- Define role-specific ramp goals and how to measure them
- Set a manager check-in cadence for new hires
- Write onboarding feedback surveys (day 7 / 30 / 90, eNPS-style)
- Standardize onboarding across a team or company

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Onboarding customers or users to a product — use **customer-onboarding**
- Writing the job posting or defining the role — use **job-descriptions**
- Designing interviews or scorecards for candidates — use **interview-kits**
- Documenting the team's operating procedures the new hire will read — use **sop-writing** (this skill links to SOPs; it doesn't write them)
- Setting company or team OKRs — use **okrs-and-goals** (ramp goals here are individual, time-boxed, and onboarding-specific)

## Inputs to gather

Ask the user for these before drafting. When the user doesn't know, use the stated default and flag the assumption in the deliverable.

| Input | Why it matters | Default if unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Role title and level (junior/mid/senior/lead) | Drives ramp goals and autonomy expectations | Mid-level individual contributor |
| Start date | Anchors every date in the plan | First Monday of next month |
| Work mode (remote / hybrid / onsite) | Changes day-one logistics and buddy design | Hybrid, 2-3 office days/week |
| Manager name and team size | Check-in cadence, who owns which step | Team of 5-8, manager owns the plan |
| Company size | Determines formality (startup vs. enterprise process) | 20-200 employees |
| Tool stack (email, chat, docs, role-specific systems) | Access checklist contents | Google Workspace, Slack, Notion, plus 2-4 role tools |
| Existing onboarding assets (handbook, SOPs, prior plans) | Reuse instead of reinvent | None — build from scratch |
| What "fully ramped" means for this role | Anchors the day-90 exit criteria | Performing core duties independently at ~80% of tenured-peer output |
| Buddy candidates (peers with 1+ year tenure) | Buddy assignment | Ask the manager to nominate 2, hire's buddy is not their manager |
| Probation/review requirements (legal or policy) | Must align 30/60/90 reviews to formal reviews | 90-day informal review, no formal probation gate |

If the user gives you only a role title, proceed with all defaults and say so — do not stall the whole deliverable on missing inputs.

## Process

1. **Confirm scope.** Ask whether the user wants the full onboarding package or one component (e.g., just the 30/60/90 plan). Build only what's asked, but offer the full package once.

2. **Define "fully ramped" first, then work backward.** Write one sentence: "By day 90, [name/role] independently [core outputs] at [measurable level]." Every earlier milestone must ladder up to this. If the user can't articulate it, propose one based on the role (see benchmarks in step 5) and get confirmation.

3. **Build the pre-boarding checklist (offer signed → day 1).** Everything here completes BEFORE the start date:
   - Send welcome email 5-7 days before start: start time, location/video link, day-one agenda, what to bring, dress norms, parking/badge or shipping-tracking for equipment.
   - Order and ship/stage hardware 10+ business days out (remote hires: confirm delivery 3 business days before start).
   - Provision every account (see access checklist template) — target 100% before day 1; hard ceiling: within 4 business hours of start.
   - Assign the buddy and brief them (send them the buddy one-pager).
   - Manager blocks their own calendar: 60 min day-one welcome, daily 15-min touchpoints for week 1.
   - Book the recurring meetings NOW: weekly 1:1, team standups, 30/60/90 review slots.

4. **Draft the first-week schedule.** Use the template below. Rules:
   - Cap scheduled time at 4-5 hours/day; leave 30-40% of each day unstructured for setup, reading, and decompression.
   - Day 1 is logistics + belonging (manager welcome, buddy lunch, equipment/access verification) — no role work expected.
   - Every meeting on the schedule names an owner. Unowned meetings get dropped or reassigned.
   - By end of week 1 the hire should have: all access verified, met every teammate 1:1 or in small groups, shipped one tiny real contribution (see step 5), and know their 30-day goals.

5. **Write the 30/60/90 plan with measurable exit criteria.** Structure each phase as Learn (30) → Contribute (60) → Own (90). Every exit criterion must be verifiable by a third party — a number, an artifact, or a demonstrated behavior, never "understands X" or "is familiar with Y." Calibrate to role benchmarks:
   - **Engineers:** first PR merged by day 5-10; closing normal-sized tickets solo by day 30-45; on-call-ready or owning a small feature by day 60-90.
   - **Sales (AE/SDR):** certified on pitch by day 21-30 (recorded mock, scored on a rubric); running calls with shadow by day 30-45; carrying 50% quota by day 60, full quota by day 90-120 (enterprise sales: full ramp is realistically 4-6 months — say so rather than faking a 90-day full ramp).
   - **Support agents:** answering tickets with review by day 10-14; solo queue by day 21-30; at 70-80% of team-average tickets/day with CSAT within 5 points of team average by day 60-75.
   - **Marketing/ops/other ICs:** one shipped deliverable owned end-to-end by day 45-60.
   - Managers hired into leadership: replace output goals with listening-tour goals (all directs interviewed by day 21, team assessment written by day 45, first org/process change proposed by day 60-75).
   Include 3-5 exit criteria per phase — more than 6 dilutes focus.

6. **Build the tooling and access checklist.** Group by: identity (email, SSO, MFA), communication, docs/knowledge, role-specific systems, physical (badge, hardware), and payroll/HR. For each row: item, owner, deadline, status. Ask the user for their actual stack; fill gaps with the default stack from Inputs.

7. **Design the buddy system.** Buddy ≠ manager and ≠ trainer. Selection: same team or adjacent, 1+ year tenure, strong culture representative, volunteers preferred. Commitment: 3-4 hours/week in weeks 1-2, ~1 hour/week weeks 3-4, 30 min/week months 2-3; formal relationship ends at day 90. Give the buddy the one-pager template below. If the team is under 4 people or all-new, assign a cross-team buddy rather than skipping the program.

8. **Set the manager check-in cadence.** Default:
   - Week 1: daily 15-min touchpoint (end of day)
   - Weeks 2-4: 2x/week, 20-30 min
   - Months 2-3: weekly 30-min 1:1
   - Formal reviews: 45-60 min at day 30, 60, and 90, each scored against that phase's exit criteria with a written pass / needs-attention / at-risk outcome
   Each formal review answers three questions: Are exit criteria met? What blocked them? What changes in the next 30 days? At day 60, if 2+ criteria are unmet, the manager writes a get-well plan — do not let problems surface for the first time at day 90.

9. **Write the feedback surveys.** Three pulses: day 7 (logistics and welcome), day 30 (clarity and support), day 90 (ramp and retention signal). Keep each survey to 6-10 questions, under 5 minutes, mostly 1-5 Likert with 1-2 open text. Include one eNPS-style question at day 90 ("How likely are you to recommend working here to a friend?", 0-10). Route results to the hiring manager AND whoever owns onboarding company-wide; any item scoring ≤2/5 triggers a conversation within 3 business days.

10. **Assemble and sanity-check the deliverable.** Compile per the Output format section, run the Quality checklist, and flag every assumption you made in a short "Assumptions" note at the top so the user can correct them.

## Frameworks & templates

### The Learn → Contribute → Own arc (30/60/90 framework)

- **Days 1-30 — Learn:** absorb context, build relationships, ship one small real thing. Success = oriented and trusted with small tasks.
- **Days 31-60 — Contribute:** do real work with support, increasing volume. Success = reliable output with decreasing supervision.
- **Days 61-90 — Own:** independent ownership of a defined slice. Success = manager would re-hire them without hesitation ("keeper test").

### Template 1: 30/60/90 plan (fill-in-the-blank)

```
30/60/90 PLAN — [Name], [Role], starting [date]
Manager: [name]   Buddy: [name]   Fully-ramped definition: [one sentence]

DAYS 1-30 — LEARN
Goals:
- Complete [onboarding curriculum / required reading list]
- Meet 1:1 with [list of 5-10 key people]
- Ship first small contribution: [specific tiny deliverable]
Exit criteria (verified at day-30 review):
- [ ] [Artifact or number, e.g., "First PR merged" / "Pitch certification passed at 80%+ on rubric"]
- [ ] [ ... 3-5 total, each third-party verifiable ]
Support needed: [training, shadowing, docs]

DAYS 31-60 — CONTRIBUTE
Goals:
- [Real workload at ~50-70% of tenured-peer volume]
- [One deliverable owned with review]
Exit criteria (verified at day-60 review):
- [ ] [e.g., "Closed 12+ tickets solo" / "Ran 10 discovery calls, 3 progressed to stage 2"]
- [ ] [ ... ]
If 2+ criteria unmet at day 60: manager writes get-well plan within 5 business days.

DAYS 61-90 — OWN
Goals:
- [Independent ownership of: named project, territory, queue, or system]
Exit criteria (verified at day-90 review):
- [ ] [e.g., "Owns feature X end-to-end, shipped to production" / "At 70-80% of team-average output with quality within 5 points of team average"]
- [ ] [ ... ]
Day-90 outcome: ☐ Fully ramped  ☐ Extended ramp plan (30 more days, specific gaps listed)  ☐ Performance conversation
```

### Worked example: mid-level Account Executive (SaaS, $30-60k ACV)

- **Day 30 exit criteria:** pitch certification passed (recorded 20-min mock demo scored ≥80% on the team rubric); shadowed 8+ live calls with written notes; CRM hygiene training complete; can articulate all 3 customer personas and top 3 competitors without notes (verbal check with manager).
- **Day 60 exit criteria:** ran 15+ discovery calls as lead with manager shadowing ≤50% of them; 4+ opportunities self-sourced or progressed to demo stage; carrying 50% quota; call scores averaging ≥3.5/5 on the rubric.
- **Day 90 exit criteria:** full pipeline coverage at 3x of ramped quota; carrying 100% quota (note: expect full attainment by month 4-6, not day 90); first closed-won or clear path to one; forecast accuracy within ±20% for the month.

### Template 4: Buddy program one-pager (give this to the buddy)

```
You're [Name]'s onboarding buddy for their first 90 days. You are NOT their
manager or performance evaluator — you're their safe person for "dumb" questions.

Time commitment: 3-4 hrs/week (weeks 1-2), ~1 hr/week (weeks 3-4),
30 min/week (months 2-3). Formal role ends at day 90.

Your jobs:
1. Day 1: greet them, lunch/coffee, help verify tool access.
2. Weeks 1-2: daily informal check-in (can be async). Explain unwritten rules —
   how decisions really get made, meeting norms, who knows what.
3. Weeks 1-4: review their first pieces of work before wider visibility.
4. Ongoing: introduce them to at least 5 people outside the immediate team.
5. Escalate privately to the manager if you see them stuck or disengaged —
   that's help, not snitching.

You will never be asked to formally evaluate them. If asked "how are they
doing?" in a review context, redirect to the manager.
```

### Survey question banks

**Day 7 (6 questions, Likert 1-5 unless noted):** My equipment and access were ready when I started. / My first-week schedule was clear. / I know what's expected of me in my first 30 days. / My buddy has been helpful. / I felt welcomed by the team. / Open text: What's one thing that would have made week 1 better?

**Day 30 (8 questions):** I understand how my role contributes to team goals. / I have the tools and information to do my job. / My manager's check-ins are useful. / I'm getting enough feedback on my work. / The training so far matches what my job actually requires. / I know who to ask when I'm stuck. / Open text: What's still confusing? / Open text: What should we keep doing?

**Day 90 (8 questions):** I can perform the core parts of my role independently. / The 30/60/90 plan matched the reality of the job. / I see a path to grow here. / The job matches what I was told during hiring. (Score ≤2 here is a red flag for **job-descriptions** and **interview-kits** accuracy — feed it back.) / eNPS: 0-10 recommend question. / Open text: What almost made you disengage? / Open text: One change for the next hire? / Open text: Anything else?

## Output format

Deliver a single markdown document titled "Onboarding Plan — [Name/Role], starting [date]" with these sections in order:

1. **Assumptions** — bullets listing every default you applied (3-6 lines).
2. **Fully-ramped definition** — the one-sentence day-90 target.
3. **Pre-boarding checklist** — dated, owner-assigned tasks (offer → day 1).
4. **Tooling & access checklist** — the table from Template 3, populated.
5. **First-week schedule** — day-by-day, from Template 2, adapted to the role and work mode.
6. **30/60/90 plan** — from Template 1, fully populated with role-specific measurable exit criteria.
7. **Buddy assignment** — who, why, and the one-pager.
8. **Manager check-in cadence** — table of meeting, frequency, duration, purpose.
9. **Feedback surveys** — the three question sets with send dates and score-trigger rules.

If the user asked for only one component, deliver that component plus the fully-ramped definition (nothing works without it). Keep the full package under ~3 pages equivalent; put role-specific detail in the 30/60/90 section, not repeated everywhere.

## Quality checklist

Verify every item before delivering; fix any failure.

1. Fully-ramped definition exists and is one sentence with a measurable level.
2. Every 30/60/90 exit criterion is third-party verifiable (number, artifact, or observable demo) — zero instances of "understands," "is familiar with," or "is comfortable with."
3. Each phase has 3-5 exit criteria, never more than 6.
4. Ramp targets match realistic role benchmarks (e.g., enterprise AE full quota is NOT promised at day 90).
5. Access checklist has an owner and a deadline on every row, and everything except badge/day-1 physical items is dated before the start date.
6. First-week schedule caps scheduled time at 4-5 hours/day with 30-40% unstructured time visible on every day.
7. Every scheduled meeting names an owner other than the new hire.
8. A concrete first contribution is assigned by day 3 and delivered by day 5-10.
9. Buddy is not the manager, has stated time commitments, and the relationship has a 90-day end date.
10. Check-in cadence includes daily week-1 touchpoints and formal 30/60/90 reviews with a written pass/needs-attention/at-risk outcome.
11. Day-60 review includes the get-well-plan trigger (2+ unmet criteria).
12. All three surveys exist, each ≤10 questions, with the day-90 eNPS item and the ≤2/5 escalation rule stated.
13. Every date in the document is an actual date derived from the start date, not "week 1" alone.
14. Assumptions section lists every default applied.

## Common mistakes

- **The firehose week one.** Scheduling 7 hours/day of meetings and trainings in week 1. Retention of that content is near zero and the hire ends the week exhausted with no setup done. Cap at 4-5 hours/day, spread deep-dives into weeks 2-3.
- **Access as an afterthought.** The hire spends days 1-3 waiting on logins. This is the single most common day-7 survey complaint. Provisioning is a pre-boarding task with owners and deadlines, verified by the hire before noon on day 1.
- **Activity goals masquerading as exit criteria.** "Attend sales training" is an activity; "pass pitch certification at ≥80% on the rubric" is an exit criterion. If nobody can mark it pass/fail, rewrite it.
- **Copy-pasting one 30/60/90 for every role.** An SDR, a senior engineer, and a new VP have completely different ramp shapes. Managers hired into leadership need listening-tour goals, not output goals, in the first 30 days.
- **Making the manager the buddy.** The whole value of a buddy is a judgment-free channel. Questions the hire won't ask the person writing their review are exactly the questions that matter.
- **Onboarding ends when HR paperwork does.** Treating day 5 as "onboarded." Real ramp is 90 days minimum (4-6 months for enterprise sales and senior leadership); the check-in cadence and buddy program must run the full arc.
- **First feedback at day 90.** By then, fixable week-1 problems have hardened into disengagement. Pulse at day 7 and day 30, and act on any ≤2/5 score within 3 business days.
- **No first contribution.** A hire who ships nothing real in the first two weeks starts doubting the role. Pre-select a small, genuinely useful, low-risk task (a doc fix, a small PR, a shadow-then-run call) before they start.
- **Problems surfacing for the first time at day 90.** If the day-90 review is where the manager first says "this isn't working," the day-30 and day-60 reviews were theater. The day-60 get-well trigger exists to force the hard conversation early.
- **Faking sales ramp math.** Promising full quota at day 90 for a 6-month-cycle enterprise role sets the hire up to look like a failure. State the realistic ramp (50% quota day 60, 100% by month 4-6) and pro-rate targets accordingly.

# Podcast Production

Take a podcast from idea to distributable episode. This skill covers the full production loop: designing the show format, sourcing and booking guests, building interview question arcs that get answers guests haven't given a hundred times, running a clean recording session, writing show notes and titles that earn plays, and extracting short clips for social distribution. The goal every time is a repeatable episode pipeline, not a one-off recording.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Design or refine a podcast concept, format, or episode structure
- Find, vet, and book guests (including writing the pitch/booking messages)
- Prepare for an interview: research briefs, question arcs, pre-call notes
- Build recording checklists or run-of-show documents
- Write show notes, episode descriptions, or episode titles
- Identify clip-worthy moments in a transcript and package them for social

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Scripted YouTube videos or video essays — use **youtube-scripts**
- Editing specs, gear-heavy video shoots, or multi-camera production — use **video-production**
- Turning finished episodes into blog posts, newsletters, or threads at scale — use **content-repurposing** (this skill hands off clip candidates and show notes to it)
- Captioned vertical clip strategy beyond selection (hooks, pacing, platform posting cadence) — use **short-form-video**
- Live webinars or presentation scripts — use **webinar-scripts**
- Pitching the HOST as a guest on other people's shows — use **media-outreach**

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these before starting. If the user doesn't know, apply the default and say you did.

1. **Show stage**: New show, existing show (<20 episodes), or established (20+). Default: new show.
2. **Audience and niche**: Who listens and why. If vague, run a 3-question narrowing: Who exactly? What problem or curiosity brings them? What do they do after listening? (For deep audience work, point to **customer-personas**.)
3. **Format**: Interview, solo/monologue, co-hosted conversation, panel, or narrative/documentary. Default: interview (lowest production cost per episode).
4. **Episode length target**: Default 30-45 min for interview shows, 10-20 min for solo, 20-35 min for co-hosted. Under 20 min suits commute listening; over 60 min only works for established shows with proven retention.
5. **Cadence**: Default weekly. Biweekly is acceptable; less than biweekly kills algorithmic momentum and listener habit. Never recommend daily to a new podcaster.
6. **Recording setup**: Remote (Riverside, Zencastr, SquadCast) or in-person; audio-only or video. Default: remote, video recorded (video unlocks clips even if the show is audio-first).
7. **Task at hand**: Which part of the pipeline they need right now — planning, booking, prep, recording, post, or clips. Deliver that piece; offer the adjacent pieces after.
8. **For show notes/clips tasks**: The transcript or recording summary. If none exists, ask for the raw transcript export (Riverside, Descript, and most tools produce one) — never invent quotes.

## Process

### Phase 1 — Show and episode planning

1. **Define the show promise in one sentence**: "[Show name] helps [audience] [outcome] through [format] with [differentiator]." If the user can't fill this in, workshop it before anything else — every downstream decision (guests, titles, clips) depends on it.
2. **Pick the episode skeleton** and lock it so every episode is predictable to produce:
   - Cold open (best 20-40 second moment from the episode, pulled in post) → intro (host, show promise, guest one-liner; max 60 seconds) → body (3-5 segments) → payoff segment (the "keep them subscribed" recurring bit) → outro with ONE call to action.
   - A recurring payoff segment (e.g., "the one thing you'd tell your younger self", a rapid-fire round, a listener question) trains listeners to stay to the end and gives you a reliable clip source.
3. **Plan in batches of 3-5 episodes**, not one at a time. Map each episode to a listener question the show promise implies. Check for topic variety: no two adjacent episodes on the same subtopic.
4. **Decision point — format fit**: If the user's topic requires heavy storytelling (true crime, history, brand documentary), flag that narrative format needs 5-10x the production time per episode (scripting, sound design, multiple takes) and confirm they accept that before proceeding.

### Phase 2 — Guest sourcing and booking

5. **Build a guest scorecard** before listing names. Score candidates 1-5 on: (a) audience relevance — do they know things the listener wants; (b) talkability — have they been articulate on other podcasts, panels, or posts; (c) reach — will they share the episode (own audience of 5k+ is a bonus, not a requirement); (d) freshness — do they have a new book/launch/story that gives them a reason to say yes. A guest scoring 4+ on talkability and relevance beats a big name scoring 2 on talkability.
6. **Source 3x the guests you need** (booking rates for cold guest outreach run 20-40% for niche shows, higher when there's a warm intro). Sources: guests of adjacent podcasts (check the last 10 episodes of 3 competitor shows), authors with books launching in the next 90 days, conference speaker lists, active posters in the niche on LinkedIn/X, the user's own network and past customers.
7. **Write the booking pitch** using the Guest Pitch Template below. Keep it under 150 words. Lead with why THEM specifically (reference one concrete thing they said or made), state the ask precisely (format, length, remote, date window), and lower the effort bar (no prep required, you'll send questions ahead). For sequencing follow-ups (send 1 bump at day 4-6, a second at day 12-14, then stop), reuse mechanics from **follow-up-sequences**.
8. **On yes, send a one-message logistics pack**: recording link, date/time with timezone, tech asks (headphones, quiet room, external mic if available, laptop not phone, hardwired internet if possible), the topic arc (3-4 bullets, not the full question list), and a release/consent line ("we record video and audio and may publish clips — reply to confirm you're OK with that").

### Phase 3 — Interview prep and question arcs

9. **Research the guest for 20-30 minutes, output a one-page brief**: their current role and the 2-3 stories they always tell (from other interviews — these are the answers to AVOID re-asking), one contrarian or underexplored angle, names/dates/numbers to get right, and anything off-limits.
10. **Build the question arc, not a question list.** Use the Arc framework in the templates section: 8-12 prepared questions max for a 40-minute interview, sequenced from concrete → story → tension → takeaway. The host should expect to use 60-70% of them and follow curiosity for the rest.
11. **Canned-answer test every question**: if the guest has answered it in another interview, on their about page, or in their book intro, cut it or sharpen it. Convert résumé questions ("tell us about your background") into moment questions ("what was the week you decided to quit?"). Convert opinion questions ("what do you think about X?") into stakes questions ("where have you changed your mind about X, and what changed it?").
12. **Write the cold-open hunt list**: 2-3 questions specifically engineered to produce a quotable 20-40 second answer (strong claim, surprising number, vivid story beat). Mark them in the prep doc so the host knows to let those answers breathe.

### Phase 4 — Recording

13. **Deliver the Recording Checklist** (template below), split into T-24h, T-10min, during, and immediately-after. Insist on local recording (each participant recorded on their own machine) — a dropped call with only a cloud recording is the most common unrecoverable failure in remote podcasting.
14. **During-recording rules for the host**: get a clean 10-second room-tone recording, ask the guest to restate the question's premise in answers when useful for clips ("So the reason we missed the launch was..."), and when a great moment lands, stay silent 2 full seconds before the next question — editors need the gap.
15. **Immediately after**: confirm all local tracks uploaded/backed up before ending the call, capture 3 timestamps of standout moments while memory is fresh, and ask the guest for their preferred name spelling, title, and links for the show notes.

### Phase 5 — Show notes and titles

16. **Write show notes from the transcript** using the Show Notes Template below. Structure: 1-2 sentence hook (the episode's most interesting claim, not a summary), guest one-liner, 4-7 timestamped chapters, 3-5 key takeaways as full sentences, links mentioned, and the single CTA. First 120 characters of the description matter most — Apple Podcasts and Spotify truncate around there in browse views.
17. **Generate 5-8 title options**, then recommend one. Title rules: front-load the payoff in the first 40 characters (mobile truncation), name the guest only if the guest name carries search or recognition weight, prefer specific claims over topics ("Why We Killed Our Best-Selling Product" beats "Product Strategy with Jane Doe"), 45-60 characters is the sweet spot, and never lead with the episode number. Format guest episodes as "Claim or question — Guest Name" or "Guest Name: Claim" only when the name pulls weight.
18. **Decision point — SEO**: if the show publishes notes as web pages and the user cares about search traffic, hand keyword targeting to **seo-content-briefs** rather than stuffing the notes.

### Phase 6 — Clip extraction

19. **Scan the transcript for clip candidates** using the Clip Selection Criteria (templates below). Target 3-6 clips per episode. Each clip needs: a hook in the first 2 seconds (claim, question, or tension — never wind-up), self-contained context (a viewer who never heard the show must follow it), and one idea only.
20. **Package each clip as a spec**: timestamp in/out, verbatim opening line, suggested on-screen hook text (under 60 characters), target platform and length — 15-45s for Shorts/Reels/TikTok, 60-90s acceptable on LinkedIn and X. Trim clip starts mid-sentence into the strong line; cut the host's question when the answer restates the premise.
21. **Hand off**: pass clip specs to **short-form-video** for captioning/pacing/posting strategy, and the full episode to **content-repurposing** for newsletter/blog/thread derivatives.

## Frameworks & templates

### Guest Pitch Template (cold outreach, <150 words)

```
Subject: [Guest first name] on [Show Name] — [specific angle]?

Hi [First name],

Your [specific piece: post/talk/book chapter] on [topic] — especially the
point about [one concrete detail] — is exactly the conversation my
listeners keep asking for.

I host [Show Name], a [cadence] podcast for [audience] ([social proof:
episode count, notable past guest, or download range — pick ONE, only if
true]). I'd love to have you on to dig into [specific angle — not their
whole career].

Logistics: [30-45] min, remote via [tool], video + audio. No prep needed —
I'll send the topic arc ahead, and you'll get the episode and clips to
share if you want to.

Would any week in [month] work? Happy to fit your calendar.

[Host name]
```

Bump at day 4-6: two sentences, reference the specific angle again, offer an alternate month. One more at day 12-14, then stop.

### Interview Arc Framework (Concrete → Story → Tension → Takeaway)

Sequence 8-12 questions across four movements for a 40-minute interview:

1. **Concrete (min 0-5)** — Start in the middle of something specific, never with a bio. "Walk me through [specific recent event/decision] — what did that day actually look like?" Warms the guest up with easy recall, and gives listeners immediate substance.
2. **Story (min 5-20)** — 3-4 questions that pull narrative: origin of the interesting thing (not of the person), the moment it almost failed, the decision nobody agreed with. Follow-up prompts to keep loaded: "What happened next?" / "What did that cost you?" / "Who told you that was a bad idea?"
3. **Tension (min 20-32)** — 2-3 questions the guest hasn't pre-packaged: "Where have you changed your mind about [their known position]?" / "What's the strongest argument against your approach?" / "What do people get wrong when they copy what you did?" This is where cold-open material lives.
4. **Takeaway (min 32-40)** — Recurring payoff segment, one "what should a listener do this week" question, then the wrap. End the prep doc with the guest's links and preferred plug.

**Worked example** — guest is a founder who bootstrapped to $2M ARR:
- Concrete: "You posted that you almost sold the company in March for a number you now call embarrassing. What was the number, and what was the day you got the offer like?"
- Story: "Take me back to the first month of charging money — who was customer #1 and what did they actually pay for?"
- Tension: "Bootstrapping is your brand now. Steelman the case that you'd be at $10M ARR if you'd raised."
- Takeaway: "Someone listening is at $10k MRR and stuck. What's the one report they should pull this week?"

### Recording Checklist Template

```
T-24 HOURS
[ ] Confirm with guest (time + timezone + link), reconfirm consent line
[ ] Prep doc finalized: brief, question arc, cold-open hunt list
[ ] Test recording platform login; check for pending software updates
[ ] Charge/connect: mic, headphones, camera; clear 20GB+ disk space

T-10 MINUTES
[ ] Close notification apps; phone on silent, out of reach
[ ] Hardwired internet if available; others off the video call network
[ ] Levels check: speak at show volume, peaks around -12 to -6 dB
[ ] Local recording ON for every participant (not just cloud)
[ ] Frame check if video: eyes top third, light in front not behind

DURING
[ ] Record 10 seconds of room tone before the intro
[ ] Timestamp standout moments in the prep doc as they happen
[ ] 2-second silence after strong answers before next question
[ ] If tech fails: stop, fix, re-ask the last question from the top

IMMEDIATELY AFTER
[ ] Keep call open until all local tracks finish uploading
[ ] Back up raw files to a second location before closing anything
[ ] Note 3 best moments + rough timestamps
[ ] Get guest's name spelling, title, links, headshot for notes
[ ] Send thank-you within 24h with expected publish date
```

### Show Notes Template

```
[HOOK — 1-2 sentences, the most surprising claim in the episode.
First 120 characters must stand alone.]

[Guest name] is [one-line credential tied to this episode's topic].
In this episode: [the arc in one sentence].

CHAPTERS
00:00 — Cold open
[mm:ss] — [Chapter as a payoff, not a topic: "The $40k mistake in the
first launch" not "Discussing launches"]
[4-7 chapters total]

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- [Full-sentence insight a listener could act on — 3 to 5 of these]

LINKS
- [Everything mentioned by name in the episode]
- [Guest's preferred link]

[ONE call to action: subscribe, review, or the show's single offer.]
```

### Clip Selection Criteria (score each candidate 0-2 per axis; ship clips scoring 7+/10)

- **Hook speed**: strong claim, number, or question within the first 2 seconds after trim
- **Self-contained**: zero prior context needed; no unexplained "it/that/she"
- **One idea**: a single claim or story beat, not a compressed segment
- **Emotion or stakes**: surprise, disagreement, money, failure, or a laugh
- **Speaker energy**: delivery is animated at that moment, not trailing off

## Output format

Match the deliverable to the requested phase; always deliver as structured markdown the user can paste into their docs:

- **Show/episode plan**: show promise sentence, locked episode skeleton with segment timings, and a 3-5 episode batch table (working title, listener question answered, guest or solo, status).
- **Guest booking pack**: scored guest shortlist table (name, why-them, talkability evidence, score), filled pitch email, bump messages, and the logistics-pack message.
- **Interview prep doc** (1-2 pages): guest brief, 8-12 question arc labeled by movement, cold-open hunt questions marked, off-limits notes, guest links.
- **Recording checklist**: the template above customized to the user's tools and format.
- **Show notes + titles**: completed show notes template plus 5-8 title options with character counts and one recommended pick with a one-line rationale.
- **Clip sheet**: 3-6 clips, each with timestamp in/out, verbatim opening line, hook text (<60 chars), score against the criteria, target platform and length.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Show promise fits the one-sentence formula with a real differentiator, not "conversations about X"
- [ ] Episode skeleton includes a cold open and a recurring payoff segment
- [ ] Guest shortlist has 3x needed guests, each scored on the 4-axis scorecard with evidence for talkability
- [ ] Guest pitch is under 150 words, references one concrete thing the guest made, and states length/format/date window
- [ ] Interview prep contains 8-12 questions max, sequenced Concrete → Story → Tension → Takeaway
- [ ] Zero questions in the arc that the guest has already answered in a findable interview or bio
- [ ] At least 2 cold-open hunt questions are marked in the prep doc
- [ ] Recording checklist mandates local recording per participant and a pre-call backup plan
- [ ] Show notes hook survives truncation at 120 characters; chapters are payoffs, not topics
- [ ] Every title option is 45-60 characters with the payoff in the first 40; counts shown
- [ ] All quotes in notes and clips are verbatim from the provided transcript — nothing invented
- [ ] Each clip scores 7+/10 on the selection criteria and opens with the hook, not wind-up
- [ ] Exactly one CTA in the outro and one in the show notes
- [ ] Out-of-scope work (SEO, repurposing, short-form strategy) is handed to the named sibling skill, not half-done here

## Common mistakes

- **Opening with "tell us about yourself."** It produces a 4-minute résumé recitation that kills retention in the exact window (first 5 minutes) where most listener drop-off happens. Start mid-story; put credentials in the host intro instead.
- **Booking for name recognition over talkability.** A famous guest giving flat, rehearsed answers produces a worse episode and worse clips than an unknown operator with vivid stories. The scorecard exists to prevent this.
- **Sending the full question list to the guest.** Guests pre-script answers and the conversation dies. Send a 3-4 bullet topic arc only.
- **Cloud-recording only.** One dropped connection and the episode is gone. Local tracks per participant, every time, no exceptions.
- **Writing chapter markers as topics ("Marketing discussion") instead of payoffs ("The ad channel that returned 8x").** Topic chapters get skipped; payoff chapters get tapped.
- **Titling episodes "Ep. 47: Interview with Jane Doe."** Episode numbers and the word "interview" carry zero pull. Lead with the claim; the number belongs in metadata.
- **Clipping the setup instead of the payoff.** A clip that spends 8 seconds on the host's question loses the viewer before the guest speaks. Trim into the strongest sentence, even mid-answer.
- **Launching at daily or even weekly-plus cadence without a batch buffer.** Recommend a 4-6 episode bank before episode 1 goes live; podfade at episode 7-10 is the most common way shows die.
- **Interviewing to the list instead of the guest.** Rigidly executing all 12 questions ignores the best material. The arc is a map; the host's job is to chase the unexpected answer with "what happened next?"
- **Inventing quotes or timestamps when the transcript wasn't provided.** If there's no transcript, produce the structure with clearly marked slots and ask for the export — never fabricate.

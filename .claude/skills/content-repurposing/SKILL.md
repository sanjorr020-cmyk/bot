# Content Repurposing

Take one substantive piece of content and systematically convert it into 10-30 platform-native derivative pieces, so the user's best thinking reaches every channel their audience uses without creating everything from scratch. The core discipline: extract discrete ideas from the pillar, then REWRITE each one for its destination platform's format, length, and culture — never paste the same text into five places.

## When to use this skill

Use when the user has (or is planning) a substantial "pillar" asset and wants derivatives from it: blog post → social posts, webinar → clips + newsletter, podcast → threads + carousels, case study → proof-point posts. Also use when the user wants a repeatable repurposing system or wants to know which derivatives to prioritize.

Do NOT use this skill when the ask is really:
- Creating the pillar itself — route to **blog-writing**, **youtube-scripts**, **webinar-scripts**, **case-studies**, or **podcast-production**.
- Deciding what topics to cover or overall content pillars — that's **content-strategy**.
- Building the posting schedule across weeks — that's **social-content-calendar** (this skill hands finished derivatives to that one).
- Writing net-new platform content unrelated to a pillar — **linkedin-content**, **twitter-x-content**, or **short-form-video**.
- Designing the whole newsletter — **email-newsletters** (this skill supplies one section, not the issue).
- Choosing which platforms to be on at all — **social-media-strategy**.

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these before starting. Where the user shrugs, apply the default.

1. **The pillar asset** (required). Get the full text, transcript, or link. If it's video/audio with no transcript, ask for one or work from the user's outline/notes. If they haven't created the pillar yet, offer to design the extraction map first so the pillar is written to be splintered (this is the better order).
2. **Target platforms.** Default: LinkedIn + X/Twitter + email newsletter, since those cover most B2B cases. Add Instagram, TikTok/Reels/Shorts, or YouTube community posts only if the user is active there — never repurpose into a channel nobody maintains.
3. **Audience and goal per platform.** Default: same audience as the pillar; goal = drive profile-level authority first, pillar clicks second (native-first content outperforms link-first content on every social platform).
4. **Voice constraints.** Ask for 1-2 example posts the user liked from their own account. Default: first person, plain language, no hashtag walls, contractions allowed.
5. **Volume and horizon.** Default: 10-15 derivatives spread over 4-6 weeks. One good pillar should not be exhausted in one week.
6. **Whether links/CTAs are allowed.** Some users want pure value posts; some need every piece to point at a signup. Default: 70% no-CTA value pieces, 30% with a soft CTA.
7. **Tracking setup.** Ask what analytics they have (platform native, UTM discipline, a spreadsheet). Default: the tracking sheet in Output format below plus UTM-tagged links (`utm_medium=social`, `utm_campaign=<pillar-slug>`, `utm_content=<derivative-id>`).

## Process

1. **Audit the pillar for repurposing worthiness.** A good pillar has at least 5 discrete extractable ideas: a contrarian claim, a numbered framework, a statistic or result, a story/anecdote, and a how-to sequence. If the pillar is thin (one idea stretched over 800 words), tell the user directly and either repurpose at reduced volume (3-5 derivatives) or recommend strengthening the pillar first.

2. **Build the extraction map.** Read the pillar and list every atomic idea as a one-line "idea card": the claim, the supporting evidence, and where in the pillar it lives (section heading or timestamp). Aim for 8-15 idea cards from a 1,500-2,500 word post or a 30-60 minute recording. Number them (P1, P2, ...) — these IDs flow into the tracking sheet.

3. **Score and select ideas.** Rate each idea card 1-3 on two axes: *standalone strength* (does it make sense with zero context from the pillar?) and *audience tension* (does it challenge an assumption, promise a payoff, or name a pain?). Only ideas scoring 2+ on both become derivatives. Typical yield: 60-75% of cards survive.

4. **Assign formats using the derivative matrix.** For each surviving idea, pick the format(s) it naturally fits — don't force every idea into every format:
   - Contrarian claim → single text post (LinkedIn and/or X), possibly a 30-45s talking-head script.
   - Numbered framework or listicle section → carousel (6-10 slides) and/or thread (5-9 tweets).
   - Statistic or customer result → short proof post + newsletter item; pair with **case-studies** output if one exists.
   - Story/anecdote → LinkedIn narrative post (150-300 words) or 45-60s video script.
   - How-to sequence → thread, carousel, or 60-90s screen-recorded walkthrough script.
   - The pillar's single best insight → gets 2-3 derivatives in different formats; it's the anchor.
   A healthy plan from one pillar: 4-6 text posts, 1-2 threads, 1-2 carousels, 2-3 short video scripts, 1 newsletter item. That's 10-14 pieces.

5. **Write each derivative platform-natively.** This is the step that separates repurposing from spam. Apply the platform rules:
   - **LinkedIn:** 150-300 words for narrative posts; first 210 characters must work standalone (that's roughly the "...see more" cutoff). One idea per post. Line breaks every 1-2 sentences. 0-3 hashtags. Links depress reach — put them in a comment or skip them.
   - **X/Twitter:** single posts ≤280 characters, punchy and declarative. Threads: 5-9 tweets, tweet 1 is a hook that promises a specific payoff, one idea per tweet, final tweet recaps + optional CTA. No "1/9 🧵" clichés unless the user's voice uses them.
   - **Carousel (LinkedIn PDF or Instagram):** 6-10 slides. Slide 1 = hook only (max ~8 words, large type). One point per slide, ≤25 words each. Last slide = summary + CTA. Write it as slide-by-slide copy, not prose.
   - **Short video script (Reels/TikTok/Shorts):** 30-60 seconds ≈ 75-150 spoken words. Hook in the first 2 seconds (first line on screen and spoken). Structure: hook → payoff → proof → CTA. Write in spoken register — read it aloud mentally. Hand production specifics to **short-form-video**.
   - **Newsletter item:** 80-150 words framed as "here's the takeaway + why it matters to you," ending with a link to the pillar. This slots into an issue owned by **email-newsletters**.
   Rewrite, don't trim: change the opening, the framing, and often the angle. A LinkedIn post and a tweet from the same idea should share a conclusion, not sentences.

6. **Write hooks last, and write 2-3 per derivative.** The hook determines 80% of performance on social. For each piece, draft alternatives using different hook types (see Frameworks) and pick the strongest; keep the runner-up for a re-run 60+ days later.

7. **Batch the production.** Recommend the user work in format batches, not piece-by-piece: all text posts in one sitting, all carousel copy in another, all video scripts in a third. Context-switching between formats is the main time sink. A practiced operator turns one pillar into 12 derivatives in 2-4 focused hours; with this skill doing drafting, budget 45-90 minutes of user review time.

8. **Sequence the release.** Spread derivatives over 4-6 weeks, lead with the strongest hook, and never post two derivatives of the same idea on the same platform within 14 days. Alternate formats day to day. Hand the actual calendar slots to **social-content-calendar**.

9. **Set up tracking.** Create the tracking sheet (Output format below). Every derivative gets its ID, platform, format, source idea card, post date, and metric columns. Define the platform's primary metric up front: LinkedIn = impressions + comments; X = impressions + replies/reposts; carousels = completion proxy (likes vs. reach); video = 3-second hold rate and completion %; newsletter item = click-through on the pillar link.

10. **Review performance and recycle.** After 2-4 weeks, check which idea cards outperformed. Rules of thumb: a derivative beating the account's 90-day median engagement by 2x+ is a winner — spin 2-3 more derivatives from that same idea card, and consider promoting the idea into its own pillar. A piece at 0.3x median or below gets one retry with a new hook (same body) before retirement; if the retry also flops, retire the idea card, not just the post. Top evergreen derivatives can be re-posted verbatim after 60-90 days on X, 90+ days on LinkedIn. Deeper measurement questions go to **analytics-reporting**.

11. **Institutionalize the loop.** If the user publishes pillars regularly, turn steps 1-10 into a standing routine: extraction map within 48 hours of pillar publication, derivatives drafted in one batch session, tracking sheet reviewed on a fixed weekly slot. Offer to codify this as a one-page runbook; if they want it formalized as a team procedure, hand that to **sop-writing**.

## Frameworks & templates

### The extraction map template

```
PILLAR: <title> | <URL or file> | <word count / duration>
GOAL: <what the derivatives should drive>

ID  | Idea (one sentence, standalone)          | Evidence/detail        | Location   | Standalone | Tension | Formats
P1  | <claim>                                  | <stat, story, example> | <H2 / 12:40> | 1-3      | 1-3     | <post/thread/carousel/video/newsletter>
P2  | ...
```

### Worked example — one idea, three platforms

Pillar idea card (P3): "Most onboarding emails fail because they teach features instead of driving the first win. Our data: users who complete one core action in 24h retain at 3x the rate."

**LinkedIn post (native rewrite):**
> We rewrote our onboarding emails and retention jumped.
>
> The old emails taught features. Tour of the dashboard. Overview of settings. Nobody cared.
>
> The new emails do one thing: push the user to a single core action in the first 24 hours.
>
> That's it. One action.
>
> Users who take it retain at 3x the rate of users who don't.
>
> Your onboarding email isn't a manual. It's a countdown.

**Tweet (not a trimmed LinkedIn post — a different angle):**
> Onboarding emails that tour your features are a waste of everyone's time. One job: get the user to one core action in 24 hours. Ours retain 3x when they do.

**Newsletter item:**
> **Stop touring, start counting down.** This week I broke down why most onboarding sequences underperform: they teach features instead of driving a first win. The short version — users who complete one core action within 24 hours retained at 3x the rate in our data. Full teardown, including the exact email rewrite, here: <link>.

Note what changed: opening line, framing, sentence rhythm, and CTA — while the conclusion stayed identical.

### Worked example — thread from a framework idea card

Idea card (P5): "Our 4-step process for cutting churn: instrument the drop-off, interview 5 churned users, fix the single biggest leak, re-measure in 30 days."

```
T1: We cut churn 22% in one quarter without shipping a single new feature.
    The process fits in 4 steps. Here it is.
T2: Most teams treat churn as a product problem. It's usually a measurement
    problem — nobody knows WHERE users actually leave.
T3: Step 1 — Instrument the drop-off. Find the exact step where usage dies.
    For us: 61% of churned users never connected a second data source.
T4: Step 2 — Interview 5 churned users. Not a survey. Calls. Five is enough
    to hear the same reason twice, and that reason is your leak.
T5: Step 3 — Fix ONE leak. Resist the redesign. We changed one onboarding
    screen and one email. That's the whole intervention.
T6: Step 4 — Re-measure at 30 days against the same cohort definition.
    If the number didn't move, your interviews pointed at a symptom.
T7: You might think 5 interviews is too small a sample. For deciding what
    to fix first, it beats a 500-response survey every time.
T8: Recap: instrument → interview 5 → fix one leak → re-measure in 30 days.
    I break down the exact interview script in the full post (link in bio).
```

Note the mechanics: T1 pairs a specific result with a promise, each body tweet leads with the point, T7 pre-empts the obvious objection, T8 recaps and carries the only CTA.

### Worked example — 30-45s video script from the same card

```
[ON SCREEN: "We cut churn 22% without shipping features"]
[VO] We cut churn twenty-two percent last quarter. Zero new features.
[VO] Here's the four-step loop. One: find the exact step where users
     drop off. For us, most churned users never connected a second
     data source. That's the leak.
[VO] Two: call five churned users. Not a survey — calls. You'll hear
     the same reason twice by call three.
[ON SCREEN: "Fix ONE thing"]
[VO] Three: fix one thing. We changed one screen and one email.
[VO] Four: re-measure in thirty days. If the number didn't move,
     you fixed a symptom, not the cause.
[ON SCREEN: "Full breakdown → link in bio"]
[VO] Full breakdown, including the interview script, at the link.
```

Word count: ~120 spoken words ≈ 40-45 seconds at a natural pace.

### Pillar type → typical derivative yield

| Pillar type | Length | Idea cards | Realistic derivatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| How-to blog post | 1,500-2,500 words | 8-12 | 10-14 |
| Webinar / talk | 45-60 min | 10-15 | 12-18 (incl. 3-5 clips) |
| Podcast episode | 30-60 min | 8-12 | 8-14 |
| Case study | 800-1,500 words | 5-8 | 6-10 |
| Opinion/essay post | 800-1,200 words | 4-7 | 5-8 |

If the extraction map yields materially fewer cards than this table suggests, flag the pillar as thin (Process step 1) rather than padding.

### Hook type menu (draft 2-3 per derivative)

1. **Contrarian:** "Everything you've heard about <topic> is optimized for <wrong goal>."
2. **Result-first:** "<Specific number result> in <timeframe>. Here's the mechanism."
3. **Mistake:** "The most expensive mistake in <domain> looks like diligence."
4. **Curiosity gap:** "We changed one sentence in <asset> and <metric> doubled."
5. **Direct address:** "If your <thing> does <symptom>, this is why."

### Thread skeleton (5-9 tweets)

```
T1: Hook — specific promise or contrarian claim. No hashtags, no link.
T2: Stakes — why this matters / cost of getting it wrong.
T3-T6: One point per tweet. Lead each with the point, then 1 line of support.
T7 (optional): Objection + rebuttal ("You might think X. But...").
T8: Recap in one line + soft CTA (follow, or link to pillar).
```

### Carousel skeleton (8 slides)

```
S1: Hook, ≤8 words. S2: The problem, ≤20 words. S3-S6: One point each, ≤25 words,
consistent phrasing pattern. S7: The payoff/result. S8: Recap + CTA + handle.
```

## Output format

Deliver a single document (markdown, or a doc if the user prefers) containing:

1. **Extraction map** — the scored idea-card table.
2. **Derivative plan** — one line per derivative: ID (e.g., P3-LI-1), platform, format, source card, suggested week.
3. **Ready-to-post copy** — every derivative fully written and clearly separated, with 2-3 hook options where relevant, video scripts formatted with [ON SCREEN] and [VO] markers, carousels as slide-by-slide copy.
4. **Tracking sheet** — a table (offer CSV) with columns: `derivative_id, idea_card, platform, format, hook_type, post_date, impressions, engagements, clicks, notes, verdict (winner/retry/retire)`.
5. **Recycling notes** — which pieces are evergreen re-runs and the earliest re-post date for each.

## Quality checklist

Pass/fail every item before delivering:

- [ ] Every derivative traces to a numbered idea card; no orphan content.
- [ ] No two derivatives share more than one identical sentence.
- [ ] Every social piece works with zero knowledge of the pillar (no "as I wrote in my post...").
- [ ] LinkedIn posts: first 210 characters function as a standalone hook.
- [ ] X singles are ≤280 characters; thread tweet 1 promises a specific payoff.
- [ ] Carousel slides: one point each, ≤25 words, slide 1 is ≤8 words.
- [ ] Video scripts are 75-150 words with the hook in the first line, written in spoken register.
- [ ] At most ~30% of derivatives carry a CTA/link; the rest are pure value.
- [ ] Same-idea derivatives on the same platform are ≥14 days apart in the sequence.
- [ ] Each derivative has 2-3 hook options, or a note on why one hook was final.
- [ ] Tracking sheet exists with per-platform primary metrics defined and UTM scheme stated.
- [ ] Weakest idea cards (scoring <2 on either axis) were cut, not padded into content.
- [ ] Voice matches the user's sample posts (spot-check three pieces against the samples).
- [ ] Plan volume matches the user's real posting capacity, not an aspirational one.

## Common mistakes

- **Copy-paste syndication.** Posting the identical text to LinkedIn and X. Each platform's audience, cutoff lengths, and culture differ; identical posts underperform on both and train followers to ignore one channel.
- **Link-first derivatives.** Making every social post a teaser + link to the pillar. Social algorithms suppress outbound links and audiences resent bait; native-value posts with occasional links outperform.
- **Atomizing a thin pillar.** Ten derivatives from a one-idea post produces ten restatements. Volume comes from idea density, not stretching.
- **Chronological extraction.** Turning the pillar's intro into post 1, section 1 into post 2, etc. Extract by idea strength, not document order — the best idea is often buried in section 4.
- **Front-loading the whole batch.** Publishing 12 derivatives in one week exhausts the asset and floods followers. Spread over 4-6 weeks and keep re-run rights for 60-90 days out.
- **Treating clips as scripts.** Ripping a raw 60-second segment from a webinar and calling it a Reel. Recorded conversation lacks a 2-second hook; write a fresh script around the moment instead.
- **One hook per piece.** Shipping the first hook drafted. Hook variants are the cheapest performance lever in this entire workflow; always draft alternatives.
- **No derivative-level tracking.** Measuring only pillar traffic, so the user never learns which ideas or formats win. Without the tracking sheet, repurposing can't compound.
- **Repurposing into dead channels.** Producing Instagram carousels for an account with 40 followers and no posting habit. Concentrate derivatives where the audience already is.
- **Ignoring winners.** A derivative that 5x'd the median is a signal, not a fluke. Failing to spin follow-ups from a proven idea card wastes the single most valuable output of the system.

# LinkedIn Content

Produce LinkedIn posts and carousels engineered for the feed algorithm and human skimming behavior: a hook that survives the "see more" cut, an archetype-driven body formatted for mobile, a comment plan that compounds reach in the first hour, and a profile that converts the attention a good post generates. The deliverable is always ready-to-paste text plus the operational instructions (when to post, how to engage) that determine whether it performs.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Draft, rewrite, or critique a LinkedIn post (text, image, carousel, poll, or document post)
- Turn a win, failure, lesson, or opinion into LinkedIn content
- Outline a carousel/PDF document post slide by slide
- Build a repeatable posting system or a batch of posts for the week
- Set up their profile (headline, banner, About, Featured) as a funnel for post traffic
- Diagnose why their posts get low impressions or engagement

Do NOT use it for:
- Connection requests, DMs, or InMail sequences — use **linkedin-outreach**
- Multi-platform planning, audience strategy, or channel selection — use **social-media-strategy**
- Scheduling and calendar operations across weeks — use **social-content-calendar**
- Adapting a blog post or video into many formats — use **content-repurposing** (then come back here for the LinkedIn-specific rewrite)
- Twitter/X threads — use **twitter-x-content**; do not port posts verbatim between platforms
- LinkedIn paid ads — that is ad platform work, closest sibling is **ad-copywriting**

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these before drafting. Assume the listed default when the user doesn't know or doesn't answer:

1. **Who is posting** — personal profile or company page? Default: personal profile (personal profiles get 3-8x the organic reach of company pages; steer users toward personal unless they insist).
2. **Target reader** — job title, seniority, industry. Default: prospective buyers of the user's product/service. One post targets one reader.
3. **Goal of this post** — reach (followers), trust (nurture), or conversion (leads/DMs). Default: trust. Only ~1 post in 5 should be conversion-oriented.
4. **Raw material** — a story, result with numbers, opinion, or process the user can share. If they have nothing, run the mining questions in Process step 2.
5. **Voice constraints** — banned words, formality level, first-person vs. we. Default: first person, conversational, no corporate jargon. If **brand-messaging** outputs exist, honor them.
6. **Format** — text-only, text + image, carousel/document, or poll. Default: text-only (lowest effort, consistently strong reach); carousel for how-to content with 5+ steps.
7. **Posting cadence and history** — how often they post now and what has worked. Default assumption: new or inconsistent poster; recommend 3 posts/week (Tue/Wed/Thu) before optimizing anything else.

## Process

1. **Pick the archetype before writing a word.** Match the raw material to one of the four archetypes in Frameworks: Story (something happened), Contrarian (common advice is wrong), How-to (repeatable process), Proof (result with numbers). If the material fits none, it is probably an announcement — reframe it as a Story ("what building this taught me") because plain announcements underperform every archetype.

2. **Mine for specifics if the material is thin.** Ask the user: "What surprised you about this?", "What number can you attach to it?", "What did you believe before that you don't now?", "What would you tell someone one step behind you?" A post needs at least one specific detail (a number, a quote, a named mistake) or it will read as generic advice and die in the feed.

3. **Write the hook — spend 40% of total effort here.** The feed truncates posts at roughly 200-220 characters on desktop and ~140 on mobile (about the first 2-3 short lines). Everything before "…see more" must earn the click. Rules:
   - Line 1: under 60 characters, no hashtags, no @-mentions, no emoji spam, no "I'm excited to announce."
   - Create an open loop: a surprising number, a confession, a contradiction, or a bold claim the body must justify.
   - Write 5-10 hook candidates, then pick using the test: "Would a stranger scrolling at a bus stop tap 'see more'?" Present the top 2-3 to the user.
   - Never resolve the hook before the fold. "I fired my best client. Best decision of the year." resolves too early; "I fired my best client last Tuesday." does not.

4. **Draft the body in the archetype's structure** (templates below). Formatting rules that are non-negotiable:
   - 1-2 sentences per paragraph; blank line between every paragraph. LinkedIn strips markdown — no bold, no headers, no bullets other than plain "-" or "→".
   - Sentence length: mostly under 12 words. Read it aloud; anywhere breath runs out, split.
   - Total length: 800-1,300 characters for Story/Contrarian, up to 1,800 for How-to. Hard platform cap is 3,000 characters — treat 2,000 as the practical ceiling; dwell time matters, but walls of text get skipped.
   - No external links in the post body — links cut reach measurably (commonly 25-50% fewer impressions). Put the link in the first comment and say "link in comments" only if the goal is conversion.
   - 0-3 hashtags at the very end, niche over broad (#b2bsaas beats #business). Hashtags are near-zero value in 2025-era LinkedIn; skip them unless the user insists.

5. **Write the close.** End with exactly one of: (a) a question that invites a specific answer ("What's the first metric you check each morning?" — not "Thoughts?"), (b) a soft CTA ("Follow for more teardowns like this"), or (c) a P.S. with the conversion ask. One CTA per post, matched to the goal from Inputs step 3.

6. **If format is carousel/document post, outline it slide by slide.** Spec: PDF upload, 1080×1350 px (4:5 portrait) or 1080×1080 square, 8-12 slides (LinkedIn allows up to 300 pages; completion rate collapses past ~12). Structure: Slide 1 = hook restated as a title (max 8 words, huge type); slides 2-10 = one idea per slide, max 25 words each; second-to-last slide = summary/recap; final slide = CTA + who the user is. The accompanying post text still needs its own hook — write it per steps 3-5 but shorter (400-700 characters).

7. **Deliver the engagement plan with the post.** The algorithm tests every post on a small sample in the first 60-90 minutes; early comments (weighted far above likes) decide distribution. Instruct the user to:
   - Post Tue-Thu, 7:30-9:30am in the target audience's timezone (Mon and Fri are second tier; weekends only for personal/story content).
   - Stay online 60 minutes post-publish and reply to every comment with a substantive reply (10+ words — replies count as new comments and re-trigger distribution).
   - Spend 15 minutes before and after posting leaving 5-10 thoughtful comments (2+ sentences, add a point, never "Great post!") on posts by people the target audience follows. Comments on others' posts are the cheapest follower acquisition on the platform.
   - Never edit the post in the first hour (edits can suppress ongoing distribution) and never delete-and-repost the same day.

8. **Run the profile-as-funnel check** whenever the goal is conversion or the user is starting a posting habit. A post that works sends strangers to the profile; the profile must convert them in under 10 seconds:
   - **Headline** (220 char max): outcome + audience + proof, not a job title. Formula: "I help [audience] get [outcome] — [proof point]". Example: "I help B2B founders book 20+ demos/mo with outbound — built 3 sales teams to $10M ARR".
   - **Banner** (1584×396 px): one sentence value prop + CTA; never leave the default blue.
   - **About**: first 3 lines visible before truncation — treat them as a hook; 2,600 char max; end with one clear CTA and a contact method.
   - **Featured section**: pin exactly one conversion asset (lead magnet, booking link, best case study) plus 1-2 best-performing posts.
   - Creator mode / "Follow" as default button if follower growth is the goal.

9. **Score the draft against the Quality checklist** below, fix failures, then hand over per Output format. If the user is building a multi-week plan, propose an archetype rotation (see Frameworks) and hand the schedule to **social-content-calendar**.

## Frameworks & templates

### The four archetypes and when to use each

| Archetype | Use when | Goal fit | Frequency in rotation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story | Something happened (win, failure, moment) | Trust, reach | 40% |
| Contrarian | Common advice in the niche is wrong | Reach, followers | 20% |
| How-to | A process others would pay to learn | Trust, saves | 25% |
| Proof | A result with real numbers | Conversion | 15% |

### Template 1 — Story post (fill in the blanks)

```
[Moment of tension in under 60 chars — no resolution]

[1-2 lines of context: when, where, stakes]

[What happened — 3-5 short paragraphs, chronological,
 one concrete detail per paragraph]

[The turn: what you realized or what changed]

The lesson:

[One sentence. Earned, not preached.]

[Question to the reader OR soft CTA]
```

Worked example:
```
I lost our biggest client with one email.

March 2024. They were 40% of revenue.

I sent a proposal with a 22% price increase.
No call. No warning. Just a PDF on a Friday.

Monday morning: "We've decided to go another direction."

I spent two weeks blaming the client.
Then I re-read my own email.

No context. No new value named. Just a bigger number.

The lesson:

Price increases are a conversation, not a notification.

What's the most expensive email you've ever sent?
```

### Template 2 — Contrarian take

```
[Common advice] is bad advice.

Everyone tells you to [common advice restated].

Here's what that actually gets you:

- [Bad outcome 1]
- [Bad outcome 2]
- [Bad outcome 3]

What works instead: [your alternative in one line]

[2-4 paragraphs: why it works, with one number or example]

[Steel-man the old advice: "X made sense when…"]

[Question inviting disagreement]
```

### Template 3 — How-to post

```
How I [specific outcome] in [timeframe] ([qualifier: without X / with only Y]):

[1 line of proof you've earned the right to teach this]

Step 1: [Verb-first instruction]
[1-2 lines: the non-obvious detail]

Step 2: ...
Step 3: ...
Step 4: ...
Step 5: ...

The mistake most people make: [the failure mode]

[Save/share CTA: "Save this for your next [situation]."]
```

### Template 4 — Proof/result post

```
[Result with a number, no context]: here's the breakdown.

Starting point: [before state, with number]

What we changed:
1. [Change + why]
2. [Change + why]
3. [Change + why]

What didn't work: [one honest failure — this line builds more
 trust than the wins]

Result after [timeframe]: [after state, with number]

[Conversion CTA in a P.S.: "P.S. I do this for [audience].
 DM me 'BREAKDOWN' if you want the template."]
```

### Carousel outline skeleton (10 slides)

1. Title hook (max 8 words) 2. The problem/stakes 3-8. One step or idea each (max 25 words/slide) 9. Recap of all steps in a list 10. Who you are + one CTA.

### Benchmarks to calibrate against (personal profiles, 1k-20k followers)

- Engagement rate (reactions+comments / impressions): 2-4% is normal, 6%+ is a winner worth repurposing.
- Impressions per follower: 0.3-1.0x on an average post; 3-10x when a post gets picked up.
- Comments are worth roughly 5-10x a reaction to distribution; a post with 20 real comments in hour one almost always outruns one with 200 likes and none.
- Follower growth: 3 posts/week + 30 daily comments typically yields 300-1,000 net new followers/month in a defined niche.

## Output format

Deliver in this order, in plain text (never markdown formatting inside the post itself, since LinkedIn strips it):

1. **Post copy** in a code block, formatted exactly as it should be pasted — line breaks included, hook as line 1, hashtags (if any) last.
2. **Alternate hooks** — 2 additional hook options with one line on the tradeoff of each.
3. **First comment** — pre-written, containing the external link (if any) or an expansion point that seeds discussion.
4. **Engagement plan** — post day/time, the 60-minute reply commitment, and 3-5 specific accounts or post types to comment on that day.
5. **Format spec** (carousels only) — slide-by-slide text, dimensions, and slide count.
6. **Profile fixes** (when funnel check was run) — headline rewrite, About first-3-lines rewrite, Featured recommendation, each ready to paste.
7. **Measurement note** — what to check at 24h (impressions, engagement rate vs. the 2-4% benchmark, profile views) and the single change to test next post.

## Quality checklist

Pass every item before delivering:

- [ ] Line 1 is under 60 characters and creates an open loop that is NOT resolved before ~200 characters
- [ ] Zero markdown syntax (no **, ##, or []()) inside the post copy
- [ ] No paragraph exceeds 2 sentences; blank line between all paragraphs
- [ ] Post contains at least one specific number, named detail, or direct quote
- [ ] Total length is 800-2,000 characters (400-700 for carousel captions)
- [ ] No external URL in the post body; link (if needed) lives in the pre-written first comment
- [ ] Exactly one CTA, and it matches the stated goal (reach/trust/conversion)
- [ ] Closing question can be answered in one sentence by the target reader (not "Thoughts?")
- [ ] 0-3 hashtags, all niche-specific, placed at the end only
- [ ] Post matches one declared archetype; an announcement has been reframed as Story or Proof
- [ ] Carousel (if any) is 8-12 slides, ≤25 words per content slide, CTA on the final slide
- [ ] Engagement plan specifies day, time window, and the 60-minute reply commitment
- [ ] Voice matches the user's constraints — read one existing post by the user if available and compare
- [ ] Nothing in the copy could be pasted under a competitor's name unchanged (the "logo swap" test for genericness)

## Common mistakes

- **Burying the hook under a wind-up.** "Last week I had an interesting conversation that got me thinking about..." — the post is dead by word six. Start at the moment of tension.
- **Resolving the open loop before "see more."** If the first 200 characters contain both the setup and the payoff, there is no reason to expand the post, and expand-clicks are a ranking signal.
- **Broetry without substance.** One-line paragraphs formatting a post that says nothing reads as manipulation. Formatting amplifies a specific idea; it cannot replace one.
- **Posting the link in the body "just this once."** The reach penalty is real and the user will conclude "LinkedIn doesn't work" from a self-inflicted wound.
- **"Thoughts?" as a CTA.** Zero-effort questions get zero-effort comments, which are weighted like zero-effort comments. Ask something with a one-sentence, opinionated answer.
- **Treating the company page as the main channel.** Company pages get a fraction of personal-profile reach; the fix is executives posting personally, with the page resharing 24h later.
- **Announcement posts.** "We're thrilled to share..." is the lowest-performing genre on the platform. Every launch is a Story (why we built it) or Proof (what it did for the first user) in disguise — see **product-launch** for the broader launch plan.
- **Engagement-pod thinking.** Tagging 15 people, "comment 'GUIDE' to get the PDF" bait on every post, or reciprocal-like groups produce impressions from the wrong audience and train the algorithm to show posts to non-buyers.
- **Editing or deleting in hour one.** Both can reset or suppress distribution mid-test. Proofread before publishing; live with a typo until the next day.
- **Posting daily with nothing to say.** Three specific posts beat seven generic ones; frequency only compounds when the engagement-rate benchmark (2-4%) is being met or beaten.
- **Ignoring comments after publishing.** Half the performance of a post happens in the replies. An unanswered comment section signals — to readers and the algorithm — that the author has left the room.

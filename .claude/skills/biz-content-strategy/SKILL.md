# Content Strategy

Produce a decision-ready content strategy document that tells a team exactly what to publish, for whom, in what formats, on what cadence, who does each step, how every piece gets distributed after publish, and when existing content gets refreshed or killed. The output is a strategy, not the content itself — it should let any competent writer or agency execute for the next two quarters without asking "what should I write about?"

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user needs the upstream system: pillars, clusters, funnel mapping, format mix, editorial standards, workflow, distribution rules, or a content audit.

Do NOT use it for adjacent jobs — route instead:
- Writing an individual article → **blog-writing**. Writing a single-page brief for one article → **seo-content-briefs**.
- Finding and prioritizing keywords → **keyword-research** (this skill consumes its output; if none exists, make reasonable keyword assumptions and flag them).
- A dated posting schedule for social channels → **social-content-calendar**; channel selection and social cadence → **social-media-strategy**.
- Turning existing pieces into other formats → **content-repurposing** (this skill decides THAT repurposing happens; that skill does it).
- Overall marketing budget/channel allocation → **marketing-plan**. Voice, positioning, and messaging hierarchy → **brand-messaging**. Persona research from scratch → **customer-personas**.
- Newsletter program design → **email-newsletters**. Video/YouTube programs → **youtube-scripts** or **short-form-video**.

If the user asks for a strategy AND a first piece, deliver the strategy first, then hand off to the writing skill.

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these before starting. Where the user shrugs, use the stated default and mark it as an assumption in the deliverable.

1. **Business and offer**: what they sell, price point, sales motion (self-serve, sales-led, ecommerce, local service). Default: infer from any URL or description given.
2. **Primary goal, ranked**: organic traffic, leads/demos, signups, sales, brand authority. Default: leads for B2B, revenue for ecommerce, signups for PLG SaaS.
3. **Personas** (1-3 max): job title or life situation, top 3 pains, where they consume content. Default: draft two provisional personas from the offer and label them "provisional — validate with customer-personas."
4. **Existing content inventory**: URL list or sitemap, analytics access (GA4, Search Console). Default: assume a small legacy blog of 10-30 posts with no cluster structure.
5. **Team capacity**: who can write/edit/design, hours per week, budget for freelancers. Default: one part-time writer (10 hrs/wk) — this caps cadence at 2-3 substantial pieces/month.
6. **Channels already working**: any channel with proven traction. Default: SEO/blog as spine, one social channel and email as distribution.
7. **Sales cycle length and key objections**: needed for MOFU/BOFU topics. Default: pull objections from competitor comparison pages and review sites.
8. **Constraints**: regulated industry claims, brand voice docs, review/approval requirements. Default: none.

## Process

1. **Audit what exists (skip only for true zero-content startups).** Build an inventory: URL, title, target query, publish/update date, monthly organic sessions, conversions or assisted conversions, backlinks. Score each piece with the Keep/Refresh/Consolidate/Kill rubric (below). Deliverable artifact: a table with a verdict per URL. Rule of thumb: a mature blog typically ends up ~40% keep, 30% refresh, 20% consolidate, 10% kill.

2. **Define 3-5 content pillars.** A pillar = a topic territory where the business has (a) genuine authority, (b) commercial relevance (buyers of the offer care), and (c) sustained demand (enough subtopics for 12+ months). Fewer than 3 pillars starves variety; more than 5 diffuses authority. For each pillar write one sentence: "We publish about X so that PERSONA achieves Y, which leads them toward OFFER." If a proposed pillar can't complete that sentence, cut it.

3. **Build topic clusters inside each pillar.** Each cluster = 1 pillar page (comprehensive hub, 2,500-4,000 words, targets a head term) + 5-10 cluster pieces (1,200-2,000 words each, target long-tail queries) all internally linked to the hub and to each other where relevant. Source topics from keyword-research output if available; otherwise from sales call questions, support tickets, community threads, and competitor content gaps. Aim for 2-4 clusters per pillar for the first two quarters.

4. **Map every topic to funnel stage x persona.** Tag each planned piece TOFU (problem-aware: "what is / why / how to"), MOFU (solution-aware: comparisons, frameworks, "best X for Y", templates), or BOFU (product-aware: vs. pages, pricing explainers, case studies, implementation guides). Default mix for a new program: **50% TOFU / 30% MOFU / 20% BOFU**. Shift toward 30/40/30 when the goal is pipeline over traffic or the sales cycle is under 30 days. Every persona must have at least one piece per stage in the first quarter — a persona with only TOFU coverage is a leak. Flag any topic that serves no persona and cut it.

5. **Decide the format mix.** Choose formats by where each persona actually consumes and by team capacity, not by trend. Baseline for a lean team: written long-form as the spine (SEO compounds), plus ONE derivative format (e.g., LinkedIn posts via content-repurposing) and ONE owned channel (email digest). Add video/podcast only when written cadence is stable for 8+ weeks. State the mix as percentages of production hours, e.g., 60% long-form written / 25% social derivatives / 15% email.

6. **Set editorial standards.** Write a one-page standard covering: voice (3 adjectives + 2 "we never" rules), point-of-view requirement (every piece must contain at least one claim a competitor wouldn't make), sourcing (first-party data or named expert quote preferred; no stats without a source and year), structure (H2 every 250-350 words, front-load the answer in the first 100 words, one CTA per piece matched to funnel stage), and readability (grade 6-9 for consumer, 8-11 for technical B2B). Reference brand-messaging output if it exists rather than reinventing voice.

7. **Design the production workflow with named roles.** Minimum viable pipeline with SLAs: Brief approved (Strategist, day 0) → Draft (Writer, +5 business days) → Edit for substance then line edit (Editor, +2 days) → SME/legal review if required (+2 days) → Visuals (Designer, +2 days, parallel with review) → SEO/on-page check (+1 day) → Publish → Distribution checklist executed within 48 hours (Owner: whoever published). Assign one accountable name per stage, even if one person wears three hats — "the team" owns nothing. Cap work-in-progress at 2x monthly cadence to prevent a half-finished backlog.

8. **Write the distribution plan — a piece is not done when published.** Budget roughly as much effort on distribution as on creation (a 50/50 split of hours is a defensible default; never below 70/30 creation/distribution). For each piece, the publisher executes the Distribution Checklist (template below) within 48 hours and logs completion. Define 2-3 "hero pieces" per quarter that additionally get paid boosting, outreach, or partnership pushes.

9. **Define the audit and refresh cycle.** Schedule: quarterly mini-audit (top 20 pages by traffic + all BOFU pages), annual full audit. Refresh triggers: organic clicks down >20% over two consecutive quarters, ranking slipped from page 1 to page 2, facts/screenshots/pricing outdated, or publish date >18 months on a competitive query. A refresh means substantive update — new data, new sections, updated internal links, updated date — not a cosmetic re-date. Budget 20-30% of ongoing content capacity for refreshes; on a mature library (>100 posts) refreshes routinely outperform net-new on effort-to-traffic.

10. **Set measurement and cadence commitments.** Pick 1 north-star metric per funnel stage (e.g., TOFU: organic sessions; MOFU: content-sourced email signups; BOFU: content-assisted demos) with a 2-quarter target. Commit to a cadence the stated capacity can sustain for 6 months — 2 good pieces/month beats 8 pieces for 6 weeks followed by silence. Expect SEO-driven pieces to take 3-6 months to rank; set expectations in the document so the program survives month 2.

11. **Assemble the deliverable** per Output format, list every assumption made, and end with a "first 30 days" action list of no more than 8 items.

## Frameworks & templates

### Pillar and cluster map (fill-in template)

```
PILLAR 1: [Topic territory]
  Why us: [1 sentence of earned authority]
  Persona(s): [names]  |  Business link: [which offer this feeds]
  CLUSTER 1A: [head term, est. volume]
    Hub page: "[Title]" — TOFU/MOFU — 2,500-4,000 words
    Cluster pieces (5-10):
      1. "[Title]" — [long-tail query] — [funnel stage] — [persona] — [format]
      2. ...
  CLUSTER 1B: ...
PILLAR 2: ...
```

### Worked example (B2B SaaS: field-service scheduling software, persona "Operations Manager Olivia")

```
PILLAR: Field workforce scheduling
  Why us: our product's core job; we have usage data from 400 customers.
  CLUSTER: technician scheduling (head term ~1,900/mo)
    Hub: "Technician Scheduling: The Complete Guide" — TOFU — 3,200 words
    1. "How to build a technician schedule in Excel (free template)" — TOFU — Olivia — article + template
    2. "Scheduling apps vs. spreadsheets: real cost comparison" — MOFU — Olivia — article
    3. "7 dispatching mistakes that burn out field techs" — TOFU — Olivia — article + LinkedIn carousel
    4. "[Product] vs. ServiceTitan for scheduling" — BOFU — Olivia — comparison page
    5. "How Acme HVAC cut overtime 22% with automated scheduling" — BOFU — Olivia — case study (via case-studies skill)
```

### Keep/Refresh/Consolidate/Kill audit rubric

Score each URL, then apply the verdict rules:
- **Keep**: page 1 ranking OR converts OR has 5+ referring domains, and content is accurate. No action beyond quarterly check.
- **Refresh**: ranked positions 4-20 or traffic decayed >20% over 2 quarters, topic still commercially relevant. Action: substantive update within the next cycle.
- **Consolidate**: 2+ URLs targeting the same query and splitting rankings (check Search Console for shared queries). Action: merge into the strongest URL, 301 the rest.
- **Kill**: <10 organic clicks in 12 months, no backlinks, no conversions, off-strategy topic. Action: delete + 301 to the nearest relevant page, or leave unindexed if it serves support/sales.

### Distribution checklist (per piece, execute within 48 hours of publish)

```
[ ] 3-5 internal links added FROM existing relevant pages to the new piece
[ ] Hub page updated to link to new cluster piece
[ ] Email: featured in next newsletter send (subject line drafted)
[ ] Social: 2-3 native posts per active channel, staggered over 2 weeks
    (not just a link drop — pull the sharpest claim or stat as the hook)
[ ] Repurposing ticket created if piece qualifies (see content-repurposing)
[ ] Sales/CS notified with a 1-line "use this when..." note (MOFU/BOFU only)
[ ] Communities: shared where the team genuinely participates (max 2)
[ ] Hero pieces only: outreach list of 10-20 people quoted/cited, paid boost
[ ] Logged: piece URL + checklist completion date in the content tracker
```

Reading the map: every cell needs at least one planned piece in quarter one.
An empty BOFU row for the economic buyer is the most expensive gap to leave.

### Format decision table

Pick formats top-down until stated capacity is exhausted; never start a
format you cannot sustain at its minimum cadence for 8 weeks.

| Format | Best for | Effort per piece | Minimum viable cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-form article (1,200-2,000 wds) | SEO spine, TOFU/MOFU | 6-10 hrs | 2/month |
| Pillar/hub page (2,500-4,000 wds) | Cluster anchor | 15-25 hrs | 1/quarter per cluster |
| Case study | BOFU, sales enablement | 8-12 hrs incl. interview | 1/quarter |
| Comparison / vs. page | BOFU capture | 8-12 hrs | as clusters demand |
| Email digest | Owned distribution | 2-3 hrs | 2/month (see email-newsletters) |
| Social derivative posts | Reach, top-of-funnel | 1-2 hrs per source piece | 3/week per channel |
| Template / calculator | MOFU lead capture | 10-20 hrs | 1-2/quarter |
| Video / podcast episode | Authority, retention | 10-20 hrs | 2/month — only after written spine is stable |

### Editorial standards one-pager (skeleton)

```
Voice: [adj], [adj], [adj]. We never: [rule 1]; [rule 2].
Every piece must: answer the query in the first 100 words; contain >=1
  original claim, example, or data point; have exactly 1 stage-matched CTA.
Sourcing: stats need source + year; prefer first-party data or named quotes.
Structure: H2 every 250-350 words; scannable lists; alt text on images.
Readability target: grade [8-11]. Banned: filler intros, "in conclusion",
  unverifiable superlatives, competitor disparagement without evidence.
```

## Output format

Deliver a single markdown document titled "[Company] Content Strategy — [Quarter/Year]" with these sections, in order:

1. **Executive summary** — goal, north-star metrics with 2-quarter targets, cadence commitment, one-paragraph strategy statement. Max half a page.
2. **Personas and funnel map** — table: persona x TOFU/MOFU/BOFU with the content job at each intersection.
3. **Pillars and clusters** — the filled pillar/cluster map, every piece tagged (stage, persona, format, target query, priority P1/P2/P3).
4. **Format mix** — percentages of production hours by format, with one-line justification each.
5. **Editorial standards** — the filled one-pager.
6. **Production workflow** — pipeline stages, named owner per stage, SLA per stage, WIP cap.
7. **Distribution plan** — the checklist plus hero-piece criteria and quarterly hero list.
8. **Audit results and refresh calendar** — verdict table for existing content (if any), refresh triggers, quarterly audit dates.
9. **Assumptions and open questions** — everything defaulted or guessed.
10. **First 30 days** — max 8 concrete actions with owners.

If the user asks for only one component (e.g., just an audit), deliver that section at full depth plus the executive summary.

## Quality checklist

Verify every item before delivering; fix any failure.

1. There are 3-5 pillars, each with the "We publish about X so PERSONA achieves Y toward OFFER" sentence completed.
2. Every planned piece has all five tags: cluster, funnel stage, persona, format, target query.
3. Each persona has at least one piece at each funnel stage in the first quarter's plan.
4. The TOFU/MOFU/BOFU ratio is stated as percentages and justified against the user's goal and sales cycle.
5. Each cluster has exactly one hub page and 5-10 cluster pieces, with internal linking rules stated.
6. Cadence is arithmetically consistent with stated team capacity (pieces/month x hours/piece <= available hours, including distribution time).
7. Every production stage has a named owner and an SLA in days.
8. The distribution checklist exists, has a 48-hour execution window, and names an owner.
9. Refresh triggers use numeric thresholds (e.g., >20% two-quarter decay), not "when it feels stale."
10. The audit table (if content exists) gives every URL one of exactly four verdicts: Keep/Refresh/Consolidate/Kill.
11. One north-star metric per funnel stage is defined with a numeric 2-quarter target.
12. All assumptions and defaults are listed in section 9, not silently embedded.
13. No piece in the plan targets a query already owned by a Keep-verdict existing page (no self-cannibalization).
14. The "first 30 days" list has <=8 items, each with an owner and a verb.

## Common mistakes

- **Pillars defined by what the team likes writing, not what buyers search.** "Our company culture" is not a pillar unless you sell recruiting. Every pillar must pass the commercial-relevance sentence test.
- **All-TOFU plans.** Traffic grows, pipeline doesn't. If the user's goal is leads and the plan has <20% MOFU+BOFU, rebalance before delivering.
- **Cadence set by ambition instead of capacity.** A plan that requires 16 pieces/month from one part-time writer will die by week 6. Halve the cadence and add distribution instead.
- **Treating publish as the finish line.** No distribution checklist means each piece gets one social link-drop and dies. This is the single most common failure; the 48-hour checklist is non-negotiable.
- **Ten orphaned posts instead of one cluster.** Pieces without a hub and internal links compete with each other and never build topical authority. Cluster first, then write.
- **Refreshing by changing the date.** Google and readers both notice. A refresh without new substance (data, sections, examples) is a Kill candidate wearing a Keep badge.
- **Auditing without a verdict.** An inventory spreadsheet that ends in "interesting" is wasted work. Every row gets Keep/Refresh/Consolidate/Kill and an action date.
- **Launching three formats simultaneously.** Blog + podcast + YouTube from a standing start means three mediocre channels. One spine format for 8+ weeks, then add derivatives.
- **Copying a competitor's cluster map wholesale.** Their strategy encodes their authority and their customer base. Steal gap topics they've missed, not their whole architecture (use competitor-analysis for the gap work).
- **No point-of-view requirement.** Content that any competitor could have published earns no links, no shares, and increasingly no rankings. The "one claim a competitor wouldn't make" rule filters this at the brief stage.
- **Ignoring sales and support as topic sources.** The best MOFU/BOFU topics are verbatim questions from calls and tickets, free and pre-validated; keyword tools only show what people already know to search.

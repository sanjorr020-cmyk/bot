# Blog Post Writing

Produce a publish-ready SEO blog post, not a plausible-looking wall of text. The job runs from angle selection (what the post argues and why it beats what already ranks) through research, outlining, drafting, editing, and a final on-page package. Every post must satisfy two readers at once: a human skimming on a phone, and a search engine matching intent. When the two conflict, the human wins — posts that get read, linked, and finished are the posts that rank.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants a blog post or article written, outlined, expanded from notes, or edited for publication — especially when it needs to rank for a target keyword or support organic traffic goals.

Do NOT use this skill for:
- **Finding which keywords to target** — that is `keyword-research`. This skill assumes a target topic or keyword exists (or picks one pragmatically from the user's suggestion).
- **Producing a brief for another writer** — that is `seo-content-briefs`. This skill writes the post itself.
- **Site-wide or existing-page optimization audits** — that is `on-page-seo` (or `technical-seo` for crawl/speed issues). This skill applies on-page best practice to one new post.
- **Landing pages, homepages, or sales pages** — that is `website-copywriting` or `landing-page-cro`. Blog posts inform first; those pages convert first.
- **Deciding what to publish over a quarter** — that is `content-strategy` and `social-content-calendar`.
- **Case studies, newsletters, or press releases** — use `case-studies`, `email-newsletters`, or `press-releases`.
- **Spinning a finished post into other formats** — that is `content-repurposing` (offer it as a follow-up after delivery).

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these up front. If the user does not know, apply the default and say so — do not stall the project on missing inputs.

| Input | Ask | Default if unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Primary keyword/topic | "What keyword or topic should this target?" | Derive a specific long-tail phrasing from their topic (e.g., "email marketing" → "email marketing for small ecommerce brands") |
| Audience | "Who reads this — role, sophistication level?" | Practitioner with beginner-to-intermediate knowledge; assume they searched the keyword themselves |
| Search intent | "Is the searcher trying to learn, compare, or buy?" | Infer from keyword modifiers: "how/what/why" = informational; "best/vs/review" = commercial; "buy/pricing/discount" = transactional |
| Business goal & CTA | "What should a reader do after finishing?" | Soft CTA: newsletter signup or related-content click; never a hard sales pitch on informational intent |
| Word count | "Any length target?" | Match the SERP median (Step 2), typically 1,200–2,200 words; never pad to hit a number |
| Voice & style | "Formal or conversational? First or second person?" | Conversational-expert, second person, contractions allowed, no slang |
| E-E-A-T raw material | "Do you have firsthand data, customer stories, screenshots, or an author with credentials?" | Flag the gap and write placeholders the user must fill — see Process Step 4 |
| Internal link targets | "Which existing pages should this link to?" | Ask for a sitemap or 3–5 URLs; if none, mark internal-link slots in the draft |

## Process

1. **Confirm the assignment in one sentence.** Restate: "A ~1,800-word informational post targeting '[keyword]' for [audience], with the goal of [CTA]." Get a yes before proceeding. This one sentence prevents 90% of rewrites.

2. **Analyze the SERP.** If web search is available, search the exact primary keyword and inspect the top 10 organic results. Record: (a) dominant intent, (b) dominant format — listicle, how-to, comparison, definition/guide, opinion, (c) median word count of the top 5, (d) what every result covers (table stakes), (e) what none of them cover well (the gap), (f) SERP features present — featured snippet, People Also Ask, video pack. If no web access, ask the user to paste the top 5 titles and any featured snippet, or proceed on stated assumptions and label them.
   - **Decision point:** Match the dominant format ~80% of the time. Only break format when the user has a genuinely superior asset (original data, contrarian expertise) — a how-to guide will not outrank eight listicles on "best CRM software" no matter how good it is.

3. **Select the angle.** Do not write "the same post but longer." Pick exactly one differentiation lever and state it in one sentence before outlining:
   - **Specificity:** narrow the audience ("...for solo consultants") or the scenario ("...when you have no email list").
   - **Recency:** the ranking posts cite stale data or predate a major change.
   - **Firsthand proof:** original data, a real experiment, screenshots the competitors lack.
   - **Contrarian take:** the SERP consensus is wrong or incomplete, and you can prove it.
   - **Superior format:** a decision framework, calculator logic, or comparison table where competitors offer prose.

4. **Research and collect E-E-A-T ammunition.** Build a working list before drafting: 3–6 statistics with named sources and years (nothing older than 3 years unless historical), 1–3 concrete examples or mini-cases, and at least one element only this author could produce — a firsthand result, an internal data point, a quoted opinion with reasoning, a mistake they made and fixed. If the user supplied none, insert clearly-marked bracketed slots like `[AUTHOR: insert your result/screenshot here — the post is measurably weaker without it]` and tell the user these are blocking.

5. **Outline and get approval.** Use the Outline Template (below). Rules: H2s every 250–350 words of planned content; each H2 must be descriptive enough to work as a standalone answer (front-load the keyword-relevant noun, not a pun); cover every table-stakes subtopic from Step 2 plus the gap; order sections by the inverted pyramid — most-searched-for answer first, background later. Show the outline to the user and get sign-off before drafting. This is the cheapest point to change direction.

6. **Draft the post.**
   - **Intro (≤100 words, 3–5 sentences):** use the APP or PAS formula (below). The reader must know within two sentences that this page answers their query. Never open with a definition of the obvious, a rhetorical question, or throat-clearing like "In this article, we will explore...".
   - **Answer-first block:** for informational keywords, place a 40–55 word direct answer to the core query immediately after the intro or under the first H2 — this is the featured-snippet target. Bold nothing inside it; keep it one paragraph or one tight list.
   - **Body:** paragraphs of 1–3 sentences (max ~50 words); a subheading, list, table, image slot, or bolded key phrase at least every 150–200 words; bulleted lists for any series of 3+ items; tables for any comparison of 2+ options across 2+ attributes; second person throughout; one idea per paragraph.
   - **E-E-A-T weaving:** distribute the Step 4 ammunition — at least one firsthand element in the first half, cite every statistic inline ("(Source, 2025)" or linked), state opinions as opinions with reasoning ("I recommend X because..."), and include an author-relevant credential mention where natural.
   - **Keyword placement:** primary keyword in the H1, within the first 100 words, in at least one H2, and 2–4 more times naturally across the body. Use variants and related terms elsewhere. If a sentence reads awkwardly because of the keyword, rewrite the sentence — never publish stilted phrasing.
   - **Conclusion (75–150 words):** use the Conclusion Formula (below). No "In conclusion" header — title it with a payoff, e.g., "Start with one campaign this week."

7. **Ruthless editing pass.** Do this as a separate step, never merged into drafting. Targets:
   - Cut 10–20% of the draft's word count. If nothing feels cuttable, cut adverbs, hedges ("very", "really", "quite", "in order to", "it's important to note"), and any paragraph that restates a previous one.
   - Delete or rewrite any sentence that could appear verbatim in a competitor's post on the same keyword — generic filler is the enemy of both rankings and readers.
   - Read the intro last: does it still match what the post actually delivers? Fix the intro, not the post.
   - Verify every claim has a source or is marked as opinion/experience.
   - Check reading level: aim for Flesch Reading Ease ≥ 60 (roughly grade 7–9) for general audiences; technical audiences may run lower, but no sentence over 30 words survives without a defense.

8. **Build the on-page package.** Deliver alongside the post: title tag (≤60 characters, keyword near the front, a reason to click), meta description (150–160 characters, includes keyword, ends with an implicit or explicit benefit), URL slug (3–5 words, keyword-based, no dates or stop words), 3–8 internal links with descriptive anchor text (not "click here"), 2–5 external links to authoritative non-competing sources, image alt-text for every image slot, and a suggested schema type (Article; FAQPage only if the post has a genuine FAQ section; HowTo if step-based).

9. **Deliver and hand off.** Present the deliverable (Output format below), list any bracketed author-input slots still open, and offer next steps: `content-repurposing` to spin the post into social/email assets, `link-building` if the post is a linkable asset, `on-page-seo` for a 90-day post-publish performance check.

## Frameworks & templates

```

Intro: hook = ___ / promise = ___ / credibility line = ___
[Snippet block: 40–55 word direct answer to "___"]

H2: [Most-searched sub-question, answered directly]
  - key points: ___
  - proof element: [stat / example / firsthand]
H2: [Table-stakes subtopic from SERP]
  - H3: ___
  - H3: ___
H2: [The gap — what competitors miss]
  - proof element: ___
H2: [Objection or common mistake section]
Conclusion H2 (payoff-titled): key takeaway + single CTA: ___

Internal links: 1. ___ 2. ___ 3. ___
External sources: 1. ___ 2. ___
```

### Intro formulas

**APP (Agree–Promise–Preview)** — default for informational posts:
1. *Agree:* name the reader's situation so they feel seen (1 sentence).
2. *Promise:* state exactly what they'll get from this post (1 sentence).
3. *Preview:* one credibility marker or a hint of the method (1–2 sentences).

**PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solve)** — better when the pain is acute:
1. *Problem:* state it plainly. 2. *Agitate:* the cost of not solving it, made concrete. 3. *Solve:* this post is the fix.

**Worked example (APP, keyword "how to write a cold email", 62 words):**
> Most cold emails get deleted in under three seconds — not because the product is bad, but because the first line reads like every other pitch in the inbox. This guide walks through the exact five-part structure we used to book 41 meetings from 500 sends last quarter, with the templates and subject lines included.

### Conclusion Formula

1. **Compress:** the single most important takeaway, restated fresh — not a bullet recap of every H2 (1–2 sentences).
2. **Lower the bar:** the smallest first action the reader can take today (1 sentence).
3. **One CTA:** exactly one — subscribe, download, read the linked next post, or try the product. Two CTAs = zero CTAs.

**Worked example (52 words):**
> A cold email lives or dies on relevance, not cleverness — everything else in this guide is a multiplier on that. Pick ten prospects, write one genuinely specific first line for each, and send them today. If you want the full sequence that follows the opener, grab the follow-up templates here.

### Post-type benchmarks

| Post type | Typical length | Structure signature | Best-fit intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definitive how-to | 1,500–2,500 words | Numbered steps as H2s, one outcome | Informational ("how to") |
| Listicle | 1,200–3,000 words | Numbered items, parallel H2 phrasing, verdict per item | Commercial ("best", "tools") |
| Comparison / vs. | 1,000–2,000 words | Comparison table high on page, verdict by use case | Commercial ("X vs Y") |
| Concept guide | 1,200–2,000 words | Definition block first, then depth | Informational ("what is") |
| Opinion / thought leadership | 800–1,500 words | Thesis in intro, evidence sections, no snippet block needed | None (distribution-driven, not SEO-driven) |
| Original research / data post | 1,500–3,000 words | Key findings up top, methodology at bottom | Linkable asset (pairs with `link-building`) |

### Title tag formula bank

Pick one; keep ≤60 characters; keyword in the first half:

- **[Number] + [Adjective] + [Keyword] + [Promise]:** "11 Proven Cold Email Templates That Book Meetings"
- **How to [Keyword] ([Differentiator]):** "How to Write a Blog Post (With a 15-Point Checklist)"
- **[Keyword]: [Specific outcome or scope]:** "Email Segmentation: A Setup Guide for Stores Under 10k Subscribers"
- **[Keyword] in [Year]: What Actually Works:** use only when Step 2 shows dated competitors.
- **We [Did the thing]. Here's [Result]:** "We Tested 7 CRMs for 90 Days. Here's What Won" — reserve for genuine firsthand posts.

### Worked example: SERP gap analysis (keyword "how to price freelance work")

> Top 10: eight how-to guides, one calculator page, one forum thread. Median length of top 5: ~1,900 words. Table stakes: hourly vs. project pricing, cost-plus math, raising rates. Gap: every post gives formulas but none shows a real negotiation, and all examples use round hypothetical numbers. Featured snippet: a 4-step list from position 3. **Angle chosen:** format-match (how-to guide, ~1,900 words), differentiate on firsthand proof — walk through the author's actual pricing evolution with real numbers, and target the snippet with a tighter 4-step answer block.

### Skimmability rules (apply during drafting and editing)

- One idea per paragraph; 1–3 sentences per paragraph.
- A visual break (H2/H3, list, table, image, blockquote, bold phrase) every 150–200 words.
- H2s must make sense read in isolation — a skimmer reading only headings should get the post's full argument.
- Front-load every section: conclusion first, explanation second (inverted pyramid at the section level too).

### Edit-pass search-and-destroy list

Run these as literal find operations on the draft, then fix:

| Find | Fix |
|---|---|
| "very", "really", "quite", "just", "actually" | Delete; strengthen the verb or adjective instead |
| "in order to" | "to" |
| "it's important to note that", "keep in mind that" | Delete the phrase; the sentence survives alone |
| "there is/are ... that" | Rewrite with the real subject doing the action |
| Sentences over 30 words | Split, or cut the clause that adds least |
| Passive voice on action steps ("the email should be sent") | Active second person ("send the email") |
| "In conclusion", "To sum up", "Without further ado" | Delete; the structure already signals it |
| Two consecutive paragraphs making the same point | Keep the sharper one |
| A claim with no number, source, or example attached | Add one, or downgrade the claim honestly |

## Output format

Deliver a single markdown document containing, in order:

1. **Metadata block:** title tag (with character count), meta description (with character count), URL slug, primary keyword, target intent, suggested schema type, word count.
2. **The full post:** H1, intro, snippet-target answer block, body with H2/H3 structure, payoff-titled conclusion with CTA. Image slots marked as `[IMAGE: description — alt text: "..."]`. Internal/external links inline with real anchor text (or `[LINK: suggested target]` where the user must supply a URL).
3. **Author-input list:** every remaining bracketed slot the user must fill, ranked by how much each one strengthens E-E-A-T.
4. **Pre-publish checklist:** the Quality checklist below, with each item marked pass/fail for this specific draft.

## Quality checklist

Verify each item as strict pass/fail before delivering:

1. Post format matches the dominant SERP format, or the deviation is justified in writing.
2. The angle is stated in one sentence and the post visibly delivers on it.
3. Intro is ≤100 words and the reader knows the post answers their query by sentence two.
4. A 40–55 word direct answer to the core query appears within the first 300 words (informational intent).
5. Primary keyword appears in H1, first 100 words, at least one H2, title tag, and URL slug — and no sentence reads stilted because of it.
6. No paragraph exceeds 3 sentences; no stretch of 200+ words lacks a visual break.
7. Reading only the H2s conveys the post's complete argument.
8. Every statistic has a named, dated source; every strong claim is either sourced or framed as experience/opinion.
9. At least one element exists that no competitor could copy (firsthand data, original example, tested result) — or it is flagged as a blocking author-input slot.
10. The editing pass cut at least 10% from the first draft.
11. Exactly one CTA in the conclusion, matched to the search intent (no hard sell on informational posts).
12. Title tag ≤60 characters; meta description 150–160 characters; both include the keyword and a reason to click.
13. 3–8 internal links and 2–5 authoritative external links, all with descriptive anchor text.
14. Every image slot has alt text that describes the image and, where natural, supports the keyword.
15. Zero filler phrases survive: "in today's world", "it's important to note", "delve", "in conclusion", "without further ado".

## Common mistakes

- **Writing before reading the SERP.** The #1 cause of posts that never rank: a beautifully written how-to competing against ten listicles. Format-match first, differentiate second.
- **Burying the answer.** Putting the actual answer 800 words deep behind "What is X?" and "Why X matters" sections. Searchers bounce, dwell time craters, rankings follow. Answer first; background for those who scroll.
- **Padding to a word count.** "Top posts are 2,400 words, so write 2,400 words" produces bloat. Length is an output of covering the topic, not an input. A 1,300-word post that fully answers the query beats a padded 2,500-word one.
- **Clever headings.** "The Plot Thickens" tells a skimmer and a search engine nothing. Every H2 should be the answer to a question someone actually types.
- **Claimed expertise instead of shown expertise.** "As experts in the field..." is worthless. One screenshot of real results outweighs three paragraphs of credentials.
- **The interchangeable draft.** If swapping your brand name for a competitor's changes nothing, the post adds nothing to the index and Google has no reason to rank it. This is the specific failure mode of unedited AI drafting — the ruthless edit (Step 7) exists to kill it.
- **Exact-match keyword stuffing.** Repeating "best project management software for freelancers" eight times verbatim reads as spam to readers and engines alike. 3–6 natural uses plus variants is plenty.
- **Two-audience intros.** Trying to serve total beginners and advanced practitioners in one post. Pick one (Step 1); link out to the other.
- **A recap conclusion with no next step.** Summarizing every section and ending. The conclusion's job is momentum: one takeaway, one small action, one CTA.
- **Publishing an orphan.** No internal links in or out means no crawl paths and no authority flow. Wire the post into the site before it goes live — 3 links minimum.
- **FAQ sections stuffed for schema.** Five questions nobody asks, added only for FAQPage markup, dilute the post. Include an FAQ only when People Also Ask (Step 2) shows real adjacent questions the body doesn't cover.
- **Skipping the separate edit pass.** Editing while drafting protects your own sentences. Draft hot, edit cold, cut 10–20% — in that order, as distinct steps.

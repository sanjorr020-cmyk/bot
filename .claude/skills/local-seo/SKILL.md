# Local SEO

Guide the user through improving local search visibility end-to-end: auditing and optimizing their Google Business Profile (GBP), building consistent NAP citations, generating and responding to reviews, creating location landing pages, and earning local links — all tied back to the three local pack ranking factors (relevance, distance, prominence). The deliverable is a prioritized local SEO action plan with ready-to-paste copy for every asset.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the request involves ranking in Google Maps / the local pack, GBP optimization, citations, local reviews, "near me" queries, service-area businesses, or multi-location page strategy.

Do NOT use it for:
- General keyword discovery → **keyword-research** (come back here to localize the winners)
- Site-wide title tags, headers, internal linking beyond location pages → **on-page-seo**
- Site speed, crawlability, sitemaps → **technical-seo**
- Non-local link acquisition (guest posts, digital PR at national scale) → **link-building**
- Collecting testimonials for marketing collateral rather than local rankings → **reviews-and-testimonials**
- Paid local ads (Local Services Ads, location-targeted campaigns) → **google-ads**
- Conversion optimization of the pages themselves → **landing-page-cro**

## Inputs to gather

Ask the user for these before starting. Where they don't know, use the defaults.

1. **Business basics**: exact legal/storefront name, address(es), phone number(s), website URL, primary category of work. No default — required.
2. **Business model**: storefront customers visit, service-area business (SAB) that travels to customers, or hybrid. Default: storefront if they have a public address.
3. **Number of locations**: single or multi-location (how many, which cities). Default: single.
4. **Primary services/products**: top 3-7 revenue-driving services. Default: infer from website or category.
5. **Target geography**: city, neighborhoods, or service radius. Default: the business's own city plus adjacent suburbs.
6. **Current state**: Is GBP claimed and verified? Approximate review count and average rating? Any past suspensions? Default: assume claimed but under-optimized, <20 reviews.
7. **Top 2-3 local competitors**: who shows in the pack for their money keyword. Default: ask the user to search "[primary service] + [city]" and report the 3-pack.
8. **Constraints**: franchise brand rules, regulated industry (legal, medical, finance — review solicitation rules vary), staff capacity for review outreach. Default: none.

If the user has access to their GBP dashboard, ask them to report: primary category, number of secondary categories, description length, photo count, post recency, and Q&A count. This turns the audit from guesswork into specifics.

## Process

### Step 1 — Baseline audit and scorecard

1. Score the current state across the five pillars (GBP, citations, reviews, location pages, local links) on a 0-10 scale each. Present as a table with the single biggest gap flagged.
2. Weight effort by the local pack ranking factor weights (industry surveys such as Whitespark's consistently land near): GBP signals ~30-35%, reviews ~15-20%, on-page ~15-20%, links ~10-15%, behavioral ~8-10%, citations ~5-7%. Prioritize accordingly — GBP and reviews almost always come first.
3. Decision point: if the profile is unverified or suspended, stop and resolve that first; nothing else matters until the listing is live.

### Step 2 — Google Business Profile, field by field

Work through every field and produce ready-to-paste values:

1. **Business name**: exact real-world name only. Adding keywords or city ("Joe's Plumbing | Best Plumber Austin") violates guidelines and is the #1 suspension trigger. If competitors keyword-stuff, note it as a "Suggest an edit" / redressal opportunity, not something to copy.
2. **Primary category**: the single most specific category matching the main revenue line (e.g., "Emergency plumber" beats "Plumber" if emergencies are the business). Primary category is the strongest relevance signal in the profile — choose based on what the top 3-pack competitors use.
3. **Secondary categories**: add up to 9, but only categories the business genuinely serves. Each irrelevant category dilutes relevance.
4. **Address / service area**: storefronts show the address; SABs must hide the address and set up to 20 service-area cities/zips. Never use a PO box, virtual office, or coworking hot desk — all are suspension triggers.
5. **Phone**: a local area-code number, identical everywhere (see NAP rule below). Call tracking numbers are fine only as the "primary" with the real line as "additional," so the real number stays associated.
6. **Hours**: real hours plus holiday hours set 2+ weeks ahead. Wrong hours generate "Closed when I arrived" 1-star reviews.
7. **Description**: up to 750 characters; only the first ~250 show before "More," so front-load service + city + differentiator. No URLs, no promotional ALL CAPS. Draft it using the template below.
8. **Services**: add every service with its own name and up to 300-character description containing the natural keyword. 10-25 services is typical for a healthy trades/professional profile.
9. **Products** (retail/showroom businesses): add 5-20 with photos and prices.
10. **Attributes**: check every applicable one (women-owned, wheelchair accessible, online estimates, etc.) — they surface as filters and justifications.
11. **Photos**: minimum viable set is 10-15; strong profiles run 50-100+. Specs: JPG/PNG, 720x720 px minimum; logo square; cover 16:9 (1024x576 or larger). Upload 2-4 new photos monthly — recency matters. Real photos only; stock photos get filtered and erode conversion.
12. **Google Posts**: up to 1,500 characters but only ~75-100 characters show in the card, so lead with the hook. Cadence: 1 post per week minimum ("Update" posts expire from the carousel after ~6 months; offers/events expire at end date). Every post gets a photo (1200x900 recommended) and a CTA button.
13. **Q&A**: seed 5-10 real customer questions and answer them from the owner account (anyone can answer otherwise, including competitors). Monitor weekly.
14. **Booking/appointment links, menu links** where the category supports them.

### Step 3 — NAP citations

1. State the NAP rule: Name, Address, Phone must be **character-identical** everywhere — "Ste 200" vs "Suite 200" and "(512) 555-0100" vs "512-555-0100" count as inconsistencies to fix, though minor formatting matters far less than wrong/old data.
2. Audit: search Google for `"[old phone number]"`, `"[business name]" [city]`, and prior addresses to find stale listings. Tools like BrightLocal, Whitespark, or Moz Local automate this; manual works for a single location.
3. Fix the **data aggregators first** (Data Axle, Foursquare, and the GPS/data feeds like Neustar/Localeze equivalents) — they syndicate downstream and fix dozens of directories at once.
4. Build/claim the **core 10**: Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps), Yelp, Facebook, Nextdoor, BBB, Yellow Pages, Foursquare, and the top industry vertical (Avvo for lawyers, Healthgrades for doctors, Houzz for contractors, TripAdvisor for hospitality).
5. Add 10-30 niche + local citations: chamber of commerce, city business directories, industry associations. Diminishing returns kick in fast past ~50 quality citations — consistency beats volume.
6. Track everything in the citation tracker table (template below).
7. Decision point: business moved or renamed recently? Treat cleanup of old NAP as higher priority than building new citations — conflicting data suppresses rankings more than missing data.

### Step 4 — Review generation and response

1. **Set the target**: get above both a 4.5 average and the median review count of the current 3-pack (check competitors' counts). Steady velocity beats bursts — 4-10 new reviews/month looks natural; 40 in one week looks purchased and risks filtering.
2. **Build the ask system**: identify the "moment of delight" (job completed, product delivered, problem solved) and trigger the ask within 24 hours while satisfaction peaks. SMS converts best (roughly 15-35% of asks become reviews); email runs ~5-15%. Use the templates below.
3. **Use the direct review link**: GBP provides a short review URL ("Ask for reviews" in the dashboard). Put it in SMS, email signatures, receipts, and a QR code at point of sale.
4. **Never gate**: asking only happy customers via a pre-survey filter ("How was your experience?" → only 5-star sentiments get the Google link) violates Google policy and, in the US, FTC rules. Ask everyone.
5. **Never incentivize** Google reviews (discounts, entries, gifts) — policy violation and removable.
6. **Respond to 100% of reviews**: positive within 1 week, negative within 24-48 hours. Positive responses: 1-3 sentences, vary the wording, naturally mention the service ("Glad the water heater install went smoothly"). Negative responses: use the 4-A framework below.
7. **Keywords in reviews** help relevance — you can't script customers, but the ask template can prompt naturally ("mention which service we did for you").
8. Flag policy-violating reviews (fake, competitor, off-topic) through the dashboard; expect a 3-14 day turnaround and don't respond defensively in the meantime — post one calm factual reply.

### Step 5 — Local landing pages (multi-location / multi-city)

1. Decision point: one location → optimize the homepage + a contact page with full NAP and embedded map; don't build thin city pages. Multiple locations or an SAB targeting several cities → one page per location/city.
2. URL structure: `/locations/[city]/` or `/[city]-[service]/`. One page per city; never one page per city × service permutation unless each can carry genuinely unique content.
3. Each page needs 500-800+ words of **genuinely local** content — swapping the city name into a template is the classic doorway-page failure. Localize with: staff at that location, local photos, neighborhoods served, local reviews, location-specific offers, driving directions from landmarks.
4. Embed the Google Map for that location, full NAP in crawlable HTML text (not just an image), and `LocalBusiness` schema (or the specific subtype: `Plumber`, `Dentist`, `Restaurant`) with name, address, phone, geo, openingHours, and URL.
5. Title tag pattern: `[Primary Service] in [City], [ST] | [Brand]` — keep under ~60 characters. H1 mirrors it without duplicating exactly.
6. Link each location page from the site footer or a locations hub page so it's ≤2 clicks from the homepage, and set each GBP's website field to its matching location page (not the homepage) for multi-location brands.
7. Hand detailed on-page execution to **on-page-seo**; this skill defines what makes the page *local*.

### Step 6 — Local link tactics

Prioritize links that only a local business can get — they're cheap, relevant, and competitors rarely bother:

1. Chamber of commerce + local business associations (membership = link, typically $200-500/yr).
2. Sponsorships: youth sports teams, 5K runs, school events, festivals — most publish sponsor pages with links ($100-1,000).
3. Local news: offer expert commentary on local angles (storm damage → roofer; tax season → CPA). Pitch via **media-outreach** patterns.
4. Local resource pages: "[city] + [industry] + resources", neighborhood guides, "best of" lists — many accept submissions.
5. Partner cross-links: suppliers, complementary businesses (plumber ↔ electrician), venues you work with. Coordinate via **partnership-development**.
6. Local scholarships and event hosting for .edu/.org links — use sparingly and only if genuine.
7. Target: 2-5 new local links/month is enough to move most local markets; measure against the pack competitors' referring domains, not national benchmarks.

### Step 7 — Measurement and cadence

1. Track: local pack position for 5-15 money keywords (use a geo-grid tool like Local Falcon or BrightLocal — rank varies by searcher location, so a single rank number is misleading), GBP insights (calls, direction requests, website clicks), review count/rating monthly, citation accuracy quarterly.
2. Expect movement in 30-90 days for GBP + review changes; 3-6 months for pages and links.
3. Monthly recurring loop: 4 GBP posts, 2-4 photos, answer new Q&A, respond to all reviews, 2-5 review asks/week per staffer, 1-2 local link placements. Write this into an owner-facing checklist as part of the deliverable.

## Frameworks & templates

### Relevance–Distance–Prominence (Google's stated local factors)

- **Relevance**: does the profile/page match the query? Levers: primary category, services list, description, on-page content, review keywords.
- **Distance**: proximity of the business to the searcher. Levers: limited — service-area settings, location pages in target cities. Never fake addresses.
- **Prominence**: how well-known the business is. Levers: review count/rating/velocity, citations, links, branded search volume, engagement (calls, direction requests).
Diagnose ranking problems in this order: wrong category (relevance) → outside the radius (distance, often unfixable) → outgunned on reviews/links (prominence).

### GBP description template (≤750 chars, front-load first 250)

> [Business name] provides [primary service] and [secondary service] to [city + area] [customers/homeowners/businesses]. [One differentiator: years in business, certification, guarantee, specialty]. Our [team descriptor] handles [service 3], [service 4], and [service 5], with [proof point: same-day service / free estimates / 24/7 availability]. [Trust line: licensed & insured, family-owned since YYYY, 4.9-star rated by NNN customers]. Call or book online for [offer/CTA].

Worked example (487 chars):
> Ridgeline Plumbing provides emergency plumbing repair and water heater installation to Boise homeowners and businesses. Family-owned since 2009, we answer calls 24/7 and reach most Treasure Valley addresses within 90 minutes. Our licensed plumbers handle drain cleaning, repiping, sump pumps, and tankless water heater upgrades, with upfront flat-rate pricing and a 2-year workmanship warranty. Licensed, bonded, and insured in Idaho. Call or book online for same-day service.

### Review request templates

**SMS (send within 24h of service; keep under 320 chars / 2 segments):**
> Hi [FirstName], thanks for choosing [Business] for your [service] today! If [tech/staff name] took good care of you, would you leave us a quick Google review? It takes 60 seconds and helps our small business a lot: [short review link]

**Email (subject: "How did we do, [FirstName]?"):**
> Hi [FirstName],
> Thanks for trusting [Business] with your [service] on [date]. We'd love to hear how it went — reviews from customers like you are how neighbors in [city] find us.
> [Button: Leave a Google review — takes 1 minute]
> If anything wasn't right, reply to this email and [owner name] will personally make it right.
> — [Name], [Business]

### 4-A negative review response framework

1. **Acknowledge** — use their name, name the specific issue (proves you read it).
2. **Apologize** — for the experience, without admitting legal fault in regulated industries.
3. **Act** — state the concrete fix or investigation underway.
4. **Advance offline** — give a direct name + phone/email to continue privately.

Worked example:
> Hi Marcus, I'm sorry your Tuesday installation ran three hours past the window we promised — that's not the standard we hold ourselves to. I've reviewed the scheduling miss with our dispatch team and we've added buffer time for jobs like yours. I'd like to make this right personally: please call me at (208) 555-0142 or email dan@ridgelineplumbing.com. — Dan, Owner

Keep it under ~150 words, never argue facts publicly, never offer compensation in the public reply (invites copycat complaints).

### Location page skeleton

1. H1: [Service] in [City], [ST]
2. Intro (100-150 words): who you serve in this city, response time, differentiator
3. Services offered at this location (linked to service pages)
4. Neighborhoods/zips served (real list, 8-15 items)
5. Local proof: 2-3 reviews from customers in this city, local job photos
6. Team/office section: staff names, photo of the actual location or crew
7. Embedded Google Map + full NAP + hours in HTML text
8. FAQ (3-5 city-specific questions: permits, response times, local pricing factors)
9. CTA block with local phone number
10. `LocalBusiness` schema in JSON-LD

## Output format

Deliver a single local SEO action plan document containing:

1. **Scorecard** — the five-pillar 0-10 table with the top 3 priorities flagged.
2. **GBP optimization sheet** — every field with its exact recommended value, ready to paste (name, categories, description, 10+ services with descriptions, attributes list, first month of 4 post drafts).
3. **Citation plan** — the tracker table pre-filled with the core 10 + niche directories for their industry, aggregators listed first, inconsistencies found flagged.
4. **Review system** — target count/rating vs. the pack competitors, ask workflow (trigger, channel, timing), SMS + email templates customized to the business, and response templates for 5-star, 3-star, and 1-star reviews.
5. **Location page briefs** — for multi-location: URL, title tag, H1, and the 10-section skeleton filled with location-specific direction for each page (or homepage guidance for single location).
6. **Local link target list** — 10-20 named local opportunities (actual chambers, teams, events, directories in their city where identifiable; categories with search instructions where not).
7. **Monthly maintenance checklist** — the recurring loop from Step 7 as an owner-facing checklist.

Deliver as markdown. If the user wants a client-facing version, offer a cleaner formatted document with the audit findings up front.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] GBP business name recommendation contains zero added keywords or city names beyond the real-world name
- [ ] Primary category is a single specific choice, justified against what pack competitors use
- [ ] Description is ≤750 characters with service + city in the first 250
- [ ] Every service listed has a description ≤300 characters containing a natural keyword
- [ ] NAP is written out once, canonically, and every template/table uses it character-identically
- [ ] Data aggregators are sequenced before individual directory cleanup in the citation plan
- [ ] Review targets are benchmarked against the actual 3-pack competitors' counts and ratings, not generic numbers
- [ ] Review ask templates include the direct Google review link placeholder and no incentive language
- [ ] Negative review response follows 4-A, stays under 150 words, moves offline
- [ ] Single-location businesses are NOT given thin city-page recommendations
- [ ] Each multi-location page brief includes ≥3 elements impossible to template-swap (local staff, local reviews, local photos)
- [ ] `LocalBusiness` schema (or subtype) specified for every location page
- [ ] Link targets are locally scoped (chamber, sponsorships, local press), not generic guest-post advice
- [ ] Plan includes a measurement method that accounts for rank varying by searcher location (geo-grid, not single rank)
- [ ] Monthly maintenance checklist exists and is executable by a non-SEO owner

## Common mistakes

- **Keyword-stuffing the GBP name** because competitors do it. It works until the suspension, and reinstatement takes 2-6 weeks of lost visibility. Report competitors instead.
- **Choosing a broad primary category** ("Contractor") when a specific one exists ("Bathroom remodeler"). The primary category is the highest-leverage single field on the profile.
- **Building 50 city pages from one template** with only the city name swapped. Google's doorway-page systems suppress these; 5 genuinely local pages outrank 50 thin ones.
- **Chasing citation volume** after the core ~50 are done. The 200-directory packages sold by cheap vendors add junk listings you'll spend years cleaning up.
- **Review gating** via "rate us first" surveys. Policy violation, FTC exposure, and the filtered unhappy customers post publicly anyway — on Yelp, unresponded.
- **Bursting reviews** — 30 reviews in a week after a staff contest, then silence for six months. Velocity spikes trigger filtering; steady drip does not.
- **Copy-pasting the same review response** ("Thank you for your feedback!") on every review. It reads as automated to both customers and Google; vary wording and mention the service.
- **Using a virtual office or coworking address** to fake a presence in a second city. Leads to suspension and often takes the legitimate listing down with it.
- **Setting the GBP website link to the homepage for every location** of a multi-location brand instead of each location's own page — this severs the relevance connection the location pages exist to create.
- **Ignoring Q&A**, letting customers (or competitors) answer questions with wrong prices and hours that then display on the profile.
- **Tracking one rank number** for "plumber near me." Local rank varies block by block; a geo-grid report showing you rank #2 downtown and #15 in the suburbs changes the strategy entirely.
- **Treating local SEO as a one-time project.** Posts expire, photos go stale, reviews age; profiles that stop updating bleed pack positions to active competitors within a quarter.

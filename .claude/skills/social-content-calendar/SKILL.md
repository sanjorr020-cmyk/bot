# Social Content Calendar

Turn a social strategy into a concrete, dated 30-day plan: which pillar, which format, which hook, on which platform, at what time — plus the batching workflow to produce it all in a few focused sessions and a monthly review loop so each calendar gets smarter than the last. The deliverable is a calendar the user can execute without making a single "what do I post today?" decision mid-month.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user needs:
- A dated 30-day (or 4-week) posting plan for one or more social platforms
- A repeatable system: pillar rotation, format mix, batching schedule, hook bank
- A calendar built around a specific event (launch, seasonal campaign, conference)
- A monthly retro that turns last month's data into next month's calendar

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Choosing platforms, audience, positioning, or pillars from scratch — that's **social-media-strategy**. If the user has no pillars defined, either run that skill first or set provisional pillars here and flag them as assumptions.
- Writing the actual posts in platform-native voice — hand individual posts to **twitter-x-content**, **linkedin-content**, or **short-form-video**. This skill plans slots and hooks; those skills fill them.
- Turning one asset into many posts — **content-repurposing** owns the transformation mechanics; this skill just reserves calendar slots for repurposed content.
- Replying, DMs, and engagement routines — **community-management**.
- Paid promotion of posts — **meta-ads** or **google-ads**.
- Deep performance analysis beyond the monthly review — **analytics-reporting**.

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these up front. Where the user shrugs, apply the default and state it in the deliverable's assumptions section.

| Input | Ask | Default if unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Platforms | "Which platforms, ranked by priority?" | 1 primary + 1 repurpose-target; never plan 4+ platforms for a solo operator |
| Content pillars | "What are your 3–5 content pillars and rough weights?" | Propose 4: Educate 40%, Engage 25%, Authority/proof 20%, Promote 15% |
| Posting frequency | "How many posts per week per platform can you sustain?" | LinkedIn 3–4/wk, Instagram 4–5/wk, TikTok 3–5/wk, X/Twitter 5–7/wk, Facebook 3/wk, YouTube 1/wk |
| Production capacity | "Who makes content, and how many hours/week?" | Solo, 4–5 hrs/week total — cap the plan at ~15–18 posts/month on the primary platform |
| Fixed dates | "Any launches, promos, holidays, or events this month?" | None; check the calendar month for obvious holidays anyway |
| Existing winners | "Your 3–5 best-performing posts from the last 90 days?" | None — first calendar runs on hypotheses, flagged for the review loop |
| Audience timezone | "Where does your audience live?" | User's own timezone |
| Offer/CTA | "What should promotional posts point to this month?" | Single primary CTA (newsletter, demo, product page) |

Decision point: if the user wants 3+ platforms but has under 8 hrs/week of capacity, push back. Recommend one "creation platform" where native content is made and one "distribution platform" fed by repurposing.

## Process

1. **Lock the month's skeleton.** Place fixed dates first: launch day, promo windows, relevant holidays. Work backward from each launch: teaser posts at T-7 and T-3, announcement at T-0, proof/social-post at T+2, recap at T+7. These slots are immovable; everything else flows around them.

2. **Set the pillar rotation.** Convert pillar weights into a repeating weekly pattern so no two consecutive posts share a pillar. For a 4-post week at 40/25/20/15: Mon = Educate, Wed = Engage, Thu = Authority, Fri = Educate; the Promote pillar takes one slot every ~7th post (2 slots/month at 15%). Enforce the 80/20 rule: no more than 1 in 5 posts is directly promotional, launch weeks excepted (there, allow up to 40% promo for that one week, then compensate the following week).

3. **Assign the format mix per platform.** Match formats to pillars and to what each platform's algorithm currently favors:
   - **LinkedIn:** 50% text posts (150–300 words, line breaks every 1–2 sentences), 30% carousels/PDF documents (8–12 slides), 20% native video (30–90 sec). Links go in comments, not the post body.
   - **Instagram:** 40% Reels (15–45 sec), 40% carousels (6–10 slides — carousels typically earn 1.2–1.9x the reach of single images), 20% single image/quote; Stories daily-ish but off-calendar.
   - **TikTok:** 100% video, 15–60 sec; hook inside the first 1–2 seconds.
   - **X/Twitter:** 60% single posts (under 280 chars), 25% threads (5–9 tweets), 15% image/screenshot posts.
   - **YouTube:** long-form weekly (8–15 min) or Shorts 3x/week (under 60 sec) — not both unless a team exists.
   Rotate formats so the same format never runs 3+ times consecutively on one platform.

4. **Fill every slot with a working title + hook.** Each calendar cell gets: pillar, format, working title, hook drawn from the hook bank (below), and CTA type (none / soft / hard). A slot that just says "educational post" is unfinished — the batching session should never start with a blank ideation problem. Generate 20–30 topic ideas per pillar by mining: customer questions, objections heard on sales calls, keyword research the user already has (see **keyword-research** output if available), competitor gaps, and contrarian takes on industry defaults.

5. **Reserve repurposing slots.** Mark 20–30% of slots as "derived" — a thread rewritten as a carousel, a video clipped from a webinar, a blog section turned into a text post. Note the source asset in the cell. This is how a 15-hour month covers 2 platforms.

6. **Set posting times.** Use these as starting points (audience-local time), then let the review loop override them:
   - LinkedIn: Tue–Thu, 8:00–10:00am; secondary window 12:00pm
   - Instagram: weekdays 11:00am–1:00pm or 7:00–9:00pm
   - TikTok: 6:00–10:00pm daily; weekends mid-morning
   - X/Twitter: weekdays 9:00am–12:00pm; avoid Fri evening–Sun morning for business content
   - Facebook: weekdays 9:00am–1:00pm
   - YouTube: publish Thu/Fri 2:00–4:00pm to catch weekend watch time
   Consistency beats precision: same days, same windows, every week, for at least 4 weeks before judging.

7. **Design the batching workflow.** Schedule 4 recurring blocks and write them into the deliverable:
   - **Block 1 — Monthly planning (2 hrs, last week of prior month):** run the review loop, fill next month's calendar.
   - **Block 2 — Writing batch (2–3 hrs weekly or 1 full day monthly):** draft all text content for the coming week(s), batched *by format* — all hooks first, then all bodies, then all CTAs. Context-switching between formats costs roughly 20–30% throughput.
   - **Block 3 — Production batch (2 hrs weekly):** record all videos back-to-back in one setup; design all carousels from one template.
   - **Block 4 — Scheduling (30–45 min weekly):** load everything into the scheduler (Buffer, Later, Metricool, or native tools like Meta Business Suite); verify previews, alt text, first comments.
   Rule: nothing is written on its posting day except reactive/newsjacking posts (leave 1–2 flex slots/month for these).

8. **Run the monthly review loop** (this is Block 1's first 45 minutes):
   a. Export engagement rate (interactions ÷ impressions or ÷ followers — pick one and keep it) for every post.
   b. Rank. Flag the top 20% as winners, bottom 20% as losers. Benchmarks for "good" ER by followers: Instagram 1–3%, LinkedIn 2–5%, TikTok 4–8%, X 0.5–1.5%, Facebook 0.5–1%.
   c. For each winner, diagnose the variable: pillar? hook type? format? time? Write one sentence per winner.
   d. Feed forward: schedule 2–4 "winner remixes" next month — same idea in a new format, a sequel, an updated version, or a straight repost after 6–8 weeks (reposting top content after 45+ days typically recovers 60–75% of original engagement to a mostly-new audience slice).
   e. Kill or fix: any pillar or format underperforming the account average by 50%+ for two consecutive months loses slots to the best performer.
   f. Adjust one variable at a time (times OR format mix OR pillar weights) so cause is attributable — coordinate with **ab-testing** logic if the user wants rigor.

9. **Deliver and handoff.** Produce the output format below. Point the user to **twitter-x-content** / **linkedin-content** / **short-form-video** to draft the actual posts, and offer to draft week 1 now.

## Frameworks & templates

### Pillar rotation matrix (4-post week, default weights)

| Week | Mon | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Educate (how-to) | Engage (question) | Authority (case result) | Educate (myth-bust) |
| 2 | Educate (framework) | Engage (poll) | **Promote (offer)** | Educate (tutorial) |
| 3 | Educate (how-to) | Authority (behind-scenes) | Engage (hot take) | Educate (listicle) |
| 4 | Educate (mistakes) | Engage (question) | **Promote (proof + CTA)** | Authority (milestone) |

Result over the month: Educate 44%, Engage 25%, Authority 19%, Promote 12% — within tolerance of the 40/25/20/15 target. Adapt the grid to the user's frequency; the invariants are (a) no consecutive same-pillar posts, (b) promo ≤20% of slots, (c) every promo post is preceded within 3 slots by an authority/proof post.

### Hook bank (fill-in-the-blank, grouped by job)

Curiosity:
- "Most [audience] get [topic] wrong. Here's what the top 1% do instead."
- "I [did X] for [timeframe]. These [N] things surprised me."
- "The [industry] advice everyone repeats — and why it quietly fails."

Specific-result:
- "[Result] in [timeframe], without [common painful method]. The exact steps:"
- "We changed one thing about [process] and [metric] went from [X] to [Y]."

Contrarian:
- "Unpopular opinion: [common practice] is costing you [outcome]."
- "Stop [common activity]. Do [alternative] instead. Here's the math."

List/utility:
- "[N] [tools/mistakes/templates] for [outcome] — #[K] is the one nobody uses."
- "Steal my exact [process/checklist] for [task] (took [time] to figure out):"

Story:
- "In [year], I [low point]. Last month, [high point]. What changed:"
- "A client asked me [question]. My answer changed how they [do X]:"

Engagement-bait (use sparingly, max 1/week):
- "[Option A] or [Option B] — which one and why?"
- "What's the one [tool/habit/book] you'd never give up for [goal]?"

Maintain this bank in the deliverable; the review loop adds each month's winning hooks and retires any hook style that lands in the bottom 20% twice.

## Output format

Deliver a single markdown document titled "[Brand] — [Month Year] Content Calendar" containing, in order:

1. **Assumptions & inputs** — platforms, frequency, pillar weights, capacity, fixed dates; flag every default you applied.
2. **Pillar rotation matrix** — the month's grid as in the template above.
3. **The calendar** — one table per week with columns: Date, Platform, Time, Pillar, Format, Working title + hook, CTA, Source (Native/Derived←asset). Every slot filled; 1–2 slots/month marked FLEX (reactive).
4. **Hook bank** — 12–15 fill-in-the-blank hooks grouped by job, seeded with any of the user's proven winners.
5. **Batching schedule** — the 4 blocks with specific proposed days/times and durations.
6. **Posting-time defaults** — per platform, with the note that the review loop overrides them.
7. **Monthly review template** — pre-filled with this month's post list, ready to complete at month end.
8. **Handoffs** — which sibling skills to invoke to draft the posts themselves.

Offer a CSV export of the calendar table (Date, Time, Platform, Pillar, Format, Title, Hook, CTA, Status) if the user schedules via a tool that imports CSV.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every calendar slot has a specific working title and hook — zero cells that say only "educational post" or similar.
- [ ] No two consecutive posts on the same platform share a pillar.
- [ ] Promotional posts are ≤20% of total slots (excluding a declared launch week).
- [ ] Every promo post is preceded within 3 slots by an authority/proof post.
- [ ] Same format never appears 3+ times in a row on one platform.
- [ ] Post volume fits stated capacity: total production hours ≤ user's weekly hours × 4 (estimate 20–30 min/text post, 45–60 min/carousel, 60–90 min/short video).
- [ ] Fixed dates (launches, holidays) are placed and have T-7/T-3/T-0/T+2 support posts where applicable.
- [ ] 20–30% of slots are marked Derived with a named source asset.
- [ ] 1–2 FLEX slots exist for reactive content.
- [ ] Every post has an assigned local time inside the platform's recommended window (or a documented reason to deviate).
- [ ] Hook bank has ≥12 hooks across ≥4 hook types.
- [ ] Batching blocks are scheduled with days, times, and durations — not "batch weekly".
- [ ] The review template is included and pre-filled with the month's post list.
- [ ] Assumptions section discloses every default applied for missing inputs.

## Common mistakes

- **Planning volume for the team you wish you had.** A calendar with 25 posts/month for someone with 3 hrs/week collapses by week 2, and inconsistency reads worse to algorithms than lower volume. Cut frequency until the math works, then raise it after two consistent months.
- **The blank-slot calendar.** Cells labeled "tips post" defer all the hard thinking to production day, which defeats batching. Titles and hooks are calendar work, not writing-day work.
- **Promo bunching.** Users cluster all promotional posts into launch week and then feel weird promoting again, so month 2 has zero promo. Spread the 15–20% evenly and treat launch weeks as a declared exception with a compensating week after.
- **Copy-paste cross-posting.** Pushing the identical caption to LinkedIn, X, and Instagram ignores length limits, link behavior (LinkedIn punishes body links; X truncates), and tone. Derived slots mean *adapted*, not duplicated — that's the repurposing skill's job.
- **Judging posting times after one week.** Timing signal is noisy at small follower counts; a post's reach varies ±50% on identical setups. Hold times constant for 4+ weeks and compare windows, not individual posts.
- **Measuring likes instead of engagement rate.** A 10,000-follower account's 80 likes (0.8%) is losing to a 1,000-follower account's 30 likes (3%). Normalize by impressions or followers or the review loop rewards the wrong posts.
- **Retiring winners after one use.** Most of the audience never saw the original — typical organic reach is 5–15% of followers per post. Not remixing or reposting top content after 6–8 weeks is leaving the cheapest wins on the table.
- **Changing three variables at once in the review.** New pillar weights + new times + new formats in the same month means next month's numbers explain nothing. Max two decisions per review, one variable each.
- **Letting the calendar ban timeliness.** A 100%-locked calendar makes the account feel canned and misses newsjacking moments. The 1–2 FLEX slots are load-bearing; defend them.

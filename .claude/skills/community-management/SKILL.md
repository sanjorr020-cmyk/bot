# Community Management

This skill produces a complete community engagement operations manual: who responds to what, how fast, in what voice, when to escalate, how to source and legally reuse user-generated content, and what to do in the first hour of a viral pile-on. The deliverable is an SOP a new community manager could run on day one without asking questions — every rule has a number, a template, or a decision point attached.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user needs:
- Response-time targets and coverage plans for comments, mentions, DMs, and reviews
- A tone guide and reply templates for public-facing responses
- Moderation rules: what gets hidden, deleted, banned, or escalated
- An escalation matrix (who handles legal threats, press inquiries, safety issues)
- A UGC sourcing workflow with rights-clearance language
- A crisis response protocol for viral negativity or coordinated pile-ons

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Deciding which platforms to be on or what content to post — use **social-media-strategy** and **social-content-calendar**
- Writing the posts themselves — use **twitter-x-content**, **linkedin-content**, or **short-form-video**
- Recruiting and paying creators — use **influencer-marketing**
- Soliciting and responding to reviews on Google/G2/Yelp as a growth program — use **reviews-and-testimonials** (this skill covers only the response SLA for reviews)
- Turning community feedback into product insight — use **customer-feedback-analysis**
- Writing a public statement for press during a crisis — use **press-releases**; this skill covers the social-channel side only
- Support ticket deflection and help content — use **help-docs**

## Inputs to gather

Ask the user for these. Where they don't know, apply the default and state the assumption in the deliverable.

1. **Active channels and rough daily inbound volume** (comments + DMs + mentions per day). Default: assume Instagram + X/Twitter + LinkedIn + one review platform at <50 total interactions/day (a one-person operation).
2. **Team and coverage hours.** Who responds, in what timezone, weekends or not. Default: one community manager, business hours Mon–Fri, no weekend coverage.
3. **Brand voice adjectives** (3–5 words) or links to existing brand guidelines. Default: "helpful, plainspoken, warm, never sarcastic." If a **brand-messaging** deliverable exists, inherit from it.
4. **Regulated or sensitive industry?** (health, finance, alcohol, kids' products, legal). This changes moderation rules and forbids some reply content. Default: not regulated.
5. **Escalation contacts.** Who owns support, legal, PR/comms, and security. Default: founder is the catch-all for all four; flag this as a single point of failure.
6. **Existing tooling.** Native inboxes vs. Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Buffer, Zendesk, Discord bots. Default: native platform inboxes only.
7. **UGC ambitions.** Do they want to repost customer content, and will any of it be used in paid ads? Paid usage requires stricter rights language. Default: organic reposts only.
8. **Known flashpoints.** Past controversies, recurring complaint themes, competitor drama, activist attention. Default: none known, but include the generic crisis triggers.

## Process

1. **Map the inbound surface.** List every place the public can talk to or about the brand: post comments, @-mentions, quote posts, DMs, review platforms, Reddit/forum threads, and (if applicable) owned communities like Discord or a Facebook Group. For each, note whether it is public or private, and whether the platform algorithm punishes slow responses (Instagram and TikTok comments are engagement-window sensitive in the first 60 minutes; reviews are not).

2. **Set response-time SLAs per channel.** Use the benchmark table in Frameworks as the starting grid, then adjust for the user's team size. Rules: (a) public complaints always get the tightest SLA because silence is visible; (b) never set an SLA the stated coverage hours can't meet — a 1-hour target with no weekend coverage needs an explicit "next business day" carve-out published in the bio or auto-reply; (c) set a separate "first human touch" target vs. "resolution" target for anything requiring support (e.g., first touch 2 hours, resolution handoff to support within 24 hours).

3. **Write the tone guide.** Take the 3–5 brand voice adjectives and translate each into one "do" and one "don't" with a sample sentence (template below). Add three hard rules that apply regardless of voice: never argue past two public replies (move to DM), never blame the customer publicly, never delete a critical comment that isn't rule-breaking. If the brand voice is playful/snarky, add an explicit "snark whitelist": snark is allowed toward the brand itself and toward lighthearted jokes, never toward an individual user's complaint.

4. **Build the public triage matrix.** Classify every inbound public interaction into one of six lanes: Praise, Question, Complaint, Troll/Bad-faith, Misinformation, Crisis-signal. For each lane define the action, the SLA, and the template family (matrix in Frameworks). The decision point that separates Complaint from Troll: a complaint contains a fixable grievance or a specific experience; a troll contains neither and escalates when engaged. When ambiguous, treat as Complaint once — one good-faith reply — then reclassify based on the response.

5. **Build the DM triage workflow.** DMs collapse into five buckets: support requests, sales/lead inquiries, partnership/influencer pitches, spam, and personal/sensitive disclosures. Define per bucket: destination (answer in DM, route to support tool, route to sales), SLA, and a saved-reply template. Sales inquiries route per the user's **lead-qualification** process if one exists. Sensitive disclosures (self-harm mentions, abuse reports, safety issues) get an immediate escalation path and a compassionate holding reply — never a template that reads automated. Require a daily DM zero: inbox cleared to triaged status once per coverage day.

6. **Write moderation rules with a 3-strike ladder.** Define two lists: (a) instant-remove-and-ban offenses — hate speech, threats, doxxing, spam links, scam impersonation accounts, sexually explicit content; (b) strike-ladder offenses — personal insults toward other community members, repeated off-topic derailing, unfounded accusations. Ladder: strike 1 = hide/delete + private warning; strike 2 = delete + final warning; strike 3 = ban and document. Log every ban with screenshot, date, and reason in a moderation log (a simple spreadsheet is fine) — this is the defense when a banned user claims censorship publicly. Never hide or delete criticism of the brand itself unless it breaks a rule on the lists; instead answer it.

7. **Define the escalation matrix.** Four tiers with named owners and notification deadlines (template below). The community manager's authority boundary must be explicit: they may apologize for experience ("sorry this was frustrating") but may not admit fault or liability ("we broke it"), may offer goodwill gestures up to a stated dollar cap (default $50), and must escalate anything involving legal threats, press, injury/safety, data breaches, or employee conduct within 30 minutes of seeing it.

8. **Build the UGC sourcing and rights workflow.** (a) Sourcing: monitor the branded hashtag, tagged posts, and location tags weekly; keep a shortlist of repostable content in a tracking sheet with link, creator handle, and rights status. (b) Rights: for organic reposts, an affirmative written reply is required — a public comment ask plus the creator replying with an agreed consent hashtag (e.g., "#YesBrandName") or a DM "yes" both count; a like or non-response never counts. (c) For paid/ads usage, hashtag consent is not enough — send the written rights request template (below) covering scope, duration, and compensation if any, and store the confirmation. (d) Always credit the creator in the caption with their handle; crediting does not replace permission. (e) Repost within 30 days of consent or re-confirm.

9. **Write the crisis protocol.** Define quantitative triggers that flip the operation from normal to crisis mode (defaults, tune to account size): negative mention volume ≥3x the 7-day hourly baseline for 2+ consecutive hours; any single negative post about the brand exceeding 100k views or 500 shares/quote-posts; a journalist or an account >250k followers amplifying a complaint; any allegation involving safety, discrimination, or law-breaking regardless of reach. In crisis mode: (1) pause all scheduled posts within 15 minutes — a cheerful scheduled meme during a pile-on is self-inflicted damage; (2) notify the escalation owner; (3) do not mass-reply — post or pin ONE holding statement (template below) within 2 hours; (4) reply individually only to the top 3–5 most-visible posts, pointing to the statement; (5) log everything hourly (volume, sentiment, top posts); (6) never delete the original criticism unless it breaks moderation rules — screenshots outlive deletions. Define the exit condition: mention volume back under 1.5x baseline for 24 hours, then post the substantive follow-up (what happened, what changed).

10. **Assemble, socialize, and instrument.** Compile everything into the single SOP document (Output format below). Add a measurement section: track weekly median first-response time per channel, response rate (% of non-spam inbound answered), and sentiment mix (% praise/complaint); review the SOP quarterly and after every crisis activation. Hand reporting design to **kpi-dashboard-design** if the user wants a dashboard.

## Frameworks & templates

### Response-time SLA benchmarks (starting grid — tighten with team size)

| Channel | First response target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| X/Twitter public complaint | 1 hour (business hours) | Most visible, most screenshot-prone |
| Instagram / TikTok comments | 2–4 hours; within 60 min on posts <24h old | Early replies boost post reach |
| Facebook comments & Messenger | 2–4 hours | Meta shows "typically replies in X" badge under 24h avg |
| LinkedIn comments | Same business day | Slower norms; substance over speed |
| DMs (support-type) | 2–4 hours business hours; auto-reply after hours | Auto-reply must state real hours |
| Owned Discord / community | 30–60 min during stated active hours | Or deputize member moderators |
| Reviews (Google, G2, Yelp) | 24–48 hours, 100% response rate on ≤3-star | See reviews-and-testimonials for solicitation |
| Reddit / forum mentions | 24 hours, only via a verified/disclosed account | Undisclosed brand accounts get destroyed |

### HEARD framework (public complaint replies)

- **H — Hear:** restate their specific issue so the reply is visibly not canned ("the order arrived two weeks late").
- **E — Empathize:** one genuine sentence, no corporate padding ("that would frustrate me too").
- **A — Acknowledge/Apologize:** apologize for the experience; admit fault only if cleared to.
- **R — Resolve:** state the concrete next step and channel ("DM us your order number and we'll reship today").
- **D — Diagnose:** offstage — log the root cause in the feedback tracker (feeds **customer-feedback-analysis**).

Worked example: *"Ouch — two weeks late and no tracking updates is not the experience we want anyone to have. I'm sorry. DM us your order number and I'll get a replacement out today and find out where the first one went. — Sam"*

### Public triage matrix

| Lane | Signal | Action | SLA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Praise | Compliment, tag, positive UGC | Reply with specific thanks; shortlist for UGC | Same day |
| Question | Product/how-to ask | Answer publicly (others read it); link help doc | Per channel SLA |
| Complaint | Fixable grievance, real experience | HEARD reply; max 2 public exchanges then DM | Tightest SLA |
| Troll | No grievance, engagement bait, slurs | One factual reply max or none; hide/ban per rules | No SLA owed |
| Misinformation | False claim about product/company | One public correction with evidence; don't debate | 4 hours |
| Crisis-signal | Matches any crisis trigger | Stop; run crisis protocol step 9 | 15 min to escalate |

### DM triage routing table (starting defaults)

| Bucket | Signal | Destination | SLA | Saved reply? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Support request | Order issue, bug, how-to | Answer in DM or hand to support tool with ticket link | 2–4 hours | Yes — ask for order/account ID |
| Sales / lead | Pricing, demo, bulk order | Route per lead-qualification process; tag as lead | 2 hours | Yes — qualify + booking link |
| Partnership / influencer pitch | Collab ask, media kit | Weekly batch review; forward fits to influencer-marketing owner | 1 week | Yes — polite hold with criteria |
| Spam / scam | Link drops, crypto, impersonators | Report + block, no reply | — | No |
| Sensitive disclosure | Self-harm, abuse, safety, minors | Immediate human escalation (Tier 3); compassionate holding reply | 15 minutes | Yes — but personalize before sending |

### Worked reply examples by lane

- **Praise:** *"This made our week — the shelf install looks better than our own product shots. Mind if we share it? Reply #YesAcme and we'll credit you. 🙌"*
- **Question (public):** *"Great question — it's dishwasher-safe on the top rack up to 65°C. Full care guide here: [link]. Anything else, just ask."*
- **Troll disengage (one reply, then done):** *"Happy to help with any actual order issue — DM us the details. Otherwise, have a good one."*
- **Misinformation correction:** *"Quick correction for anyone reading: our products are manufactured in [place] and certified by [body] — cert #[number], verifiable at [registry]. Happy to answer specifics."*
- **Take-it-to-DM (after 2 public exchanges):** *"I want to fix this properly rather than go back and forth here — I've just DM'd you. Everything we agree there, I'll confirm publicly in this thread."*
- **Sensitive-disclosure holding reply (personalize, never send raw):** *"Thank you for trusting us with this — I've read it and a real person (me, [name]) is looking at it right now. I'm escalating it immediately and you'll hear from us within [time]. If you're in immediate danger, please contact [local emergency number / crisis line]."*
- **After-hours auto-reply:** *"Thanks for reaching out! Our team replies Mon–Fri, 9am–6pm [TZ]. You'll hear from a human within [X] hours of opening. Urgent order issue? Email [address] with 'URGENT' in the subject."*

### Tone guide template (one row per brand adjective)

| Adjective | Do | Don't | Sample line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm | Use first names, sign with the CM's name | Pet names ("hun"), exclamation stacking | "Thanks for flagging this, Maria — on it." |
| Plainspoken | Short sentences, name the problem | "We apologize for any inconvenience" | "That's a bug. Fix is shipping Thursday." |

### Escalation matrix template

| Tier | Trigger | Owner | Notify within |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Routine complaint, refund ≤ $[50] | Community manager resolves | — |
| 2 | Refund > cap, repeat complainer, product defect pattern | Support lead | Same business day |
| 3 | Legal threat, press inquiry, injury/safety, data/privacy issue | [Legal/PR contact] | 30 minutes |
| 4 | Crisis trigger met (step 9) | [Exec sponsor] + all Tier 3 | 15 minutes |

### UGC rights request (DM, for paid usage)

> Hi [name] — we love the [photo/video] you posted on [date] featuring [product]. We'd like to feature it in [brand]'s marketing, including [organic social / paid ads / website], worldwide, for [12 months / perpetuity], with credit to @[handle]. [We'd like to send you [product/$X] as a thank-you.] If you're happy with that, just reply "I agree" and we'll take care of the rest. You keep ownership of your content and can ask us to stop using it anytime by emailing [address].

For organic-only reposts the short form is a public comment: *"We love this! 😍 Can we share it on our page with credit? Reply #Yes[Brand] to say yes."*

### Crisis holding statement (fill-in, ≤ 80 words, post within 2 hours)

> We've seen the posts about [issue, named plainly — do not euphemize]. We're taking it seriously and are [investigating / have paused X / are contacting affected customers] right now. We'll share a full update by [specific time within 24–48h]. If you're affected, [DM us / email address] and a human will respond. — [Name, role]

Rules: name the issue (vagueness reads as evasion), give a deadline you will actually hit, sign it with a human name, and never include "but," a justification, or blame in a holding statement.

### Crisis first-hour checklist (print this)

1. **0–15 min:** Pause all scheduled posts on every channel. Screenshot the triggering post(s) with timestamps and view counts.
2. **0–15 min:** Notify Tier 4 owner by phone/Slack, not email. State: trigger met, which one, current volume vs. baseline.
3. **15–45 min:** Draft holding statement using the template; get one approval (pre-agree who — a 5-person approval chain kills the 2-hour deadline).
4. **By 2 hours:** Post/pin the holding statement on the channel where the fire started, then mirror to other active channels.
5. **Hourly:** Log volume, top 5 posts by reach, sentiment shift, any journalist or >250k-follower amplification. One shared doc, timestamped entries.
6. **Ongoing:** Reply only to the 3–5 most-visible posts, each pointing to the statement. Everyone else gets no individual replies until volume subsides — mass-replying multiplies visibility.

### Post-crisis review (run within 5 business days of exit)

Answer in writing: Which trigger fired and how long until detection? Was the holding statement out within 2 hours — if not, what blocked it? What was peak volume vs. baseline and time-to-return under 1.5x? Which SOP rule was missing or wrong? Update the SOP version number with the fixes.

### Moderation log format (minimum columns)

`Date | Platform | User handle | Content (screenshot link) | Rule violated | Action taken (hide/delete/warn/ban) | Strike # | Moderator initials`

Keep it in a shared sheet with edit history on. Review monthly for repeat handles and ban-evasion patterns (same phrasing, new accounts).

## Output format

Deliver one document titled **"[Brand] Community Management SOP — v1.0 — [date]"** with these sections in order:

1. **Coverage & channels** — table of channels, owners, coverage hours, tools
2. **Response-time SLAs** — the filled SLA table plus after-hours auto-reply copy
3. **Tone guide** — adjective table, three hard rules, snark policy if applicable
4. **Public triage matrix** — the six-lane table with brand-specific examples
5. **Reply template library** — minimum 8 fill-in templates: praise, question, complaint (HEARD), troll disengage, misinformation correction, take-it-to-DM, after-hours auto-reply, sensitive-disclosure holding reply
6. **DM triage** — five-bucket routing table with destinations and saved replies
7. **Moderation rules** — instant-ban list, strike-ladder list, moderation log format
8. **Escalation matrix** — filled four-tier table with real names and contact methods
9. **UGC program** — sourcing cadence, rights workflow, both request templates, tracking sheet columns (link, handle, date, consent type, scope, expiry)
10. **Crisis protocol** — numeric triggers, first-hour checklist, holding statement template, exit criteria, post-crisis review questions
11. **Metrics & review** — weekly KPIs (median first-response time, response rate, sentiment mix) and quarterly review cadence

Every SLA, dollar cap, and threshold appears as a specific number. Every "who" is a named person or role, never "the team."

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every active channel has a first-response SLA in hours, and none exceeds the stated coverage capacity
- [ ] After-hours and weekend gaps have an explicit auto-reply or published-hours mitigation
- [ ] Tone guide translates each brand adjective into a do, a don't, and a sample sentence
- [ ] The reply library contains at least 8 templates and none contains "we apologize for any inconvenience"
- [ ] Complaint vs. troll distinction is defined with a decision rule, not left to judgment
- [ ] Public argument cap (max 2 replies then DM) is stated
- [ ] Moderation rules separate instant-ban offenses from strike-ladder offenses, and brand criticism is explicitly protected from deletion
- [ ] Every ban action requires a logged screenshot, date, and reason
- [ ] Escalation matrix has named owners and notification deadlines in minutes/hours for all four tiers
- [ ] Community manager's authority boundary states the goodwill dollar cap and the forbidden admissions
- [ ] UGC rights require affirmative written consent, with a stricter written grant for paid usage, and credit is documented as not replacing permission
- [ ] Crisis triggers are quantitative (volume multiplier, view/share counts, follower thresholds), not "if things get bad"
- [ ] Crisis protocol includes pausing scheduled posts within 15 minutes and a holding statement deadline of 2 hours
- [ ] Sensitive-disclosure DMs (safety, self-harm) have an immediate human escalation path
- [ ] Document includes weekly metrics and a quarterly review date

## Common mistakes

- **Deleting criticism instead of answering it.** Deleted comments get screenshotted and reposted with "they're censoring customers" — now there are two problems. Delete only rule-breaking content, and keep the rule list public-facing so removals are defensible.
- **Copy-pasting one apology template verbatim across dozens of replies.** Users screenshot the identical replies side by side; it reads as a bot and becomes its own mini-story. Templates are skeletons — the H in HEARD (restating their specific issue) is what makes each reply unique.
- **Reflexively saying "DM us" on every complaint.** Onlookers only see the complaint and a brush-off. Resolve what you can publicly; move to DM only for account details, order numbers, or after two public exchanges.
- **Treating hashtag rights-grabs as ad clearance.** "#Yes[Brand]" consent covers organic reposts; putting that content in paid ads without a written scope-and-duration grant is how brands end up with takedown demands or worse. Paid usage needs the long-form agreement, stored.
- **Letting the brand account be snarky at a customer.** Snark toward competitors' jokes or the brand itself can work; snark toward an individual's complaint turns a 12-like gripe into a quote-post pile-on. Write the snark whitelist down.
- **Leaving scheduled posts running during a crisis.** A chirpy product promo landing mid-backlash is the single most screenshot-able own-goal in this domain. Pause within 15 minutes is a hard rule, so put queue-pause access in the crisis checklist.
- **Vague crisis thresholds.** "If it blows up, escalate" guarantees the community manager waits too long. 3x hourly baseline, 100k views, 500 shares, one journalist — numbers remove the judgment call at the worst moment to be making judgment calls.
- **Feeding trolls to "set the record straight."** Every reply is fuel. One factual correction for the audience's benefit, then hide/mute/ban per policy. The audience, not the troll, is who the single reply is for.
- **Ignoring low-volume channels.** A Google review sitting unanswered for three months is seen by every prospect who searches the brand — worse exposure than a fast-scrolling timeline. The 24–48h/100% rule on ≤3-star reviews exists for this reason.
- **Apologizing in ways that create liability.** "We're sorry our defective product hurt you" is an admission; "we're sorry this happened and we're looking into it" is empathy. The SOP must draw this line so the community manager doesn't improvise it during a Tier 3 incident.
- **No moderation log.** The first time a banned user posts "banned for asking a question!", the log with the screenshot of their slur is the entire defense. No log means relitigating every ban in public from memory.

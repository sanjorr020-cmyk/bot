# Webinar Scripts

Write webinars that people show up to, stay for, and buy from. A webinar is not a lecture with a sales pitch stapled on — it is a single persuasive arc where the registration promise, the teaching, the pitch, and the follow-up all argue the same thesis. This skill covers the full funnel: registration page, run-of-show script with interaction points, the teach-to-pitch transition, offer presentation, Q&A, and the replay/follow-up sequence.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants:
- A full webinar script (live, automated/evergreen, or hybrid)
- A registration/signup page for a webinar or masterclass
- A run-of-show or minute-by-minute agenda
- The pitch segment, offer stack, or teach-to-sell transition
- Q&A planning, seeded questions, or objection handling inside the webinar
- Replay strategy and post-webinar email follow-up

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Standalone sales pages or opt-in pages unrelated to a live event — use **landing-page-cro**
- Recorded YouTube content with no live pitch — use **youtube-scripts**
- 1:1 sales conversations — use **sales-call-scripts** and **objection-handling**
- General email campaigns not tied to a webinar — use **email-marketing** or **follow-up-sequences**
- Promoting the webinar with paid traffic — use **google-ads**, **meta-ads**, or **ad-copywriting** for the ad creative (this skill supplies the registration page they land on)
- Turning the finished webinar into clips and posts afterward — use **content-repurposing**

## Inputs to gather

Ask the user for these. Where they don't know, apply the default and state the assumption in your output.

1. **Offer**: What is being sold at the end? Price, format (course, service, software, coaching), guarantee. If nothing is sold, this is a lead-gen or authority webinar — the "pitch" becomes a call booking or trial signup, and the same structure applies.
2. **Audience**: Who registers, what they've tried already, and their #1 painful symptom. If unknown, draft a one-line persona from the offer and flag it (or point to **customer-personas** for deeper work).
3. **The one big promise**: The single transformation the webinar teaches. Default: derive it from the offer's core outcome.
4. **Format**: Live, automated/evergreen, or live-then-replay. Default: live with a 72-hour replay window.
5. **Length**: Default 60 minutes (40 teach / 15 pitch / 5+ Q&A). Accept 45 or 90; push back on anything over 90 minutes.
6. **Host credibility assets**: Origin story, results, client outcomes with numbers. If thin, plan to borrow proof (case studies, data, named methods).
7. **Platform**: Zoom Webinars, Zoom Meetings, Demio, WebinarJam, Livestorm, StreamYard, etc. This determines available interaction tools (polls, chat, hand-raise, CTA buttons).
8. **Traffic source**: Ads, email list, partners/affiliates. Cold traffic needs more credibility-building early; warm lists tolerate a faster start.
9. **Deadline mechanics**: What expires and when (bonus, price, cart close). Default: fast-action bonus expires when the webinar ends; full offer closes 72 hours after.

## Process

1. **Define the Big Domino.** Write one sentence: "If I can get them to believe that [new belief about the method], then all objections collapse and buying [offer] is the obvious next step." Every segment must push this one belief. If the user's outline teaches five unrelated tips, restructure — a webinar sells one belief, not a curriculum.

2. **Write the registration page promise.** Structure: headline (specific outcome + timeframe or mechanism, under 15 words), subhead naming the audience, 3–5 "what you'll learn" bullets (each a curiosity-gapped specific: "The 3-line email that revived 214 dead leads — and why it fails if you send it on Monday"), host credibility strip, date/time with timezone, and a form asking for email only (each extra field cuts conversion; name+email is the max). Benchmark: 25–45% of page visitors register from warm traffic, 15–30% from cold ads. Add "seats limited" only if attendance is genuinely capped by the platform tier.

3. **Design the show-up sequence** (registration → live). Show-up rates run 35–50% for warm lists, 20–35% for cold ads; the sequence is what moves the number:
   - Instantly: confirmation email with calendar file (.ics), what to bring, and a 2–3 minute "pre-training" asset that pre-sells the Big Domino
   - 24 hours before: reminder + one open loop ("On the call I'll show you the exact dashboard from the $2M case study")
   - Morning of, 1 hour before, and 15 minutes before: short reminders, link-first
   - Add SMS reminders if the platform supports it (lifts show-up 5–15 points)

4. **Build the run-of-show skeleton** before writing any prose. Allocate minutes to each block (use the 60-minute template below), place an interaction point every 8–10 minutes, and mark the exact minute of the pitch transition. Attendance decays steadily after minute 40 — the pitch must start while 60%+ of peak attendees are still on.

5. **Script the opening (minutes 0–7).** Cover: promise restated ("In the next 55 minutes you'll learn..."), a reason to stay to the end (announce a specific bonus or resource delivered at the end — this is your retention anchor), quick host credibility (90 seconds max, results not biography), and the first poll within 5 minutes ("Where are you right now: A / B / C?") — early interaction trains attendees to participate and gives you segment data to reference later.

6. **Script the teaching segments using "what and why, not how."** Teach the framework, the belief shifts, and proof that the method works — the step-by-step implementation is what the offer delivers. Structure the core as three segments, each killing one false belief:
   - Segment 1 attacks the **vehicle** belief ("the old way doesn't work anymore; here's the new mechanism")
   - Segment 2 attacks the **internal** belief ("you don't need [talent/tech skill/experience] because...")
   - Segment 3 attacks the **external** belief ("you don't need [money/time/audience] because...")
   Each segment = claim → story or case study with numbers → the reframed belief → micro-recap. Insert an interaction point per segment: a poll, a "type YES in chat if this is you," or a "give me a 1–10 in chat." Genuine teaching still matters — each segment should contain at least one insight an attendee could act on alone; that's what earns trust for the pitch.

7. **Script the transition (the highest-stakes 60 seconds).** Never say "let me switch to my sales pitch" and never apologize. Bridge with a permission-based recap: summarize the three shifts, name the gap ("you now know what to do — the difference between knowing and doing is speed and support"), then ask: "Is it okay if I take 10 minutes to show you what working together looks like? Type YES if you want the details." Wait for the chat to respond, then proceed. A worked example is in the templates section.

8. **Script the offer stack.** Present the offer as components with individual value framing: core program → each bonus (each bonus should neutralize a specific objection: a template pack kills "I don't have time," a community kills "I'll get stuck alone") → total value → actual price → price justification (cost of the alternative or of inaction) → risk reversal (guarantee, stated plainly) → deadline/scarcity (only real ones) → CTA with exact instructions ("Click the button below the video / the link in chat — it's [URL]"). Repeat the CTA at least 3 times during the close. Show the checkout page on screen so buyers know what to expect.

9. **Script the Q&A as a second close, not an afterthought.** Prepare 5–7 seeded questions in advance covering the top objections (price, time, "will it work for my niche," "what if I'm a beginner," guarantee details). Answer live questions in the pattern: validate → answer → tie back to the offer ("...and that's exactly what Module 3 handles"). Keep the offer/CTA on screen for the entire Q&A. Re-state the deadline every 3–4 questions. End with a final push: last recap of stack + deadline + one-sentence identity close ("The people who get results are the ones who decide fast — I'd love for that to be you").

10. **Plan the replay and follow-up sequence.** Only 40–60% of buyers purchase on the live call; the rest convert in follow-up, so this is not optional:
    - T+1 hour: replay link + recap of offer + deadline restated
    - T+24h: case study email (one detailed story matching the primary persona)
    - T+48h: objection email (FAQ format answering the top 3 hesitations — coordinate with **objection-handling**)
    - T+66h: "closing tonight" logic email (why the deadline exists)
    - T+71h: final notice, short, link-only
    - Kill the replay when the cart closes. An evergreen replay with a fake countdown destroys trust and often violates platform ad policies.
    Hand detailed sequence copy to **follow-up-sequences** if the user wants deeper email work; this skill defines the strategy and drafts the skeleton.

11. **Adapt for automated/evergreen webinars** if applicable: remove all "live" language ("tonight," "I see Sarah in the chat"), replace live polls with on-screen self-assessment prompts, use a genuine evergreen deadline (per-registrant countdown enforced by the cart, e.g. Deadline Funnel-style), and shorten to 45–60 minutes since drop-off is steeper on replays.

12. **Deliver and QA.** Assemble the full deliverable (see Output format), run the Quality checklist, and flag any missing proof elements the user must supply (specific numbers, client names, screenshots).

## Frameworks & templates

### Framework: Perfect Webinar arc (Brunson-style, adapted)

1. **Hook + promise** (min 0–7): why stay to the end
2. **Origin story** (min 7–12): host's before/after establishing the mechanism was discovered, not invented
3. **Three Secrets** (min 12–40): kill vehicle, internal, external false beliefs — one segment each
4. **Transition** (min 40–41): permission-based bridge
5. **Stack + close** (min 41–52): offer components, value framing, price, guarantee, deadline, CTA x3
6. **Q&A close** (min 52–60+): seeded questions, offer on screen throughout

### Framework: "Teach the what/why, sell the how"

For every teaching point, ask: does this build belief that the method works (keep, teach fully) or is it implementation detail (summarize, and name the module of the offer that delivers it)? A webinar that teaches all the "how" gets thank-yous and no sales; one that teaches no substance gets refund-level distrust. Target: 3 genuinely usable takeaways across the teaching block.

### Template: registration page (fill in the blanks)

```
HEADLINE: How to [specific outcome] in [timeframe] without [pain/objection]
SUBHEAD: A free live training for [audience] on [date] at [time + timezone]

ON THIS TRAINING YOU'LL LEARN:
• The [named method] that [result] — even if [common disqualifier]
• Why [common practice] is quietly killing your [metric], and what to do instead
• The exact [asset: template/checklist/dashboard] we used to [specific numeric result]
• [Curiosity bullet: number + unexpected mechanism]

HOSTED BY: [Name], [one-line credibility: result, not title]
[BUTTON: Save My Seat →]   (form: email only)
Bonus for attending live: [named bonus delivered at the end]
```

### Template: 60-minute run-of-show

| Min | Segment | Interaction |
|-----|---------|-------------|
| 0–3 | Welcome, promise, "stay to the end" bonus announce | "Type your city in chat" |
| 3–7 | Agenda + host credibility (90 sec) | Poll #1: current state A/B/C |
| 7–12 | Origin story → Big Domino statement | — |
| 12–21 | Secret 1 (vehicle belief) + case study | "Type YES if you've tried [old way]" |
| 21–30 | Secret 2 (internal belief) + case study | Poll #2: biggest obstacle |
| 30–39 | Secret 3 (external belief) + case study | "1–10 in chat: how confident now?" |
| 39–40 | Recap of 3 shifts + deliver promised bonus | — |
| 40–41 | Transition (permission bridge) | "Type YES for details" |
| 41–50 | Offer stack → price → guarantee → deadline | CTA link in chat, x3 |
| 50–52 | Fast-action bonus (expires at end of call) | Show checkout on screen |
| 52–60 | Q&A (seeded + live), offer on screen | Final CTA + deadline restate |

### Worked example: the transition script

> "So let's recap. You've seen that [old way] stopped working because [reason] — and that [new mechanism] is what's driving results like [case study number]. You've seen you don't need [feared requirement], because [reframe]. So here's the honest truth: you could take everything from today and do it yourself, and some of you will. But most people tell me the hard part isn't knowing what to do — it's [speed/accountability/avoiding the 6 months of trial and error]. So with your permission, I'd like to take ten minutes and show you the fastest way I know to get this done. Type YES in the chat if you want to see it."
>
> *(Wait 5–10 seconds. Read 2–3 names aloud: "Great — Maria, Dev, Sam...")* "Okay — here's exactly what it looks like."

### Worked example: offer stack skeleton

> "Here's everything you get. The core program: [name] — [#] modules that take you from [A] to [B] ($X value). Bonus 1: [template pack] so you never start from a blank page ($X value) — this one exists because the #1 thing people tell me is 'I don't have time.' Bonus 2: [community/office hours] so you're never stuck alone ($X value). Bonus 3: [audit/review] ($X value). Total value: $[sum]. You're not paying that. Not even half. Today it's $[price] — less than [relatable comparison]. And it's backed by [guarantee, stated in one plain sentence]. The link is in the chat: [URL]. And if you enroll before we close the call tonight, you also get [fast-action bonus] — that one disappears at the top of the hour."

## Output format

Deliver a single markdown document titled with the webinar name, containing in order:

1. **Strategy summary** — Big Domino statement, audience, offer, format, deadline mechanics (5–8 lines)
2. **Registration page copy** — full copy using the template structure
3. **Show-up sequence** — email subjects + 2–3 line summaries for each touch, with send timing
4. **Run-of-show table** — minute-by-minute with interaction points marked
5. **Full script** — word-for-word for the opening, transitions, pitch, and close; bulleted talking points (not full prose) for teaching segments so the host sounds natural; interaction cues in **[BRACKETS]** (e.g., **[LAUNCH POLL 2]**, **[PASTE LINK IN CHAT]**)
6. **Q&A kit** — 5–7 seeded questions with scripted answers, plus the objection→answer→offer-tieback pattern
7. **Replay/follow-up plan** — timeline table with each email's job, subject line draft, and the cart-close mechanics
8. **Assumptions & gaps** — every default applied and every proof element the user still needs to supply

## Quality checklist

Verify each before delivering; fix any failure:

- [ ] One Big Domino sentence exists and every teaching segment maps to it
- [ ] Registration headline is under 15 words and contains a specific outcome, not a topic
- [ ] Every "you'll learn" bullet has a specific (number, named asset, or named mechanism) — zero generic bullets
- [ ] Registration form asks for email only (or name + email, with rationale)
- [ ] Show-up sequence has at least 4 touches, including one within 15 minutes of start
- [ ] An interaction point appears at least every 10 minutes of the run-of-show
- [ ] Pitch begins by minute 42 of a 60-minute webinar (70% mark or earlier)
- [ ] The transition asks permission and never apologizes for selling
- [ ] Each bonus in the stack is mapped to a specific objection it neutralizes
- [ ] Price appears only after the full stack and total value framing
- [ ] CTA with exact URL/instruction appears at least 3 times in the close
- [ ] Q&A includes 5+ seeded questions covering price, time, and "will it work for me"
- [ ] Every scarcity/deadline claim is mechanically true (cart actually closes, bonus actually expires)
- [ ] Follow-up sequence has 4+ emails ending at cart close, and the replay dies with the cart
- [ ] Teaching contains at least 3 takeaways an attendee could act on without buying

## Common mistakes

- **Teaching the full "how."** Attendees leave satisfied and buy nothing. Teach frameworks and proof; the implementation is the product.
- **The apologetic pivot.** "Sorry, I have to do a quick pitch now..." signals even the host doesn't believe in the offer. Selling the solution is a service to people who want help — script it that way.
- **Front-loading credentials.** Five minutes of biography before any value tanks retention in the first 10 minutes, exactly when drop-off is steepest. 90 seconds of results-based credibility, then teach.
- **Pitching at minute 55 of 60.** By then half the peak audience is gone. Start the pitch at the 65–70% mark of total runtime.
- **Interaction as gimmick.** Polls that don't feed the script ("What's your favorite season?") train people to ignore prompts. Every poll should either segment the audience for a later callback or force a micro-commitment toward the Big Domino.
- **Fake scarcity on evergreen webinars.** A countdown that resets on refresh gets noticed, screenshotted, and kills the brand. Use per-registrant deadlines enforced at the cart, or don't use deadlines.
- **One follow-up email.** 40–60% of revenue arrives after the live call. Skipping the 72-hour sequence forfeits nearly half the campaign.
- **Q&A as a free-for-all.** Unseeded Q&A drifts into edge cases and implementation minutiae that unsell the room. Seed it, keep the offer on screen, and tie every answer back.
- **A registration promise the webinar doesn't pay off.** If the page promises "the 3-step system" and the webinar gates all three steps behind the offer, refund requests and chat hostility follow. The promised takeaway must be genuinely delivered before the pitch.
- **Reading dense slides aloud.** Slides are cue cards for the audience, not the script. Max ~10 words per slide in pitch sections; put the prose in the speaker notes.

# X/Twitter Content

Write X/Twitter content that earns reach through the platform's actual mechanics: a hook that stops the scroll in under 2 seconds, structure that keeps readers moving, and engagement signals (replies, bookmarks, profile visits) that the algorithm amplifies. This skill covers single posts, threads, long-form posts, reply strategy, and reading X analytics to iterate — always in the user's voice, never in generic "content creator" voice.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Write or edit a single X post, a thread, or a long-form (X Premium) post
- Turn a blog post, launch, podcast, or idea into X-native content
- Build a reply/engagement routine to grow reach or followers
- Decide the right format (single post vs. thread vs. long-form vs. quote post)
- Diagnose why posts are underperforming using X analytics

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Overall channel strategy, platform mix, or posting cadence across networks → `social-media-strategy`
- Scheduling posts into a weekly/monthly plan → `social-content-calendar`
- LinkedIn posts (different tone, length, and algorithm) → `linkedin-content`
- Adapting one asset across many platforms → `content-repurposing`
- Running the account's DMs and community replies as a support/community function → `community-management`
- Paid promotion of posts → `meta-ads` / `ad-copywriting`
- Creator partnerships and sponsored posts → `influencer-marketing`

## Inputs to gather

Ask for these up front. If the user doesn't know, use the defaults.

1. **Goal of this post/account** — reach, followers, email signups, product awareness, authority. *Default: grow followers + authority.*
2. **Audience** — who follows them or who they want to reach (job title, interest, level). *Default: practitioners in the user's field, not total beginners.*
3. **Topic and raw material** — the idea, story, data, or source content. If they have nothing, ask for one strong opinion or lesson from their work this month.
4. **Voice sample** — 2-3 of their past posts, or ask: lowercase-casual, plain-professional, or punchy-contrarian? *Default: plain-professional, first person, no hashtags, no emojis unless their samples use them.*
5. **Account status** — Premium or free (free = 280-character cap, no long-form), current follower count, and typical impressions per post. *Default: free account, under 5,000 followers.*
6. **CTA constraints** — anything to plug (newsletter, product, waitlist)? *Default: soft CTA (follow) only; hard CTAs at most 1 in 5 posts.*
7. **Analytics access** — if diagnosing performance, ask for impressions, likes, replies, bookmarks, profile visits, and new follows for their last 10-20 posts.

## Process

1. **Pick the format for the job.** Decide before writing:
   - **Single post** (default): one idea, one insight, one opinion. Under 280 characters. Best for frequency and testing what resonates.
   - **Thread**: a process, story, list, or breakdown that genuinely needs 5-12 steps. Threads must earn every additional post; a 4-post thread is usually a bloated single post.
   - **Long-form post** (Premium, up to 25,000 chars): beats a thread when the content is one continuous narrative or essay readers will bookmark — deep tutorials, personal stories, annual reviews. Threads beat long-form when each unit stands alone (tips, examples, screenshots) because each post is a separate re-engagement hook. Long-form also can't be unbundled by readers skimming — expect fewer but deeper engagements.
   - **Quote post**: when reacting to a live conversation; adds your take to borrowed reach.

2. **Write the hook first, and write 5-10 of them.** The first line decides 80% of performance. A hook must do one of: make a specific claim, open a curiosity gap, name the reader's exact pain, or lead with a surprising number. Kill any hook that starts with throat-clearing ("I've been thinking about...", "Thread on...", "So..."). Test each hook against: "Would a stranger stop scrolling for this?" Pick the strongest; keep the runners-up for future posts.

3. **Draft the body.**
   - *Single post*: hook line, then 1-3 short lines of payoff. Line breaks between every sentence or two. One idea only — if a second idea appears, cut it into its own post.
   - *Thread*: follow the Hook → Escalation → Payoff → CTA architecture (see Frameworks). Every post must stand alone when screenshotted, stay under 280 characters (even on Premium — short posts read better in threads), and end with a reason to read the next one.
   - *Long-form*: strong first 280 characters (that's all that shows before "Show more"), then subheads or line-broken sections every 3-5 sentences, one concrete example per section.

4. **Optimize for the algorithm's ranking signals.** In rough order of weight: replies > reposts > profile-visit-then-follow > bookmarks > likes > dwell time. Practical implications:
   - End with something reply-able: a question, a strong claim people will push back on, or "What did I miss?"
   - Put external links in a reply, not the main post — posts with links get measurably suppressed in the For You feed.
   - Make posts bookmark-worthy (checklists, numbers, templates) — bookmarks are a strong quality signal.
   - Native images/screenshots help dwell time; use 16:9 (1200x675) or 1:1 (1080x1080), max 4 images, always add alt text.

5. **Set the engagement plan for the first hour.** The algorithm tests posts on a small audience in the first 30-60 minutes; early engagement decides distribution. Tell the user to:
   - Post when their audience is online (start with 8-11am in their audience's dominant timezone on weekdays; verify against their own analytics after 2 weeks).
   - Reply to every comment in the first 60 minutes — each reply is a new ranking event and doubles the post's conversation depth.
   - Never post and vanish; a post with 0 author replies caps its own reach.

6. **Build the reply-strategy routine (for account growth).** Replies to bigger accounts are the highest-leverage growth tactic under 10,000 followers:
   - Pick 10-20 accounts in the niche with 10x-100x the user's following.
   - Reply within 15-30 minutes of their posts (early replies get the most impressions from that post's audience).
   - Every reply must add value: a supporting example, a respectful counterpoint, a relevant number, or a sharp one-liner. Never "Great post!" or emoji-only replies.
   - Target 10-20 quality replies per day; expect replies to drive 2-5x the profile visits of the user's own posts at small follower counts.

7. **Read analytics and iterate (every 2-4 weeks).** Pull the last 10-20 posts and compute:
   - **Engagement rate** = total engagements / impressions. Benchmarks: under 0.5% weak, 0.5-1% typical, 1-3% good, 3%+ excellent for accounts under 50k followers.
   - **Bookmark rate** (bookmarks / impressions): 0.2%+ signals genuinely useful content — make more of that topic/format.
   - **Follow conversion** (new follows / profile visits): under 5% means the profile (bio, pinned post) is leaking; fix the profile before writing more.
   - **Reply count vs. likes**: high likes + low replies = agreeable but forgettable; add sharper opinions.
   Identify the top 3 posts by engagement rate, name what they share (topic, format, hook type), and schedule 2-3 more posts in that pattern. Kill the bottom pattern entirely.

8. **Deliver and package.** Present the final content per the Output format, including posting-time recommendation, first-hour engagement plan, and (for threads) the link-in-reply if there's a CTA link.

## Frameworks & templates

### Single-post formulas (pick one per post)

1. **Contrarian take**: `Everyone says [common advice]. / [Blunt disagreement]. / [Reason + evidence in 1-2 lines]. / [What to do instead].`
2. **Hard-won lesson**: `[Number] [years/projects/failures] taught me one thing about [topic]: / [The lesson, stated plainly]. / [One concrete example].`
3. **Stat + interpretation**: `[Surprising number with source context]. / Here's what that actually means: [insight the number implies].`
4. **Before/after**: `[Timeframe] ago: [specific bad state]. / Today: [specific good state]. / The difference: [one change].`
5. **Mini-list**: `[N] [things] that [outcome]: / 1. ... / 2. ... / 3. ...` — keep to 3-5 items; more belongs in a thread.

**Worked example (contrarian take, 243 chars):**
> "Post every day" is the worst advice for small accounts.
>
> Nobody sees your posts yet. Volume without distribution is shouting into a canyon.
>
> Spend 80% of your time replying to accounts your audience already follows. Post 3x a week.

### Thread architecture: Hook → Escalation → Payoff → CTA

- **Post 1 — Hook**: Big claim or promise + credibility + implicit table of contents. No hashtags, no links. End with a colon or a reason to keep reading. This post gets 10-50x the impressions of the rest — it must work standalone.
- **Posts 2 to N-1 — Escalation**: One point per post, ordered so value rises (put the second-best point second, best point second-to-last). Each post: point stated in line 1, then example/number/screenshot. Add a soft open loop every 3-4 posts ("The next one is the mistake everyone makes:").
- **Post N-1 — Payoff**: The best insight, or a summary post ("TL;DR: 1)... 2)... 3)...") that people will bookmark and repost.
- **Post N — CTA**: One ask only. Format: `If this was useful: / 1. Follow @handle for [specific topic] / 2. Repost the first post to share it` — then put any external link in a reply to this post. Ideal thread length: 5-9 posts. Over 12, cut or split into two threads.

**Fill-in thread template (7 posts):**
```
1/ [Result or claim with a number]. Here's the exact [process/playbook], step by step:
2/ Step 1: [action]. [Why it matters in one line]. [Concrete example or number].
3/ Step 2: [action]. [Common way people get this wrong].
4/ Step 3: [action]. [Screenshot or specific example]. Most people stop here. The next step is where the results come from:
5/ Step 4: [action]. [The non-obvious detail that makes it work].
6/ TL;DR: 1) [step] 2) [step] 3) [step] 4) [step]. Bookmark this for [use case].
7/ If this helped: follow @[handle] — I post [topic] every [cadence]. Repost the first post to help someone else find it.
```

**Worked thread example (6 posts, SaaS founder audience):**
```
1/ We cut churn from 6.1% to 3.4% monthly in one quarter.

No new features. No price change. Four changes to onboarding.

Here's the exact playbook:

2/ Change 1: We defined the "activated" moment with data, not opinion.

Users who created 3+ projects in week 1 retained at 92%. Everyone else: 41%.

That number became the only onboarding goal.

3/ Change 2: We cut the welcome tour from 9 steps to 3.

Completion went from 22% to 71%.

Every step you add to onboarding is a door people leave through.

4/ Change 3: Empty states became starting points.

Instead of a blank dashboard: one pre-built sample project users could edit.

Time-to-first-project dropped from 2 days to 11 minutes. This next one mattered most:

5/ Change 4: A founder email at day 3 — plain text, one question:
"What were you hoping [product] would do that it hasn't yet?"

31% reply rate. Every reply was a churn reason we could fix before the cancel.

6/ TL;DR — the 4 changes:
1. Define activation with retention data
2. Cut the tour to 3 steps
3. Ship pre-filled empty states
4. Day-3 plain-text founder email

Bookmark this before your next onboarding sprint. I post one retention breakdown like this every week — follow @handle for the next one.
```
Why it works: numeric hook with a "no X, no Y" tension line; one change per post, each with a before/after number; open loop at the end of post 4; bookmark-able TL;DR merged with a single CTA.

### Hook patterns (for post 1 or single posts)

- **Number + outcome**: "I grew from 0 to 12,000 followers in 9 months with one repeatable system:"
- **Costly mistake**: "This one pricing mistake cost us $40k. Don't repeat it:"
- **Forbidden knowledge**: "What nobody tells you about [topic]:"
- **Speed/leverage**: "[Impressive result] in [short time], without [expected cost]:"
- **Enemy**: "[Common practice] is quietly killing your [outcome]. Here's what to do instead:"

### Reply templates (growth routine)

- **Add-a-number**: `This matches what I've seen — [specific stat or result from own experience]. The part most people miss is [detail].`
- **Respectful counter**: `Mostly agree, but [specific exception]. In [context], [alternative] works better because [reason].`
- **Extend the idea**: `Great frame. It also applies to [adjacent domain]: [one-line example].`

### Thread vs. long-form decision table

| Signal | Choose thread | Choose long-form (Premium) |
|---|---|---|
| Content shape | Discrete tips/steps/examples | One continuous narrative or argument |
| Visuals | Screenshot or image per point | Few or no images |
| Reader behavior wanted | Repost individual posts, quote single points | Bookmark and read later |
| Account size | Any | Works best 10k+ followers (long-form leans on existing trust) |
| Skimmability | Each post is a skim unit | Reader must commit; add subheads every 3-5 sentences |
| Fallback | If under 5 real points, collapse to a single post | If it reads like 10 standalone tips, unbundle into a thread |

### Analytics review — worked example

Given a user's last 12 posts, produce a table like this and name the pattern:

| Post (hook topic) | Format | Impressions | Eng. rate | Bookmarks | New follows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pricing mistake story | Single | 18,400 | 3.1% | 61 | 24 |
| Onboarding thread | Thread | 41,200 | 2.4% | 210 | 57 |
| "We're hiring" | Single | 1,100 | 0.4% | 0 | 0 |
| Industry hot take | Single | 22,800 | 1.9% | 12 | 31 |

Reading: numbered playbooks with real numbers (rows 1-2) drive bookmarks and follows — double down with 2-3 more; announcements (row 3) earn nothing organically — move them to a reply under a high-performer or cut them; hot takes (row 4) drive follows but few bookmarks — keep at ~1/week for reach, not more.

### Platform specs quick reference

- Post length: 280 chars (free); Premium up to 25,000 but only first ~280 show pre-"Show more"
- Images: 16:9 (1200x675) or 1:1; max 4 per post; alt text up to 1,000 chars
- Video: up to 2:20 free, longer on Premium; native video outperforms YouTube links
- Hashtags: 0-1 max; hashtags read as spam on modern X and add no reach
- Cadence baseline: 1-2 posts/day + 10-20 replies/day; 1-2 threads/week

## Output format

Deliver in this structure (markdown):

1. **Format decision** — one line: chosen format and why (skip if the user specified).
2. **The content** — each post in its own blockquote, numbered for threads (1/, 2/, ...), with character count noted next to any post near the 280 limit. For long-form: full text with the first-280-characters hook marked.
3. **Alternate hooks** — 2-3 runner-up hooks the user can test later.
4. **Posting plan** — recommended day/time (with timezone), link-in-reply text if applicable, and the first-hour engagement checklist (reply to all comments, pin if it takes off).
5. **If analytics were reviewed**: a short table of the last posts with engagement rate and bookmark rate, the top pattern to double down on, the bottom pattern to drop, and 3 next-post ideas in the winning pattern.

## Quality checklist

Verify every deliverable against these before presenting:

1. Hook line survives the stranger test: a non-follower would stop scrolling (specific claim, number, or curiosity gap — no throat-clearing).
2. Every free-account post is ≤ 280 characters (count them; don't eyeball).
3. No external links in the main post or thread body — links live in a reply.
4. Zero hashtags, or one with a documented reason (event/community tag).
5. One idea per single post; one point per thread post.
6. Thread is 5-12 posts, each readable as a standalone screenshot.
7. Thread includes a bookmark-able summary post (TL;DR or checklist).
8. Exactly one CTA, and it appears only in the final post.
9. Line breaks every 1-2 sentences; no paragraph over 3 lines on mobile width.
10. Voice matches the user's samples (spot-check: could their followers tell it wasn't them?).
11. Every claim has a number, example, or story attached — no unsupported platitudes.
12. Post ends with something reply-able (question, strong claim, or invitation to disagree).
13. Posting time and first-hour engagement plan are included.
14. If analytics were provided: recommendations cite the user's actual numbers, not generic benchmarks alone.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing the thread before the hook.** If post 1 can't earn a stop, posts 2-9 never get seen. Hooks first, always, in multiples.
- **"Thread 🧵 (1/12)" as the opening.** Announcing a thread is not a hook. The thread count belongs at the end of post 1, if anywhere.
- **Links in the main post.** External links suppress For You distribution. "Link in the replies" isn't superstition; it's how reach-conscious accounts ship CTAs.
- **Threading what should be a single post.** Three thin points padded to 6 posts trains followers to skip the user's threads. If the TL;DR fits in 280 characters, post the TL;DR.
- **Engagement-bait questions with no substance** ("What's your favorite productivity tool?"). The algorithm rewards replies, but hollow prompts attract hollow repliers and zero follows.
- **Posting and vanishing.** Skipping the first-hour reply window wastes the post's algorithmic test period — the single most common reason decent content dies at 200 impressions.
- **Copying big-account style at small-account scale.** One-word posts and inside jokes work at 500k followers because distribution is guaranteed. Under 10k, every post must carry standalone value.
- **Optimizing for likes instead of bookmarks and follows.** Likes are the weakest signal. A post with 40 bookmarks and 15 likes beat a post with 200 likes and 2 bookmarks — the first one gets recommended and converts followers.
- **Hard-selling in every post.** CTAs above roughly 1 in 5 posts read as an ad account; reach and follow conversion both decay. Earn with 4, ask with 1.
- **Ignoring reply strategy under 10k followers.** Small accounts that only broadcast grow at a crawl; borrowed audiences via early, valuable replies are the fastest path to profile visits.
- **Rewriting LinkedIn posts verbatim.** X punishes corporate tone, long windups, and "Agree?" endings. Repurpose the idea, not the text — see `content-repurposing` for the workflow.

# YouTube Scripts

Write long-form YouTube scripts (roughly 6–20 minutes) that get clicked and get watched. The core discipline: packaging (title + thumbnail concept) is decided BEFORE a single script line is written, because the video must deliver on the exact promise that earns the click. Every script produced with this skill includes an engineered hook, a retention-driven structure with open loops and pattern interrupts, inline visual/B-roll direction, and a deliberate CTA and end-screen plan.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Script a long-form YouTube video (talking-head, tutorial, video essay, listicle, case study, vlog with narration)
- Fix a video that gets views but poor watch time, or impressions but poor CTR
- Generate title/thumbnail concepts and a matching script outline
- Rewrite a weak intro/hook or restructure a rambling draft
- Turn existing material (blog post, podcast episode, webinar) into a YouTube script

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Shorts, Reels, or TikTok scripts under ~90 seconds → use **short-form-video**
- Filming, editing, gear, or production logistics → use **video-production**
- Webinar or live-presentation scripts → use **webinar-scripts**
- Podcast episode planning or show notes → use **podcast-production**
- Chopping a finished video into clips for other platforms → use **content-repurposing**
- Deciding what topics the channel should cover strategically → use **content-strategy** or **keyword-research**

## Inputs to gather

Ask the user for these. Where they don't know, apply the stated default and say you did.

1. **Topic and core claim.** What is the video about, and what is the one thing a viewer walks away with? If the user gives a vague topic ("productivity"), push for a specific angle ("why time-blocking fails for creative work").
2. **Channel context.** Niche, subscriber range, and 1–2 example videos that performed well. Default: assume a small-to-mid channel (<50k subs) where packaging matters more than brand loyalty.
3. **Audience.** Who watches and what they already know. Default: interested beginners; explain jargon on first use. (For deep persona work, point to **customer-personas**.)
4. **Format.** Talking-head, tutorial/screen-share, listicle, video essay, documentary-style. Default: talking-head with B-roll.
5. **Target length.** Default: 8–12 minutes. Under 8 minutes forfeits mid-roll ad slots; over 15 requires strong retention mechanics to justify.
6. **Goal of the video.** Subscribers, watch time, product/lead-gen, authority. Default: watch time + subscribers. The CTA strategy in step 8 depends on this.
7. **Speaking pace.** Default: 140 words per minute. A 10-minute video is ~1,400 words of spoken script (visual-heavy tutorials run shorter; fast-cut essays run to 160 wpm).
8. **Constraints.** Words/claims to avoid, sponsor segment (get its required talking points and target length — 45–60 seconds is standard), existing intro/outro assets.

## Process

1. **Nail the packaging first.** Before any scripting, produce 5–8 title options and 2–3 thumbnail concepts:
   - Titles: front-load the payload in the first 45–55 characters (YouTube truncates around 55–70 characters depending on surface; hard cap is 100). Write for curiosity + specificity, not keywords alone.
   - Thumbnail concepts: describe each as [subject/face + emotion] + [1 focal object or visual contrast] + [≤4 words of text that do NOT repeat the title]. Spec: 1280×720, 16:9, must read at 120px wide.
   - **Decision point:** title and thumbnail must make ONE combined promise. If a title needs the thumbnail's text to make sense (or vice versa), they're doing the same job — rework so they complement (title = claim, thumbnail = intrigue/stakes).
   - Present the options, recommend one pairing with a one-line rationale, and get the user's pick before proceeding. Benchmark: a browse CTR of 4–6% is solid; under 2–3% means packaging is the problem regardless of script quality.

2. **Write the promise statement.** One sentence: "By the end of this video, the viewer will ___." Every section of the script must serve this. If a planned section doesn't, cut it or make it its own video.

3. **Outline the retention skeleton.** Map the video into 4–7 chapters before writing prose. For each chapter note: the sub-payoff it delivers, the open loop it plants or closes, and its target timestamp. Sequence value so the strongest non-finale material lands in minutes 1–3 (where most drop-off happens) and the single best payoff is held for the final third (announced early — see open loops below).

4. **Script the first 30 seconds last-ish but design it now.** Choose a hook pattern (see Frameworks) and draft it to do four jobs in order:
   - 0–5s: confirm the click — visually and verbally restate the title/thumbnail promise.
   - 5–15s: raise stakes or curiosity — why this matters or what's surprising.
   - 15–25s: establish credibility or proof in one line (result, credential, or demonstration — not a bio).
   - 25–30s: signpost the plan and plant the main open loop ("...and the third method is the one that actually worked — that's at the end").
   Never start with "Hey guys, welcome back to the channel" or channel branding. Benchmark: retaining 65–70%+ of viewers at the 30-second mark is healthy; under 50% means the hook is broken.

5. **Write the body chapter by chapter.** For each chapter:
   - Open with a mini-hook (question, bold claim, or "here's where it gets weird") — never "Okay, so next..."
   - Deliver the sub-payoff with one concrete example, number, or demonstration per claim.
   - Close by teasing the next chapter (a "but" or "however" bridge: "That worked — until it didn't.")
   - Insert a pattern interrupt every 30–60 seconds of runtime: location/angle change, on-screen graphic, B-roll sequence, story beat, tone shift, or prop. Mark each one in the visual column.
   - Keep sentences spoken-word short (aim ≤20 words); read the script aloud mentally — if a sentence needs a breath mid-way, split it.

6. **Add visual direction inline.** Every 2–4 script lines, add a bracketed direction: `[B-ROLL: hands typing, slow push-in]`, `[GFX: retention graph, arrow at 0:30 cliff]`, `[SCREEN: settings page, zoom to toggle]`, `[CUT: jump cut, step closer to camera]`. The editor should be able to build a shot list from the script alone. Tutorials need a visual note on nearly every line; essays every 2–4 lines.

7. **Place the sponsor/midroll (if any).** Put sponsor reads after the first major payoff, typically 25–40% into the runtime — never inside the first 90 seconds. Bridge into it with a topical segue and time it at 45–60 seconds.

8. **Script the CTA and end screen.**
   - Subscribe ask: ONE, placed right after a moment of delivered value (usually 40–60% in), tied to a reason ("I test one of these every week — subscribe if you want the results"). Do not open the video with it.
   - Ending: no windup ("so yeah, that's basically it..."). Land the final payoff, give a one-sentence conclusion, then bridge directly into the next-video pitch. The last 20 seconds must be watchable while end-screen elements (last 5–20 seconds of the video) overlay — talking head or neutral B-roll, no critical on-screen info in the end-screen zones.
   - **Decision point by goal:** subscribers → end-screen video + subscribe element; product/lead-gen → verbal CTA + pinned comment + description link (mention "link below" once mid-video and once at end); watch time → pitch one specific related video by its hook, not "check out my other videos."

9. **Do a retention pass.** Re-read the full script hunting for: any 60-second stretch with no new information, visual change, or story beat; any claim without proof; any section that could be cut without breaking the promise. Cut 10–15% of the word count in this pass — first drafts always run long.

10. **Deliver the package** in the Output format below, including chapter timestamps and a description block with keyword-relevant first 150 characters (the portion visible in search — for deeper SEO work, point to **keyword-research**).

## Frameworks & templates

### Hook patterns (pick one for step 4)

- **Cold-open result:** show the outcome first, then rewind. "This channel gained 40,000 subscribers in 60 days. Here's the exact system — including the part everyone skips."
- **Contrarian stake:** attack a common belief. "Everything you've heard about morning routines is backwards — and the study everyone cites actually proves it."
- **In-medias-res story:** start mid-scene at the moment of tension, freeze, promise context. "At 2 a.m. the site went down — and I was the only one with the password. Let me back up."
- **Question stack:** 2–3 escalating questions the video answers. "Why do some thumbnails get 10% CTR? Why do the ugly ones sometimes win? And can you engineer it? I tested 30 to find out."
- **Countdown/list tease:** for listicles. Name the category, tease the #1 without revealing it. "Five tools — and the last one replaced the other four for me."

### Open-loop technique

Plant a specific unanswered question early, reference it mid-video, pay it off late. Rules: the loop must be concrete ("the third method," "the $400 mistake"), it must actually pay off (a weak payoff burns trust and future retention), and one main loop per video plus 1–2 chapter-level micro-loops is enough. Three or more competing loops read as clickbait churn.

### Title formulas (with fill-in-the-blank)

1. Outcome + timeframe: "I [did X] for [N days] — [unexpected result]"
2. Negative curiosity: "Stop [common practice] (do this instead)"
3. Specific number + stakes: "[N] [things] that [outcome] ([qualifier])" — "7 Camera Settings That Ruin Your Footage (Fix #4 First)"
4. How-to + differentiator: "How to [outcome] Without [dreaded requirement]"
5. Authority test: "I Tried [thing] So You Don't Have To" / "[Expert] Reacts to [thing]"
6. Versus: "[A] vs [B]: [surprising verdict frame]"

### Full script template

```
TITLE (chosen): ___________________  (___ chars)
THUMBNAIL: [face/emotion] + [focal object] + text: "____" (≤4 words)
PROMISE: By the end, the viewer will ___________________.
LENGTH TARGET: ___ min (~___ words @ 140 wpm)

======================================================
[0:00–0:30] HOOK — pattern: ____________
------------------------------------------------------
VISUAL                          | SCRIPT
[COLD OPEN: ____]               | (0–5s promise restate) "..."
[CUT: ____]                     | (5–15s stakes) "..."
[GFX/B-ROLL: proof shot]        | (15–25s credibility) "..."
[CUT to talking head]           | (25–30s roadmap + open loop) "..."

[0:30–__] CHAPTER 1: <name> — sub-payoff: ____
------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL]                        | Mini-hook line...
[PATTERN INTERRUPT @ ~1:15]     | Example/proof...
[VISUAL]                        | Bridge: "But..."

[__–__] CHAPTER 2 ... (repeat; sponsor read after first payoff if applicable)
[~50%] SUBSCRIBE ASK (one line, reason attached)
[final chapter] MAIN LOOP PAYOFF
[last 20s] CONCLUSION + NEXT-VIDEO BRIDGE (end-screen safe framing)
======================================================
DESCRIPTION (first 150 chars carry keywords): ...
CHAPTERS: 0:00 ___ / 0:30 ___ / ...
PINNED COMMENT / CTA LINKS: ...
```

### Benchmark reference (use when diagnosing an underperforming video)

- Browse/suggested CTR: 2–3% weak, 4–6% solid, 8%+ exceptional (small channels skew higher on low impressions — judge only after ~1,000+ impressions).
- 30-second retention: 65–70%+ healthy; under 50% = broken hook or packaging mismatch.
- Average view duration: 50%+ of runtime is strong for 8–12 minute videos; 40–50% typical; under 35% = restructure the body, not the hook.
- Diagnosis order: low CTR → fix packaging (step 1); high CTR + early cliff → fix hook (step 4); gradual mid-video slide → fix pacing and pattern interrupts (steps 5, 9).

### Worked example — hook for a video titled "I Deleted Half My Videos (Views Doubled)"

```
[COLD OPEN: analytics screen, views graph spiking]
"Sixty days ago I deleted 47 videos from this channel. Everyone said
it would kill it. Instead — this happened." 

[CUT: talking head, lean in]
"Views doubled. Subscribers up 30%. And the reason has nothing to do
with the algorithm 'rewarding' me — it's a mechanic almost nobody
talks about."

[GFX: folder of deleted video thumbnails]
"I'll show you exactly which videos I deleted, the three-question test
I used to pick them —"

[CUT: closer, beat]
"— and the one video I deleted that I really, really shouldn't have.
That one's at the end."
```

Note what it does: restates the title visually in 3 seconds, proof on screen before any claim, credibility via shown results not bio, and a concrete open loop ("the one I shouldn't have deleted") scheduled for the finale.

## Output format

Deliver one document containing, in order:

1. **Packaging block:** chosen title (with character count), 2 backup titles, thumbnail concept description, and the promise statement.
2. **Retention skeleton:** chapter list with target timestamps, each chapter's sub-payoff, and where the main open loop plants/pays off.
3. **Full two-column script:** VISUAL column (B-roll, GFX, cuts, pattern interrupts with rough timestamps) and SCRIPT column (word-for-word spoken lines). Timestamp markers at every chapter break. Total word count stated and reconciled against target length at 140 wpm.
4. **CTA plan:** subscribe-ask placement and wording, sponsor segment (if any), end-screen script for the final 20 seconds, next-video recommendation.
5. **Description + metadata block:** video description (first 150 characters keyword-bearing), chapter timestamps formatted for YouTube (starting `0:00`), and pinned-comment text if a link CTA exists.

## Quality checklist

Pass/fail every item before delivering:

- [ ] Title is ≤100 characters and the core claim lands within the first 55.
- [ ] Thumbnail text is ≤4 words and does not repeat words from the title.
- [ ] Title + thumbnail make one combined promise, and the script's final third delivers exactly that promise.
- [ ] First spoken line references the promise — no greeting, no channel intro, no logo sting before value.
- [ ] Hook completes all four jobs (restate, stakes, proof, roadmap+loop) by 0:30.
- [ ] One concrete main open loop is planted in the hook and explicitly paid off in the final third.
- [ ] Every 60-second stretch contains at least one pattern interrupt marked in the visual column.
- [ ] Every claim has an example, number, or demonstration within two sentences.
- [ ] Word count is within ±10% of (target minutes × 140), post retention-pass cut.
- [ ] Exactly one subscribe ask, placed after delivered value, with a stated reason.
- [ ] Sponsor read (if any) sits between 25–40% of runtime and runs 45–60 seconds.
- [ ] Final 20 seconds work with end-screen overlays: no critical visuals in overlay zones, one specific next-video pitch.
- [ ] Chapters list starts at 0:00 and has 4–7 entries with benefit-bearing names (not "Intro," "Part 2").
- [ ] No sentence in the script exceeds ~20 words without a natural pause point.

## Common mistakes

- **Scripting before packaging.** Writing a great video and then hunting for a title produces mismatched promises. The title/thumbnail pair is the brief; the script fulfills it.
- **The throat-clearing intro.** "Hey guys, welcome back, before we start make sure to subscribe..." costs 20–30% of viewers before the content begins. Value first; identity later.
- **Front-loading everything.** Dumping the full payoff in minute one gives viewers no reason to stay. Announce the best payoff early, deliver it late.
- **Open loops that don't close.** Teasing "the one trick" and delivering a platitude trains the audience to distrust your hooks — the damage shows up in the NEXT video's retention.
- **Blog-post cadence.** 35-word sentences with subordinate clauses read fine and speak terribly. Scripts are spoken word: short sentences, contractions, second person.
- **Visual direction as an afterthought.** A script with no B-roll/GFX notes forces the editor to invent pacing, and pattern interrupts get lost. If the visual column is empty for 60 seconds, retention will show it.
- **Three subscribe asks.** Repeated CTAs don't stack — they annoy. One ask, well-placed, with a reason, outperforms.
- **Chapter names that spoil or bore.** "Intro / Point 1 / Point 2 / Outro" wastes the chapter bar; "The $400 Mistake" earns skips-back instead of skips-away.
- **Ignoring the 8-minute midroll line.** Padding a 6-minute idea to 8:01 for ads tanks retention and nets less revenue than the tight cut. Only cross 8 minutes when the material earns it.
- **Ending with a fade-out apology.** "So yeah... that's pretty much it, thanks for watching..." bleeds the session. End on the payoff and hand viewers their next click immediately.
